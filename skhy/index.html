    Quarter in one view

    • Q3 FY2026: third consecutive record, and the first KRW100trn+ quarter. Revenue KRW104.6trn (+32% QoQ, +328% YoY). OP KRW83.3trn (+38% QoQ, +632% YoY); OPM 80% (+3pp QoQ) — both all-time highs. D&A KRW4.3trn; EBITDA KRW87.7trn (84% margin).
    • Earnings-quality distortion flipped sign. Non-op swung to -KRW32.6trn (FX net loss 11.6trn on won strength; investment-asset valuation losses 22.9trn), vs +62.2trn in Q2. Pre-tax KRW50.7trn; net KRW39.8trn (38% margin) — net income is now *less than half* of operating profit. The Kioxia-driven inflation is gone; a large unexplained valuation loss replaced it.
    • Guides: DRAM hit, NAND missed modestly. DRAM bits +~10% QoQ (as guided); DRAM ASP +high-20% QoQ (vs +~30% in Q2 — still decelerating in absolute terms, though management called it "similar"). NAND bits decreased slightly vs the +LSD guide — management framed it as deliberate mix discipline toward 321-layer TLC eSSD; NAND ASP +mid-20%.
    • Q4 guide is the most aggressive of the cycle: DRAM bits +mid-20% QoQ (1cnm expansion + M15X ramp); NAND bits +mid-single-digit. HBM4 to overtake HBM3E in Q4 in both volume and revenue share.
    • Shareholder returns finally landed, at scale: ~KRW100trn FY2026 program (≥30trn dividends, ≥60trn buyback/cancellation); >50% FCF payout pledge; KRW20,000/share Q3 dividend including a KRW10,000 Kioxia special.
    • Balance sheet: cash 114.5trn (+26.5trn QoQ); debt 15.8trn (-2.8trn); net cash 98.7trn — essentially at the >100trn target; D/E 5%.
    • Demand visibility claims escalated sharply: 14 LTAs signed (from ~10); >50% of 2027-28 capacity under LTA; DRAM/HBM "effectively sold out" through 2029 including volumes under discussion; 2027 HBM price/volume negotiations finalized with major customers.

    What management is focused on

    • Pre-empting the AI-bubble debate with customer financials. The CEO and CFO both front-loaded demand durability. Against the monetization question (Citi), management cited hyperscaler cloud revenue +30-80% YoY, cloud order backlogs up 2-5x YoY, rising paying users, and a broadening investor base (sovereign AI, non-tech enterprises, on-prem) that makes demand resilient to any single buyer pausing. New quantified scaffolding: CSP capex +60% in 2027, secured power capacity +100% YoY, AI data-center infrastructure market +40% CAGR to 2030 (all attributed to market research firms, not company data).
    • "Sold out through 2029" as the centerpiece. LTA count rose to 14; >50% of 2027-28 capacity committed; management said committed plus under-discussion volumes make DRAM and HBM effectively sold out for three years. Customers are reportedly requesting *extensions* beyond the typical 5-year term. This is the strongest version yet of the no-oversupply argument — and the most falsifiable.
    • Defending the capex escalation with a "shell-first, ramp-later" doctrine. Against BofA's overproduction question: new fabs (Yongin, M17) will accelerate shell construction but open cleanrooms in phases; equipment move-in and ramp adjust to demand; investment screens include customer financial health, funding capability, and supply-chain readiness. "More fabs does not necessarily mean more supply" is the new mantra, justified by HBM/process complexity consuming wafer capacity.
    • Neutralizing the HBM stack-lowering threat. Management reframed customer spec adjustments (12-high HBM4 → 8-high HBM4E) as proof of shortage, not weakness: lower stacks mean more cubes, more base dies, more back-end processing — and higher per-die capacity offsets much of the bit impact. Claimed customer volume requirements are more than 2x current supply capability.
    • Delivering the long-deferred shareholder return, and reframing investment gains as a returns channel. The Kioxia gain is explicitly being recycled into special dividends ("return most of the gains"), with the Solidigm IPO cancellation explained as market-driven and partly motivated by preserving shareholder-return capacity.

    Key numbers and quarter mechanics

    • P&L: revenue 104.6trn (+32% QoQ, +328% YoY); OP 83.3trn (80% OPM, +3pp QoQ); D&A 4.3trn; EBITDA 87.7trn (84%); non-op -32.6trn (FX -11.6trn, investment-asset valuation -22.9trn); pre-tax 50.7trn; net 39.8trn (38% margin).
    • DRAM: bits +~10% QoQ (in line); ASP +high-20% QoQ. Server DDR5 shipments +40% QoQ; HBM4 full-scale mass production and shipments began in Q3. ASP trajectory: +mid-60% (Q1) → +~30% (Q2) → +high-20s (Q3) — deceleration continues but is now explicitly mix-driven (HBM4 priced above HBM3E).
    • NAND: bits down slightly QoQ (below the +LSD guide — first bit-guide miss in at least five quarters, attributed to deliberate high-value mix adjustment); ASP +mid-20% QoQ (vs +mid-50s in Q2 — sharp deceleration).
    • Q4 guide: DRAM bits +mid-20% QoQ; NAND bits +mid-single-digit QoQ. No ASP guidance, as usual.
    • 2027 demand view: DRAM bit growth +mid-20%, NAND +high-teens — "achievable" growth similar to 2026, i.e., supply-capped, not demand-capped. Supply shortfall "projected to continue through 2030."
    • Balance sheet/capex: cash 114.5trn; debt 15.8trn; net cash 98.7trn; D/E 5%. 2027 capex to "increase significantly" vs 2026's high-40trn — no absolute figure given.
    • Shareholder return mechanics: FY2026 total ~100trn = ≥30trn dividends + ≥60trn buyback/cancellation (incl. the 40trn August program, completed last week, cancellation pending). Q3 actions: 14trn dividend (KRW20,000/share = 10,000 regular + 10,000 Kioxia special) + 20trn buyback, of which 10trn for employee comp is excluded from the shareholder-return tally — net Q3 return ~24trn. Remainder of the Kioxia special comes in the year-end dividend.
    • Persistent gaps: still no DRAM/NAND revenue split, no HBM revenue or mix quantification, no segment margins, no LTA pricing floors/caps or deposit magnitudes, and no explanation of the KRW22.9trn investment-asset valuation loss (composition, which assets, whether Kioxia-related marks reversed).

    Product and launch scorecard

    • HBM4: the strongest quarter of execution evidence yet. Full-scale mass production and shipments in Q3; 12-high shipments on schedule per customer agreements; yield "already exceeded its planned yield" — target revised up; claimed industry-best fab *and* full-flow back-end yields. Q4 crossover: HBM4 to surpass HBM3E in both shipment and revenue share, becoming the flagship. M15X ramping to full capacity by year-end. Still no absolute HBM revenue, share, or customer-count disclosure.
    • 2027 HBM negotiations: finalized, not just "progressing smoothly." Volume and price concluded with major customers, "faithfully reflecting" HBM4's added value. Notably, management expects the HBM-vs-conventional profitability gap to narrow meaningfully — readable two ways (conventional pricing catching up, and/or HBM4 pricing not fully keeping pace); direction of HBM pricing itself was not stated.
    • HBM4E: sample shipments completed in Q2 (consistent with last quarter's early-sampling claim); development/qualification on customer timelines; 8-high variant explicitly positioned as the stack-lowered option with higher per-die capacity offsetting bit impact.
    • Custom HBM: elevated to a named strategic thread — "growing importance," projects underway with major customers, "first supplier to key customers" claim. No specifics.
    • SOCAMM / server LPDDR: Q4 plan to expand 1cnm DDR5 and "AI server LPDDR5 SOCAMM" sales — note the transcript says LPDDR5 SOCAMM, not the SOCAMM2 branding used previously; worth checking whether this is translation or a product-naming change.
    • 321-layer NAND: to exceed 50% of domestic capacity in Q4 (prior target was end-2026 — effectively on/ahead of schedule); eSSD supply expanding.
    • NAND AI-storage roadmap got concrete: SALT-KV (software tiering of KV cache across HBM/DRAM/SSD by reuse value) and CMM-hybrid (CXL-based, full KV cache on SSD with DRAM preloading) named as in-development technologies; system demonstrations on real AI workloads underway with customers/platform partners; HBF repositioned as the future tier between HBM and SSD. This is the most detailed NAND architecture disclosure to date.
    • 4F²/3D DRAM (new disclosure): strategy is to extend 6F² via EUV scaling and process maturity, then transition to 4F² or 3D DRAM "at the right time" — a deliberate fast-follower posture versus competitors pushing new cell structures earlier.
    • Quiet/dropped threads: iHBM (last quarter's reveal) not mentioned; LPDDR6, CXL as a product line, PQC21, 245TB eSSD, GDDR7, OpenAI LOI, tariffs, China/VEU all still absent. Solidigm's eSSD momentum not quantified this quarter (no repeat of the ">3x QoQ" datapoint).

    Sell-side read-through

    • Tone: congratulatory, zero challenge. Every questioner opened with congratulations; no one pressed on the NAND bit-guide miss, the KRW22.9trn valuation loss, the ASP deceleration, or the HBM/conventional margin-gap comment. The sell-side has moved from testing the story (Daiwa's ASP challenge in Q2) to underwriting it.
    • Peter Lee (Citi): AI monetization risk — got the most evidence-based demand answer yet (hyperscaler cloud growth 30-80%, backlogs 2-5x, paying-user growth, investor-base diversification). Still entirely customer-cited, no SK hynix-specific data.
    • Sunwoo Kim (Meritz): dividend/buyback split rationale — answer: sustainable dividend benchmarked to peers and yield, specials for investment gains, buybacks because "our share price today is undervalued." Quarterly buyback sizing left to Board discretion — flexibility retained.
    • Giuni Lee (Goldman): LTA implications — extracted the call's biggest claim: DRAM/HBM effectively sold out through 2029 (committed + under-discussion volumes), with >50% LTA coverage of 2027-28 capacity to be maintained "over the medium to longer term."
    • Simon Woo (BofA): the only cycle-risk question (overproduction) — got the phased-cleanroom/shell-first doctrine and the demand-screening framework. A genuine answer on mechanism, but the flexibility pledge is untested.
    • Jay Kwon (JPM): HBM4/4E/custom update — produced the yield-above-plan disclosure and the Q4 crossover confirmation.
    • Dong-hee Han (SK Securities): HBM stack-lowering — the most commercially sensitive question; management's answer (demand >2x supply, more cubes/base dies, profitability "far above the peak in past cycles") is confident but concedes customers are actively re-spec'ing HBM, which is new information about supply tightness and customer behavior.
    • Young-gun Kim (Mirae): 4F² — a technology-positioning question reflecting competitive anxiety about next-gen DRAM; answer was deliberately non-committal on timing.
    • Young-ho Ryu (NH): KV-cache tiering and NAND demand — produced the SALT-KV/CMM-hybrid disclosures.
    • Notable absences: no question on the non-op loss composition; no NAND miss question; no Solidigm IPO-cancellation follow-up (funding implications for Solidigm's fab expansion unaddressed); no tariff/China/OpenAI questions for the third-plus consecutive quarter.

    Management credibility

    • Positive: the shareholder-return promise was kept, big. After two-plus quarters of "within the year" deferrals, management delivered a ~100trn program with completed (not promised) execution on the 40trn August buyback, a Board-resolved 14trn dividend, and a >50% FCF framework. The Kioxia special dividend also honors the stated principle of recycling strategic-investment gains. This is the quarter's largest credibility deposit.
    • Positive: HBM4 delivery exceeded its own plan. Yield above target (target revised up), Q4 crossover confirmed, 2027 negotiations finalized — the H2 ramp claim that was "falsifiable" after Q2 has so far been validated.
    • Positive: LTA trajectory is consistent and compounding — ~10 customers (Q2) → 14 (Q3), with the new >50%-of-2027/28-capacity quantification answering last quarter's coverage-ratio refusal.
    • Negative: first bit-guide miss in the track record. NAND guided +LSD, delivered a slight decline. Management's mix-discipline explanation is plausible (321-layer eSSD focus) but unaccompanied by eSSD revenue data this quarter — the one quarter they stopped quantifying eSSD is the quarter NAND missed.
    • Negative: the KRW22.9trn investment-asset valuation loss was disclosed as a line item and never explained. After Q2's +63.3trn gain, the swing in non-op items across two quarters is ~96trn. Reported net income remains nearly useless for trend analysis, and management volunteered nothing.
    • Mixed: "profitability gap between HBM and conventional DRAM to narrow meaningfully" is a striking disclosure buried in prepared remarks — it implicitly concedes conventional DRAM is the marginal profit driver into 2027, which cuts against the HBM-premium narrative management has cultivated. No analyst picked it up.
    • Watch: "effectively sold out through 2029" includes volumes merely "under discussion" ("highly likely to come through"). Folding uncommitted demand into a sold-out claim is aggressive framing; the hard number remains >50% of 2027-28 capacity.
    • Watch: Solidigm IPO cancellation is reasonably explained (market volatility), but it reverses the financing plan for Solidigm's manufacturing expansion with no stated alternative — a loose end.

    What changed versus the prior quarter

    • Earnings quality inverted: non-op +62.2trn → -32.6trn; net margin 118% → 38% vs OPM 76% → 80%. Operating profit is now the dominant and clean number; net income is depressed by FX and unexplained valuation losses.
    • Shareholder returns went from promise to policy: ~100trn FY2026 program, >50% FCF pledge, Kioxia special dividend — versus "within the year" deferrals gated by ADR constraints. The ADR excuse disappeared entirely.
    • Demand-visibility claims escalated an order of magnitude: ~10 LTAs → 14; coverage ratio refused → >50% of 2027-28 capacity disclosed; new "sold out through 2029" framing; customers requesting term extensions.
    • 2027 HBM negotiations concluded (volume and price) — versus "underway, progressing smoothly" in Q2. New and underexplained: HBM/conventional profitability gap to "narrow meaningfully."
    • Volume acceleration replaced price as the growth engine: Q4 DRAM bit guide +mid-20% (vs +~10% for Q3) on 1cnm + M15X; DRAM ASP growth still decelerating (+30% → +high-20s); NAND ASP decelerated hard (+mid-50s → +mid-20s).
    • NAND missed its bit guide for the first time in the recent track record (slight decline vs +LSD), and the eSSD quantification that supported the Q2 NAND story was not repeated.
    • Capex trajectory steepened: 2027 guided "significantly" above 2026's high-40trn, with a formalized shell-first/phased-cleanroom execution doctrine.
    • HBM stack-lowering emerged as a new, management-acknowledged phenomenon — customers re-spec'ing 12-high to 8-high; framed as shortage evidence.
    • Solidigm IPO cancelled — a strategic reversal attributed to market conditions.
    • PC/mobile framing blended: Q2's pure "supply starvation" became "cost burdens from rising memory prices and supply shortages" — demand destruction is back in the language alongside shortage.
    • New disclosures: SALT-KV, CMM-hybrid, 4F²/3D DRAM strategy, custom HBM emphasis, CSP capex +60%/power +100% 2027 forecasts, supply constraint "through 2030."
    • Dropped/quiet: iHBM, SOCAMM2 branding, Solidigm eSSD metrics, LPDDR6, CXL (as product), tariffs, China/VEU, OpenAI LOI.

    Bull case

    • The Q4 guide implies the cycle's biggest volume quarter yet: DRAM bits +mid-20% with HBM4 crossing over and M15X hitting full capacity — revenue and OP records are near-certain to extend even with flat ASPs.
    • Contractual visibility is now quantified and large: >50% of 2027-28 capacity under LTA with deposits, 14 customers, extension requests, and finalized 2027 HBM pricing — the oversupply bear case requires believing 14 sophisticated customers all overcommitted simultaneously.
    • HBM execution is best-in-class by management's own falsifiable metrics: yield above plan, back-end yield claimed industry-best, Q4 crossover, custom HBM pipeline — and customer demand claimed at >2x supply capability.
    • Capital returns are now real and recurring: ~100trn FY2026, >50% FCF pledge, quarterly buyback cadence, special-dividend mechanism for investment gains — with net cash at 98.7trn and 84% EBITDA margins funding it without strain.
    • NAND has an architecture-level AI story now: KV-cache tiering (SALT-KV, CMM-hybrid) and HBF create structural bit demand beyond the eSSD cycle, with 321-layer cost leadership past 50% of capacity.
    • Supply discipline is structural, management argues: process complexity and HBM resource intensity cap bit growth industry-wide "through 2030," and the shell-first capex doctrine limits overshoot risk.

    Bear case

    • Reported net income is still noise. A KRW22.9trn valuation loss with zero explanation, one quarter after a 63.3trn gain — two-quarter non-op swing of ~96trn. Whatever is being marked (residual Kioxia? other stakes?) is large, volatile, and undisclosed.
    • NAND cracked first: bit-guide miss, ASP growth halved sequentially, and the eSSD quantification quietly dropped from the script. If AI-storage demand were as strong as the architecture slides suggest, the revenue datapoint should have been repeated.
    • "Sold out through 2029" overstates the hard number. Committed LTA volume is >50% of 2027-28 capacity; the 2029 claim leans on volumes "under discussion." If even the committed half carries market-linked price ranges (as disclosed), LTAs protect volume, not price — downside participation is preserved.
    • The HBM margin-gap comment may be a quiet concession. If conventional DRAM profitability is converging *up* toward HBM, fine; if HBM4 2027 pricing came in below the premium narrative, the finalized negotiations locked that in. Management did not say which, and no one asked.
    • Stack-lowering is a real demand-leakage vector management chose to normalize: customers stretching limited HBM across more accelerators means fewer bits per system — the "more cubes, more base dies" offset is asserted, not quantified.
    • Capex is compounding at peak margins with the returns tap now open: 2027 "significantly" above high-40trn while committing ~100trn to shareholders — any demand air pocket squeezes both simultaneously, and the Solidigm IPO failure removes one funding relief valve.
    • PC/mobile demand destruction is back in the language ("cost burdens"), and the sell-side's unanimous congratulation means consensus is no longer stress-testing any of this.

    Bear case

    • The headline print is mostly one-off. Strip the KRW63.3trn investment gains and Q2 net profit is roughly half the reported 93.9trn; net margin of 118% is not an earnings-power data point. Three straight quarters of large unexamined non-op items make reported net income nearly useless for trend analysis.
    • DRAM ASP missed expectations, and the explanation (mix, H2-pushed shipments) concedes that blended pricing power is now hostage to HBM4 ramp execution. If the H2 mix recovery doesn't materialize, the ASP deceleration (+mid-60% → +30%) will look like the start of normalization, not a timing artifact.
    • NAND momentum is already fading in the guide: Q3 bits +LSD after +mid-teens, with no ASP guide — the eSSD surge may be lumpy.
    • Capex is escalating aggressively at peak margins: high-40trn in 2026 plus M15X acceleration, M17, P&T7, and a post-Yongin cluster — a strategic U-turn from "no fabs beyond Yongin" one quarter ago. If the LTA-anchored demand visibility proves softer than claimed, this is exactly how past oversupply cycles were built.
    • Shareholder returns remain a promise, not a policy: two consecutive quarters of "within the year," now gated by ADR regulatory constraints, while cash accumulates and reinvestment is prioritized.
    • Competitive anxiety is rising (explicit HBM-moat question), and management's answer rested on track record rather than quantified current share, qualification breadth, or 2027 volume commitments.
    • Still unexamined: tariffs, China/VEU, OpenAI LOI, customer concentration, LTA pricing floors/caps, and the terms under which deposits could be forfeited.
