Aug 12, 2026+34.14%Q2 FY2026
Read transcript briefing

Quarter in one view

  • Q2 FY2026: group revenue $582M, +454% YoY, +46% QoQ; Nebius AI $575M (+514% YoY), 98% of group revenue.
  • ARR $3.0B at end of June, +58% from $1.9B at end of March (transcript initially says 56%, corrected to 58%).
  • Group adjusted EBITDA $236M, 41% margin (vs. $129.5M / 32% in Q1); Nebius AI margin 50% (transcript contains a misstatement of Nebius AI EBITDA as $286M, corrected to $236M — the $236M figure appears to be the group number; the Nebius AI dollar figure is unclear from the transcript).
  • Four new ~$1B+ core AI-cloud deals (Reflection, Cohere, a scaled U.S. neolab, a large U.S. quant trading firm) at $20–25M/MW with prepayments covering 50–60% of associated CapEx.
  • First capacity auction cleared 15% above the highest prior Blackwell price (20% above pipeline pricing); short-term premium deals being negotiated at $40–50M/MW, one already signed.
  • Contracted power target raised to 5 GW by end-2026 (from ≥4 GW); 800MW–1GW connected-power year-end guidance reiterated; all FY2026 guidance reaffirmed (revenue $3.0–3.4B, ARR $7–9B, ~40% group EBITDA margin, CapEx $20–25B).
  • Funding: $2.8B raised via ATM (12.7M shares at ~$224 weighted average; 12.3M shares remain); first $775M asset-backed facility at SOFR+250; >$9B of customer prepayments expected in 2026; cash $8.0B; Q2 CapEx ~$5.7B; operating cash flow $2.3B.

What management is focused on

  • Articulating a three-tier deal architecture: (1) midterm 1–3 year AI-cloud contracts ($20–25M/MW, 50–60% CapEx prepayment coverage), (2) short-duration (<6 month) premium capacity at $40–50M/MW+, (3) long-term investment-grade contracts used as financing collateral ($40B contracted backlog).
  • Deliberately withholding 2027 capacity from sale: management claims it could sell all of 2027 capacity today at >$20M/MW with <2-year payback, but is holding back for expected better pricing — a clear, testable strategic bet.
  • New capital-light scaling path: asset-light partnership model (partners finance/build/operate facilities; Nebius supplies platform and demand for "high-margin revenue" with minimal balance-sheet capital); "dozens of inquiries" claimed, no signed deals disclosed.
  • Price discovery as strategy: auctions and short-term deals used to read real-time market pricing; selling "closer to deployment" to capture rising prices.
  • Financing hierarchy: operating cash flow → customer prepayments (target: raise coverage above 50–60%) → asset-backed debt against $40B backlog → untapped corporate debt → equity/convertibles; explicit discipline framing around cost of capital, dilution, balance sheet.
  • Capacity over-provisioning: 5 GW contracted target, >1 GW/yr new-build ambition from 2027, Bloom Energy behind-the-meter pivot, regional flexibility within cloud contracts to de-risk single-site dependence.

Key numbers and quarter mechanics

  • Revenue $582M group / $575M Nebius AI; drivers: Q1 capacity additions, higher utilization (infrastructure efficiency gains), and early high-margin contributions from asset-light, Token Factory, and acquisitions.
  • ARR $3.0B (+58% QoQ); note the new $1B+ deals and short-term premium deals come online late Q4 2026 onward — no material 2026 revenue impact, all 2027+.
  • EBITDA: group $236M / 41%; Nebius AI 50% margin; Avride/TripleTen remain the group-to-AI margin gap.
  • Prepayments: ~70% of Q2 deals included upfront prepayment; >$9B total upfront funding expected in 2026; OCF $2.3B again (same figure as Q1 — prepayment-driven; ex-prepayment quality still not disclosed).
  • Cash $8.0B (down from $9.3B in Q1 despite $2.8B ATM and $2.3B OCF — consistent with ~$5.7B Q2 CapEx).
  • ATM: 12.7M shares at ~$224 = ~$2.8B gross; 12.3M shares remain. The prior quarter's "untapped" ATM is now more than half used.
  • Asset-backed facility: $775M, SOFR+250bps ("mid-single-digit" all-in), secured by deployed GPUs and investment-grade contracted cash flows; positioned as repeatable against $40B backlog.
  • Connected power guidance 800MW–1GW for 2026 reiterated; management flagged a multi-month lag from connected power → commissioning → cluster build → platform deployment → onboarding → revenue, with revenue activity extending into 1H 2027.
  • Vera Rubin: in labs, "expected results," deployment starting late 2026/early 2027, ramping through 2027; GB300→Vera Rubin transition described as technically easier than the prior generation step.

Product and launch scorecard

  • Capacity auction (new): first auction cleared 15% above highest-ever Blackwell price, 20% above pipeline pricing; winner plans to participate in future rounds. Small capacity portion used; real price-discovery evidence, not just narrative.
  • Short-term premium deals (new): $40–50M/MW "and sometimes above" for 3–6 month dedicated GB300 clusters (time-boxed training runs, RL post-training sprints); one signed recently. Still take-or-pay per management framing.
  • Asset-light model (new): announced this quarter; "dozens of inquiries" from capital-rich partners; no signed partnerships, revenue, or margin quantification yet — earliest capacity impact claimed for 2027+.
  • Token Factory: day-0 support for recent open-model launches (Nemotron Ultra, GLM 5.2, Kimi K3, DeepSeek Flash, Minimax 3); GLM 5.2 implementation claims 100% quality score with leading Artificial Analysis benchmark performance; Eigen and Clarifai teams "fully integrated" and shipping in the roadmap. Still no revenue disclosure.
  • Tavily: first full quarter; developer community grew to 2.5M from 1M in February; launched keyless search for autonomous agents; enterprise certifications achieved. No revenue numbers.
  • Customer proof points for agentic/inference: Revolut, MasterCard, Shopify, Sword Health, Higgsfield cited as use cases; internal "Echo" infrastructure agent runs on Token Factory.
  • CPU/ARM deployments being added alongside GPUs for agentic orchestration/tool-calling workloads — new infrastructure detail.

Sell-side read-through

  • Vineland (Morgan Stanley, Ryan Lantz): management gave its most direct answer to date — building construction finished earlier this summer, engineering fit-out progressing, Bloom fuel-cell deployment expected to be fast, "no significant impact expected on the project timeline," all contractual tranches delivered to date. The hearing adjournment is framed as normal process around a site-layout amendment driven by the Bloom power switch. This partially resolves the prior quarter's evasion, though approval is still pending.
  • 2027 framing (Morgan Stanley): management put a stake in the ground — >$20M/MW, <2-year payback deals as the 2027 pricing baseline, significantly more capacity deployed in 2027 than 2026, formal 2027 guidance "later this year."
  • Debt vs. equity (Wolfe): management declined to rule out further equity; hierarchy stated but ATM use this quarter shows equity is actively in the mix, not a last resort. GPU-as-asset-class financing floated as a future source — speculative.
  • 2027 capacity allocation (Citi): no ACV/MW threshold given; priority order is existing customers → new logos → terms (price, then prepayment, then duration). Deliberately vague on mix between short- and mid-term.
  • xAI selling compute (Water Tower): dismissed as market validation, not competitive threat; no direct answer on renewal pricing specifics.
  • $40M/MW Q3 monetization (BNP Paribas): question on whether this reflects Vera Rubin pricing was not directly answered — management pivoted to the Vera Rubin deployment timeline.
  • Notably absent this quarter: no questions on customer concentration splits, Avride/TripleTen deconsolidation progress, or OCF ex-prepayments — prior-quarter gaps remain unclosed.

Management credibility

  • Delivered on the key Q1 test: Q2 margin was guided down for timing reasons, but instead came in at 41% group / 50% Nebius AI — well above Q1 levels a quarter early. This beats the "Q3 recovery" promise and strengthens the timing explanation retroactively.
  • Specific, checkable claims increased: four named/characterized $1B+ deals, auction clearing premium quantified (15%/20%), $775M facility pricing disclosed (SOFR+250), ATM usage fully disclosed (shares, price, proceeds).
  • Vineland: moved from sidestep to substantive update (construction complete, Bloom pivot, timeline intact) — credibility positive, though the approval vote is still pending and the claim is now testable.
  • Consistency watch: cash declined $1.3B QoQ despite $2.8B ATM + $2.3B OCF — arithmetic is explainable via CapEx but management did not walk the bridge; transcript contains two verbal misstatements (ARR growth %, Nebius AI EBITDA $) corrected on the fly.
  • Soft spots: "could sell all 2027 capacity today" is unverifiable; asset-light has inquiries but no contracts; no Token Factory revenue; no concentration disclosure; the $40M/MW Vera Rubin question was deflected; 2027 guidance deferred.
  • Guidance behavior: reaffirmed all FY2026 metrics while raising the power target to 5 GW — consistent with the sold-out narrative; the raised target arriving "much earlier" than planned supports execution claims.

What changed versus the prior quarter

  • Revenue $399M → $582M (+46% QoQ); ARR $1.9B → $3.0B (+58%); group EBITDA margin 32% → 41%; Nebius AI margin 45% → 50% — the guided Q2 margin dip did not materialize; the recovery happened a quarter early.
  • Contracted power target raised again: ≥4 GW → 5 GW by end-2026.
  • ATM went from untapped to 12.7M of 25M shares used (~$2.8B at ~$224); first asset-backed facility ($775M, SOFR+250) executed — both prior-quarter watchlist items resolved.
  • Deal mix disclosure is new: $20–25M/MW midterm economics, 50–60% prepayment CapEx coverage, $40–50M/MW short-term premium tier, auction mechanism — none of this was quantified in Q1.
  • New named core-cloud customers (Reflection, Cohere + two unnamed) — evidence for the diversification claim that was previously unquantified; still no revenue-split percentages.
  • Vineland moved from evaded question to on-the-record timeline affirmation with the Bloom pivot disclosed.
  • Cash $9.3B → $8.0B; Q2 CapEx ~$5.7B (first quarterly CapEx print).
  • 2027 posture shifted: from "raised CapEx tied to committed 1H27 capacity" to actively withholding 2027 capacity for better pricing, plus asset-light as a new 2027+ capacity source.
  • Prior-quarter items not mentioned: Avride/TripleTen deconsolidation, Meta optionality monetization, pipeline multiple (3.5x), Pennsylvania site milestones.

Bull case

  • Pricing power is now evidenced, not asserted: auction cleared 15% above all-time high; $40–50M/MW short-term deals signed; $20–25M/MW midterm deals with <2-year payback; management withholding 2027 supply because it expects more.
  • Margin trajectory beat: 50% Nebius AI margin in Q2 vs. the guided dip; asset-light, Token Factory, and acquisitions already contributing high-margin revenue; management sees owned-data-center capacity improving margins further from 2H 2027.
  • Diversification evidence: four competitive $1B+ wins against incumbent suppliers (including hyperscalers), validated by POCs, with expansion and Token Factory cross-sell discussions underway.
  • Financing machine working: $9B+ prepayments in 2026, repeatable ABS market validated at SOFR+250 against $40B backlog, ATM executed at $224, corporate debt untouched — multiple levers remain.
  • Capacity visibility: 5 GW contracted target, >1 GW/yr build capability from 2027, Bloom behind-the-meter unlocking sites, Vera Rubin already in labs with an easier generational transition.
  • Asset-light optionality: if partner inquiries convert, Nebius gets high-margin revenue growth without proportional CapEx — a genuinely different earnings shape.

Bear case

  • The withholding strategy is a bet on continued price escalation: if AI compute pricing softens in 2027, deliberately unsold capacity and "selling closer to deployment" reverses from advantage to exposure. The entire 2027 upside narrative rests on this.
  • Dilution is real and ongoing: over half the ATM used in one quarter; management explicitly keeps equity/convertibles in the mix for a >1 GW/yr 2027 build that dwarfs 2026.
  • Cash declined despite massive financing activity — the burn rate against $20–25B annual CapEx means the financing treadmill cannot slow; ABS market access at attractive spreads is assumed repeatable.
  • OCF quality still unaddressed: $2.3B again "supported by substantial customer prepayments"; no ex-prepayment disclosure; prepayments are a pull-forward that must be refinanced with new wins each year.
  • Vineland approval is still pending — management's confidence is now on the record, making any slip a direct credibility hit; community/political opposition risk acknowledged as structural.
  • Asset-light, auctions, and short-term deals are early and small; no signed asset-light partnerships, no Token Factory revenue, no concentration metrics — the highest-multiple parts of the story remain unquantified.
  • Transcript misstatements (ARR %, Nebius AI EBITDA $) and the unexplained cash bridge are minor but worth noting for reporting discipline.

Next-quarter watchlist

  • Vineland: does the layout-plan amendment get approved, and do Bloom fuel-cell deployment and tranche deliveries proceed on the stated timeline?
  • 2027 guidance: formal outlook promised "later this year" — capacity, pricing baseline (>$20M/MW), and how much capacity remains uncommitted.
  • Auction program: frequency, clearing prices vs. the 15% premium benchmark, and whether the winner's repeat participation materializes; any sign of clearing-price deceleration is an early demand signal.
  • Short-term premium deals: does the $40–50M/MW tier scale beyond one signed deal, and does it cannibalize or complement midterm pricing?
  • Asset-light: first signed partnership — counterparty quality, capacity committed, revenue/margin structure, and timing.
  • Financing: next ABS deals (size, spread vs. SOFR+250), any corporate debt, and whether the remaining 12.3M ATM shares get used; watch the cash balance against ~$5.7B/quarter CapEx run-rate.
  • Prepayments: whether the 50–60% CapEx coverage rises as targeted, and any disclosure of OCF ex-prepayments.
  • Margin: whether 50% Nebius AI / 41% group holds as 2H capacity ramps, and the claimed 2H27 owned-capacity margin uplift.
  • Connected power: progress toward 800MW–1GW by year-end and the revenue-conversion lag into 1H27; Vera Rubin first deployments late 2026/early 2027.
  • Token Factory/Tavily: any first revenue quantification; Tavily developer growth vs. the 1M→2.5M trajectory; enterprise certification conversion into paying customers.
  • Unresolved carryovers: customer concentration splits, Meta $15B option monetization, Avride/TripleTen deconsolidation, Pennsylvania site milestones.

Quarter in one view

  • Q1 FY2026: group revenue $399M, +684% YoY, +75% QoQ; Nebius AI (ex-TripleTen/Avride) revenue $390M, +841% YoY, +82% QoQ, 98% of group revenue.
  • Group adjusted EBITDA $130M (32% margin) vs. $15M in Q4 and -$54M a year ago; Nebius AI adjusted EBITDA margin 45%, up from 24% in Q4.
  • Net income $621M, driven by a noncash ClickHouse valuation adjustment — not operating performance.
  • Capacity sold out again; pipeline up 3.5x QoQ (record); ARR $1.9B at end of March, up >50% from $1.25B in Q4.
  • 2026 CapEx guidance raised to $20–25B (from $16–20B); full-year revenue ($3.0–3.4B), ARR ($7–9B), and ~40% group EBITDA margin guidance reiterated.
  • Contracted power now >3.5 GW (target raised to ≥4 GW this year); new 1.2 GW owned Pennsylvania site announced; >$6B raised YTD ($4.3B converts + $2B NVIDIA equity); cash $9.3B.

What management is focused on

  • Four execution dimensions: capacity/scale, full-stack product, customers/demand, capital.
  • Owning the full stack: owned contracted capacity now >75% of total power; second owned gigawatt-scale U.S. site (Pennsylvania, 1.2 GW).
  • Building software/inference/agentic layers via M&A (Tavily, Eigen, Clarifai) to deepen Token Factory and move beyond bare-metal offtake toward "hundreds of customers."
  • Financing the build-out "diligently": asset-backed financing against Microsoft/Meta contracts, corporate debt, customer prepayments, and an untapped 25M-share ATM program.
  • Framing the Meta $27B deal as a financing unlock and margin/optionality structure, not just revenue.

Key numbers and quarter mechanics

  • Revenue: $399M group; $390M Nebius AI; growth driven by capacity scaling plus "strong utilization of pricing."
  • ARR: $1.9B (Nebius AI) vs. $1.25B prior quarter — implies >50% sequential ARR growth.
  • EBITDA: group $130M / 32%; Nebius AI 45% margin; gap to group reflects Avride/TripleTen investment drag; management intends to find partners and deconsolidate these.
  • Cash flow: operating cash flow $2.3B (vs. -$198M a year ago), "primarily driven by upfront payments" — i.e., prepayments, a working-capital pull-forward, not pure earnings conversion.
  • Balance sheet: $9.3B cash; $4.3B converts at 1.25%/2.60% coupons; $2B NVIDIA equity.
  • CapEx: raised to $20–25B; >90% of the prior (February) range already secured by cash and contractual commitments; the incremental raise funds 1H27 capacity with customer commitments in place.
  • Margin path: Q2 guided lower (back-end-weighted capacity, front-loaded hiring/M&A costs), returning to Q1 levels in Q3, higher in Q4; full-year ~40% group.
  • Component inflation described as "low single digits" of total 2026 spend (transcript says "quite material, around low single digits" — internally ambiguous phrasing worth clarifying).

Product and launch scorecard

  • Aether 3.5 launched this quarter (no adoption/revenue metrics disclosed).
  • Token Factory: cited as primary inference product with "good product market fit"; new wins Revolut and monday.com; Eigen (#1 speed inference per NVIDIA, model-level) and Clarifai (system-level) acquired to strengthen it. No revenue contribution quantified.
  • Tavily: agentic search acquisition; rationale is developer adoption that would take "meaningfully longer" to build organically.
  • NVIDIA: Exemplar Cloud status on GB300 for training; early-deployment access to Vera Rubin/Vera CPU; line of sight to 5 GW capacity commitment by end-2030 tied to the $2B investment.
  • Inference called the fastest-growing segment; agentic layer acknowledged as "still to be defined" — honest but unquantified.
  • Software explicitly positioned as an enabler of compute economics, "not a separate revenue stream" — investors should not model a software revenue line.

Sell-side read-through

  • Pricing (Goldman Sachs): prices raised again in Q1, still selling out across all chip types, old and new; contract durations extending, ACVs rising, prepayments growing (including hyperscalers). No disclosure of what share of the book is older/short-term contracts that could reprice — question partially unanswered.
  • Margin step-down (Water Tower): management attributes Q2 dip purely to timing (costs land before capacity), not structural; Q3 recovery to Q1 levels, Q4 higher. This is the key claim to verify.
  • Vineland, NJ delay reports (Morgan Stanley): management did not directly confirm or deny site delays; answered that all Microsoft/Meta delivery commitments are on schedule, first Meta contract fully delivered in Q1, Microsoft tranches ramp midyear with most volume in Q3/Q4. The specific site question was effectively sidestepped.
  • Meta structure (Alex Platt): $12B committed dedicated compute (delivery early 2027) + $15B option where Nebius can sell to Meta or to AI-cloud customers at potentially higher prices; Meta backstops the capacity, enabling asset-backed financing; revenue could exceed $27B if market stays strong.
  • Pipeline (Northland): 3.5x QoQ growth excludes hyperscaler deals; covers AI cloud + Token Factory across AI natives, software vendors, enterprises; win rates "maintained" with faster cycles and higher ASPs — no conversion percentage given.
  • Concentration: management emphasizes AI-cloud diversification but gives no revenue-split numbers between Meta/Microsoft and the core cloud book.
  • Sold-out status: "vast majority" of capacity coming online over the next ~12 months is contracted or earmarked; 4+ customers competing per GPU; 2027 Vera Rubin capacity selling starts 2H26.

Management credibility

  • Delivery claims are specific and checkable: first Meta contract fully delivered in Q1; first Microsoft tranche November 2025; all commitments met to date.
  • Guidance behavior: raised CapEx and power targets (>3.5 GW contracted vs. >2 GW three months ago; target 3 GW → ≥4 GW) while reiterating revenue/margin guidance — consistent with a supply-constrained, sold-out narrative.
  • Willingness to guide margins down for Q2 with a mechanical explanation (timing, not structural) is a credibility positive; it creates a clear test in Q3.
  • Evasions/soft spots: no direct answer on Vineland delays; no quantification of Token Factory/software revenue; no customer-concentration percentages; no pipeline conversion rate; "low single digits" inflation described as "quite material" is confusing phrasing.
  • Net income headline ($621M) is mostly a noncash ClickHouse mark — management flagged it as noncash, which is appropriately transparent.

What changed versus the prior quarter

  • No prior-quarter summary supplied; baseline established from this transcript only. Transcript-referenced sequential changes: revenue +75% QoQ; Nebius AI margin 24% → 45%; group EBITDA $15M → $130M; ARR $1.25B → $1.9B; contracted power >2 GW → >3.5 GW; power target 3 GW → ≥4 GW; CapEx guide $16–20B → $20–25B; pipeline +3.5x QoQ (record).

Bull case

  • Demand durably exceeds supply: sold out across all GPU generations, prices raised and still clearing, 4+ bidders per GPU, durations and prepayments rising.
  • Meta structure is asymmetric: $15B backstop plus option to resell at premium prices enables cheap asset-backed financing and potential revenue above $27B.
  • Financing largely de-risked near term: $9.3B cash, >90% of prior CapEx range secured, multiple untapped levers (ABS on Microsoft/Meta credit, corporate debt, ATM, prepayments).
  • 2027 visibility: raised CapEx is tied to already-committed 1H27 capacity (Meta, Alabama, Missouri), not speculative build.
  • NVIDIA alignment (equity, early Vera Rubin access, 5 GW line of sight) secures supply in a constrained market; inference/Token Factory adds higher-value mix over time.

Bear case

  • Execution/timing risk is concentrated: 2026 delivery is back-end weighted; Q2 margin dip is guided, and the full-year ~40% margin depends on Q3/Q4 capacity landing on schedule — the unanswered Vineland delay question cuts directly at this.

  • Operating cash flow quality: $2.3B OCF is "primarily" customer prepayments — a pull-forward that flattens future periods and depends on continued contract wins.
  • Customer concentration: Meta ($27B + earlier deal) and Microsoft dominate the contracted book; no diversification metrics disclosed.
  • Reported net income flattered by a noncash ClickHouse mark; Avride/TripleTen remain a drag with deconsolidation only an "intention."
  • Political/community opposition to U.S. data centers acknowledged as a live risk with "no room for complacency."

Next-quarter watchlist

  • Q2 margin: does the guided dip stay within the "timing" narrative, and does Q3 return to ~45% Nebius AI / Q1 levels as promised?
  • Capacity delivery: Q3 step-up execution; any confirmation or denial of Vineland delays; Microsoft tranche ramp midyear; Pennsylvania first 250–300 MW by end-2027.
  • Financing: first asset-backed deals against Meta/Microsoft contracts — size, pricing, and whether the ATM gets used (dilution signal).
  • Pricing and prepayments: whether further price increases stick and prepayment records continue; watch OCF ex-prepayments.
  • Meta optionality: evidence Nebius is monetizing the $15B optional tranche at premium pricing vs. defaulting to Meta.
  • Token Factory/inference: any first revenue disclosure; integration milestones for Eigen/Clarifai/Tavily; Vera Rubin selling activity starting 2H26.
  • Avride/TripleTen: progress on strategic partners/deconsolidation.
  • Contracted power: progress toward the ≥4 GW 2026 target and the 5 GW NVIDIA-linked 2030 commitment.
