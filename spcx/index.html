    Quarter in one view

    • Q3 FY2026: revenue $14.6B (+161% y/y from $5.6B), first reported net income of $4.4B (vs. $1.1B loss y/y), adjusted EBITDA $8.8B (+306% y/y). Sequential revenue nearly doubled from $7.8B in Q2.
    • Segment mix: Space $1.8B (+84% q/q, +6% y/y); Connectivity $5.2B (+69% y/y, +22% q/q); SI $7.6B (+$5B q/q, +$6.7B y/y) — SI is now 52% of revenue and the dominant P&L driver.
    • SI segment adjusted EBITDA $5.0B (+$3.8B q/q); segment operating income $431M, or $1.6B excluding $1.2B of Cursor-related stock comp.
    • Capex $19.6B in Q3; Q4 capex guided sharply up to $35–40B (vs. ~$18B/quarter run-rate guided last quarter). Q1 2027 revenue guided to $33–37B — first forward quarterly revenue guide.
    • Backlog $63B (vs. $47.5B in Q2), of which $17.5B deferred revenue; ~$42B expected to be recognized within 12 months.
    • $100B ARR by December 2026 reaffirmed (defined as December month annualized). Musk introduced a new, much larger frame: $500B–$1T ARR by end of 2027.
    • Compute: 1.7GW nameplate at quarter end; ~2.2GW expected by end of October; 2.5–3GW by year-end; Musk "highly confident" of +10GW incremental in 2027 (~13GW total), with a 20GW incremental power/cooling plan and a "non-zero" chance of >20GW compute online in 2027.
    • Cursor acquisition closed mid-quarter at $60B equity value; contributed $608M of in-quarter revenue; $23B of the price allocated to retention/equity awards to be expensed over ~3 years.

    What management is focused on

    • Flight 15 dual catch attempt (next month): first attempt to recover both booster and ship; Musk says success "more likely than not." FAA license expected to include return-to-launch-site and tower catch for both stages. Reflight of second stage targeted early next year; on-orbit refueling attempt early next year.
    • Compute build-out as the core business: Musk spending ~4 days/week in Memphis on Colossus II; ~200,000 GB300s/month coming online for the rest of the year, shifting to Vera Rubin next year; 440,000-GPU Vera Rubin training cluster targeted online by end of Q2 2027.
    • Terafab: first disclosure of concrete chip-fab plans — test SRAM chip ~May/June 2027 at Austin research fab; volume production aspiration Q3/Q4 2028 at Gibbons Creek; long-term ambition of "a terawatt of SI chips per year." Musk acknowledged "insanely fast" timelines with "big error bars."
    • Starlink Mobile carrier build: FCC approval for 15,000 Mobile V2 sats; new low-band spectrum acquisition announced; Mobile V2 launches on Starship in 2027; service turn-up early 2028 (slipped from prior "end of 2027"); 22M monthly mobile users (+37% q/q).
    • Grok bot enterprise push: launched August; 800K paid weekly users, doubling in a month; wide enterprise deployment "within a few weeks"; Musk suggested Grok bot may ultimately consume most of the company's compute.
    • Louisiana spaceport: second Louisiana purchase; aspiration of first Starship launch 2028, "highly likely" at scale by 2029; ~20+ launch towers envisioned.
    • Tesla merger: both companies have independent special committees; no agreement on terms; Musk recused as conflicted; Tesla-side approval needs 50% of all shares outstanding.

    Key numbers and quarter mechanics

    • Revenue $14.6B (+161% y/y); net income $4.4B (first profitable quarter; +$5.5B y/y swing); adjusted EBITDA $8.8B (+306% y/y).
    • Space: $1.8B revenue (+84% q/q, +6% y/y) on customer mix; 35 launches in Q3 (21 Starlink Falcon 9, 2 Starship, 1 Falcon Heavy, 12 customer missions incl. 6 NSSL). Won 3 Dragon human spaceflight missions (through 2028), Artemis III Starlink comms contract, 18 new NSSL missions, a Starship lunar payload delivery contract, 3 Starfall missions.
    • Connectivity: $5.2B (+69% y/y, +22% q/q); 13.6M consumer subs (+1.6M net adds, vs. 1.7M record in Q2); ARPU +5% q/q to $69 (first ARPU increase disclosed; was flat at $66); 174 markets (Vietnam, Uganda new). Gross margin 55% including ~3pts one-time tariff-refund benefit; ex-one-time, EBITDA margin +~2pts y/y. Enterprise/government revenue +91% y/y, +18% q/q. ~12,000 aircraft contracted (~25% of available fleet); aviation production quadrupled YTD, to double again by year-end.
    • SI: $7.6B revenue; $7.0B from SI Solutions & Infrastructure including $608M Cursor; ex-Cursor, S&I revenue +192% q/q. Advertising +19% y/y. Segment EBITDA $5.0B; op income $431M ($1.6B ex-Cursor SBC). Grok Enterprise customers >$1M run-rate +50% q/q pro forma incl. Cursor.
    • Compute: 1.7GW nameplate at 9/30; ~2.2GW by end of October; 2.5–3GW year-end target (prior guide was ">2GW" — raised).
    • Pricing/payback: CFO says current pricing is "slightly above the high end" of the $30–50/watt range given last quarter; <1-year payback reiterated; Musk floated possible 6-month paybacks next year. Contracts described as short-term/spot-rate, renewing at higher $/watt; target ~50/50 internal vs. external compute split (vs. ~10% internal training estimate given last quarter — major change, see below).
    • Capex: $19.6B in Q3; Q4 guided $35–40B — roughly double the prior run-rate guide.
    • Backlog: $63B (+$15.5B q/q); $17.5B deferred revenue; ~$42B recognizable within 12 months.
    • Guidance: Q1 2027 revenue $33–37B (implies ~2.3x Q3 annualized pace); Q4 gross margins expected back to Q2-like levels (Q3 flattered by one-times and mix).

    Product and launch scorecard

    • Starship: Flight 14 (final week of Q3) reached orbit and deployed operational V3 Starlink satellites — the key Q2 watchlist item, delivered. Flight 14 carried 26 Tbps of bandwidth at ~half load, claimed equivalent to 10 Falcon 9 launches. Flight 15 dual-catch attempt next month; weekly cadence targeted H1 2027, 2x+/weekly by end-2027 — still aspirational.
    • V3 broadband satellites: operational deployment began; Shotwell now says "a few hundred" V3s needed for user-visible improvement, achievable by mid-2027 — this is a meaningful softening of last quarter's "~1,000 satellites by ~Q2 2027" framing. Starship launches to ramp toward >20x Falcon 9 downlink capacity per launch.
    • Starlink Mobile: 22M monthly users (+37% q/q); new carrier markets (Uganda, DRC, Kazakhstan, Costa Rica); FCC approval for 15,000 V2 sats; low-band spectrum deal announced; service timing now "early 2028" vs. "end of 2027" last quarter — a slip. Shotwell outlined architecture: S-band carries majority of traffic, low-band for building penetration, femtocell/small radios on Starlink install real estate. Still no terrestrial capex figure.
    • Aviation: ~12,000 planes contracted (~25% of fleet vs. "<10% penetration" claimed last quarter); 3 of 4 major US carriers signed; six international carriers flew first equipped aircraft in Q3. Claimed higher load factors on equipped flights — customer-reported, unverifiable.
    • Grok/Grok bot: Grok bot launched August; 800K paid weekly users, doubling month-over-month; enterprise wide deployment "within weeks." Grok 5 status not addressed on the call (was guided for year-end last quarter) — a disclosure gap.
    • Cursor: closed mid-quarter; $608M in-quarter revenue; integration "progressing well"; bundled plan (Grok + Grok bot + Cursor + X Premium) announced. $23B retention SBC over ~3 years is a real, now-quantified earnings drag.
    • Terafab: new program with dates (test chip mid-2027, volume Q3/Q4 2028). Musk himself flagged the timelines as aggressive; no capex number disclosed for the fab.
    • Starmind: Bastrop named as future production site; Mobile V2 and Starmind satellites to fly on Starship in 2027.

    Sell-side read-through

    • Only five analyst questions; Q&A was short and notably non-confrontational. No one challenged the $500B–$1T 2027 ARR frame, the Q4 capex doubling, or the Q1 2027 $33–37B guide.
    • Jonas (Morgan Stanley) opened with Terafab — extracted the first concrete fab timeline (test chip May/June 2027, volume Q3/Q4 2028) and implicit confirmation of ASML equipment orders ("appreciative of ASML... accelerating equipment deliveries"). Musk declined to discuss claimed fab process breakthroughs.
    • Anmuth (JPMorgan) pressed on power/cooling constraints behind the 13GW 2027 figure and funding. Musk's answer: turbine blade/vane casting is the industry bottleneck (12–18 months behind other turbine components) and SpaceX's rocket-engine casting capability bypasses it — the most specific, checkable competitive claim on the call. The broader 20GW power plan was withheld as "highly proprietary." CFO's funding answer was generic: operating cash flow, IG debt markets, vendor/customer partnerships — no quantum, no structure.
    • Yu (Deutsche Bank) asked about a Tesla merger. Musk confirmed special committees at both companies, no terms agreed, 50%-of-all-shares Tesla vote required. He endorsed the combination's merits while flagging the hurdle — the merger is now a disclosed live process, not rumor.
    • Maral (Evercore) again probed Starlink Mobile's competitive position vs. incumbents. Shotwell gave the most detailed network-architecture answer to date (85–93% of mobile traffic is WiFi; S-band majority, low-band for indoor penetration, small radios on existing installs) but Musk deflected the revenue sizing into a future "longer presentation," claiming a "clear path to several hundred billion dollars a year" in connectivity revenue. The capital-intensity question from last quarter remains unanswered.
    • Sheridan (Goldman) asked about internal vs. external compute allocation. Musk's answer — target ~50/50, dynamic, short-term/spot contracts, renewals at higher $/watt — directly contradicts last quarter's ~10%-for-Grok-training estimate and reframes the revenue model toward merchant/spot exposure. He claimed demand exceeds supply "several times over" and described hyperscalers as having no available compute (the "cheese shop" anecdote).
    • Investor-platform questions extracted two useful disclosures: V3 user impact at "a few hundred" satellites by mid-2027, and Louisiana first-launch aspiration 2028 / at-scale 2029.

    Management credibility

    • Delivered on prior commitments: Flight 14 V3 deployment (the central Q2 watch item) happened on schedule; Cursor closed; $100B ARR guide reaffirmed with a defined basis; compute tracking ahead of the prior >2GW year-end guide (2.5–3GW now); Q3 capex ($19.6B) landed near the ~$18B guide.
    • Improved specificity in places: backlog now $63B with $42B near-term conversion disclosed; Cursor economics quantified ($60B price, $23B retention SBC, $608M revenue); GPU deployment rate stated precisely (~200K GB300s/month); first forward revenue guide issued.
    • Concerns:
    • Guidance escalation continues: $500B–$1T ARR by end-2027 was delivered with a joking "crazy note" framing and no bridge; even the low end implies ~5x the December 2026 $100B ARR target within 12 months. Musk's own "I don't want to give Bret a heart attack" signals internal tension.
    • Compute targets keep moving up within and across calls: last quarter "closer to 10GW than 5GW by end-2027" became "highly confident of 13GW total by end-2027" plus a 20GW incremental power plan and a "non-zero" >20GW compute scenario. The numbers are now anchored to a stated deployment rate, which helps, but the ceiling keeps rising.
    • Internal/external compute split inconsistency: ~10% reserved for Grok training (Q2) vs. ~50/50 target (Q3) with no reconciliation. Either Grok's compute appetite grew ~5x as a share, or the earlier figure was loose.
    • V3 threshold quietly revised: "~1,000 satellites for service improvement, ~Q2 2027" became "a few hundred by mid-2027." Favorable direction, but the metric moved without acknowledgment.
    • Mobile timing slipped (end-2027 → early 2028) without being flagged as a slip.
    • Grok 5 silence: guided for year-end last quarter; not mentioned.
    • Q4 capex doubling ($35–40B vs. ~$18B run-rate guide) was presented as upside visibility, not a guide miss; funding plan for the step-up is qualitative.
    • Unverifiable superlatives persist: "most advanced casting capability on earth," "never seen anything grow this fast," load-factor claims, "hounded relentlessly" by hyperscalers.
    • Consistency check: CFO's 2.5–3GW year-end and Musk's "roughly 3 gigawatts... in the next few months" align; the 13GW 2027 figure is internally consistent between prepared remarks and Q&A. Net income positivity is consistent with the SI EBITDA ramp but flattered by the Cursor SBC exclusion in segment commentary — GAAP net income of $4.4B already includes it, which is a point in management's favor.

    What changed versus the prior quarter

    • GAAP profitability inflected: +$4.4B net income vs. -$541M in Q2 — the first profitable quarter, driven by SI.
    • SI became the majority of revenue (52% vs. 33%) and EBITDA ($5.0B of $8.8B); SI segment op income positive even including $1.2B Cursor SBC.
    • Capex guide broke sharply higher: Q4 guided $35–40B vs. "similar to Q2" (~$18B) commentary last quarter.
    • Compute targets raised across the board: year-end 2.5–3GW (was >2GW); 2027 frame moved from "closer to 10 than 5" cumulative to "highly confident" of 13GW total with a 20GW power plan.
    • Compute allocation framework reversed: ~10% internal training (Q2) → ~50/50 internal/external target (Q3); contract posture explicitly short-term/spot with renewals at higher $/watt — new disclosure.
    • ARR ambition escalated: $100B by Dec 2026 reaffirmed; new $500B–$1T by end-2027 introduced (vs. $1T-by-2030 last quarter — the timeline pulled in by 3 years).
    • Starlink ARPU rose for the first time ($66 → $69, +5% q/q) against prior flat/declining-mix commentary; net adds decelerated slightly (1.6M vs. 1.7M record).
    • V3 milestone achieved (Flight 14 deployment) but the user-impact threshold was revised down to "a few hundred" satellites by mid-2027 from ~1,000 by ~Q2 2027.
    • Mobile service timing slipped to early 2028 from end-2027; low-band spectrum portfolio expanded via new announced acquisition.
    • New programs disclosed: Terafab with dates; Louisiana spaceport with timeline; Tesla merger special committees confirmed.
    • Backlog +$15.5B q/q to $63B with first conversion-schedule disclosure (~$42B within 12 months).
    • First forward quarterly revenue guide ($33–37B Q1 2027) — a new disclosure practice.

    Bull case

    • The AI compute thesis is converting to GAAP earnings faster than promised: SI EBITDA $5.0B in the segment's second positive quarter, pricing above the high end of the prior $30–50/watt range, renewals at higher $/watt, and paybacks possibly compressing toward 6 months.
    • Demand visibility improved materially: backlog +33% q/q to $63B with $42B converting within a year; Q1 2027 guide of $33–37B implies the December $100B ARR target is credible and likely conservative.
    • Deployment velocity is the moat: ~200K GPUs/month coming online, a proprietary workaround (casting) for the industry's turbine bottleneck, and claimed inability of peers to match power/cooling delivery — checkable against the 2.2GW October and 2.5–3GW year-end marks.
    • Flight 14 de-risked the Starship→V3 deployment chain; a successful Flight 15 dual catch would validate the reusability economics underpinning broadband, mobile, and Starmind simultaneously.
    • Connectivity is compounding with improving quality: ARPU up, gross margin 55%, aviation at ~25% fleet penetration with production quadrupling, enterprise/government +91% y/y.
    • Cursor adds a scaled enterprise sales channel and $608M/quarter revenue run-rate; Grok bot's early enterprise traction (800K paid weekly users, doubling monthly) is a second monetization path for internal compute.

    Bear case

    • The capex step-up is extreme: $35–40B in Q4 alone (~$150B+ annualized) against a funding plan described only qualitatively; IG status is asserted, not demonstrated, at this spend level.
    • Revenue quality in SI remains opaque: short-term/spot contracts cut both ways — the bull case for pricing is also an admission of no long-term contracted floor; no counterparty concentration disclosed for the cloud agreements driving the $5B q/q SI revenue jump.
    • The 50/50 internal/external compute split contradicts last quarter's ~10% internal figure without explanation, raising questions about whether external demand or internal Grok needs are actually driving the build.
    • Guidance inflation is accelerating: $500B–$1T ARR by end-2027 has no disclosed bridge and was delivered semi-jokingly; Terafab timelines are self-described as "insanely fast"; the 20GW power plan is deliberately undisclosed.
    • Execution stack now includes: dual catch (Flight 15), second-stage reflight, on-orbit refueling, V3 ramp, Mobile V2 constellation, early-2028 mobile service, 440K-GPU training cluster by Q2 2027, Terafab test chip, Louisiana build-out, Cursor integration, and a possible Tesla merger — all within ~6 quarters.
    • Q3 quality items: Connectivity gross margin included ~3pts of one-time tariff refunds; Q4 margins guided back down to Q2 levels; consumer net adds decelerated sequentially; mobile service timing slipped quietly.
    • Cursor's $23B retention SBC will suppress SI segment op income for ~3 years; the $60B price for a company contributing $608M/quarter implies a very high multiple paid with stock.
    • Tesla merger overhang: a 50%-of-all-shares Tesla vote is a high bar, and any combination would import new risks and complexity into the equity story.

    Next-quarter watchlist

    • Q4 capex delivery vs. the $35–40B guide and the funding mix actually used (debt issuance, vendor financing, customer prepayments); any update to IG-rating commentary.
    • $100B ARR confirmation: December annualized revenue disclosure; Q1 2027 results vs. the $33–37B guide — the first test of the new forward-guidance practice.
    • Compute trajectory: 2.5–3GW year-end; monthly GPU deployment rate into 2027; evidence for the 13GW 2027 claim (power procurement, turbine deliveries, casting output); any disclosure of the proprietary 20GW power plan.
    • SI contract disclosure: tenors, renewal pricing evidence, counterparty concentration, and whether "spot-rate, short-term" posture persists if pricing softens; internal/external split actuals vs. the 50/50 target.
    • V3 satellite count on orbit vs. the "few hundred by mid-2027" threshold; per-launch payload ramp toward the >20x Falcon 9 claim; user-visible speed/latency improvements.
    • Starlink Mobile: low-band spectrum deal closing/terms; terrestrial capex emergence (still undisclosed); Mobile V2 launch start in 2027; confirmation or further slip of the early-2028 service date.
    • Connectivity economics: Q4 activations from the retail inventory build; ARPU trend after the $69 print; aviation install/activation pace toward the 2027 acceleration claim; enterprise/government growth vs. the 91% y/y mark.
    • Grok disclosures: Grok 5 status (guided year-end, unmentioned this call); Grok bot enterprise wide-deployment evidence; the 440K Vera Rubin training cluster by end-Q2 2027.

    • Starlink ARPU flat and management concedes blended ARPU will decline with geographic mix; consumer growth must carry margin while V3 R&D spends.
