Quarter in one view

  • June-quarter records across the board but with asterisks: revenue $109.4B, +16% y/y (vs. +14–17% guide — landed mid-range, not a beat of the prior magnitude); EPS $2.02, +29% y/y, including an $0.11 benefit from tariff refunds; net income $29.8B.
  • iPhone $54.3B, +22% y/y (third consecutive 20%+ quarter; 22% YTD) despite constraints; Mac surged +29% to $10.4B (June record) on MacBook Neo and MacBook Pro; Services decelerated to +12% ($30.7B), a June record but FX-hit and softer than guided trajectory; iPad −6% on the flagged A16 compare.
  • Headline gross margin 50.1% (+80 bps q/q) is inflated: tariff refunds contributed ~2pp; ex-refunds, GM was 48.1% — the midpoint of the 47.5–48.5% guide, with memory costs the dominant drag.
  • Pivotal forward signal: September guide of +9–11% y/y is a sharp deceleration (~500bps) driven by a ~2.5pp sequential FX headwind and a "significantly increasing" supply-constraint impact now spanning iPhone, Mac, and iPad — a broadening of constraints, not just a shift.
  • Cook's final earnings call; transition confirmed as "seamless," Ternus on the call but only answered one question with a non-answer.

What management is focused on

  • Farewell framing, not new strategy: Cook's remarks doubled as a valedictory; Ternus's single substantive answer ("really focused on our plans and very excited about it") revealed nothing about his posture.
  • Memory crisis management as the central financial theme: Cook introduced "100-year flood" language, confirmed Apple "reluctantly raised prices" (new — on iPad and Mac, per the Rakers exchange), and quantified escalating costs with decreasing carry-in inventory offsets beyond September.
  • Supply constraints reframed as forecast error: Cook was unusually candid — "the root cause… is a demand forecast issue, to be candid," with the supply chain having "less flexibility than normal" and "a quarter that we're going to be scrambling on."
  • Siri AI as the post-launch proof point: developer and public betas live with "overwhelmingly positive" feedback; first monetization signal disclosed — iCloud+ tier upgrades for heavy AI usage (explicitly framed as "not a complete plan").
  • U.S. manufacturing escalation: Broadcom agreement expected to exceed $30B ("largest-ever American manufacturing program commitment"), custom silicon/wireless components; Mac mini production coming to the Houston facility alongside AI server assembly; tariff refunds to be reinvested in the U.S.
  • Apple Upgrade: new U.S.-only hardware leasing program with Klarna, retail-channel only; Cook leaned on high residual values as the economic logic.

Key numbers and quarter mechanics

  • Revenue $109.4B (+16%); Products $78.7B (+18%); Services $30.7B (+12%). June-quarter records in every geographic segment; double-digit growth in every segment.
  • iPhone $54.3B (+22%, June record, upgraders record, IDC share gains, Worldpanel #1 in U.S./urban China/U.K./France/Australia/Japan, 99% U.S. satisfaction). Mac $10.4B (+29%; all-time records for upgraders and new-to-Mac). iPad $6.2B (−6%, flagged compare; >half of buyers new). Wearables $7.9B (+6%; Apple Watch upgrader record).
  • Gross margin 50.1% headline, 48.1% ex-tariff-refund (~2pp benefit at company level, >2.5pp on Products GM which printed 40.1%, +140 bps q/q). Services GM 75.6%, −110 bps q/q on mix.
  • Sequential GM bridge (Parekh, unusually specific): 49.3% (March) → 48.1% (June ex-refund): −120bps, "more than 100%" explained by memory, partly offset by carry-in inventory, lower non-memory BOM costs, and favorable mix; FX "minimal." June → 46.5% (September midpoint ex-refund): another −160bps, again "more than" explained by memory.
  • OpEx $19.1B, +23% y/y (top of the $18.8–19.1B guide), driven by R&D.
  • Operating cash flow $34.4B (June record). Cash/securities $147B; debt $84B; net cash ~$63B. Capital return jumped back to $33B ($4B dividends + $25.8B buybacks) vs. $15B last quarter. Dividend held at $0.27.
  • Paid subscriptions surpassed 1.5B; transacting and paid accounts at all-time highs.
  • September guide: revenue +9–11%; iPhone mid-teens y/y; Services "largely similar" to June's 12% after removing ~2.5pp sequential FX (implies high-single-digit reported); GM 47–48% including ~1pp tariff-refund benefit (~46–47% ex-refund); OpEx $19.1–19.4B; OI&E ~$350M; tax ~16.5%. No color on December despite direct questioning.

Product and launch scorecard

  • iPhone 17 family: +22% for a third straight quarter, share gains, record upgraders — the franchise is carrying the company. But September faces both FX and supply constraints; no longer framed as unconstrained.
  • Mac: +29% (vs. +6% last quarter) — the constraint-easing payoff arrived, with MacBook Neo the standout: half of U.S. education large purchases displaced Windows/Chromebook; Pinellas County (25,000 students), Peninsula SD (8,000 from Chromebooks), Midwest City-Del City (6,000, all-Apple district). Mac positioned as the enterprise on-device AI platform: Disney (reduced cloud token costs, IP security), Crédit Agricole (80% reduction in manual processing time), Morgan Stanley (20,000 iPhone 17 deployment).
  • iPad: −6%, worse than the flat-ish "tough compare" framing implied; install base still at a record. Now also subject to price increases, elasticity unknown.
  • Wearables: +6% (AirPods Pro 3, AirPods Max 2, Apple Watch records) — steady, no new disclosure.
  • Siri AI: the quarter's most important product event — unveiled at WWDC, in developer and public beta, "phenomenal" early reviews per Cook. Caveats: not launching initially in EU (DMA — working with the Commission; Mac exempt) or China (only baseline Apple Intelligence features like Cleanup just approved); compute-cost plan explicitly incomplete; iCloud+ upsell as the first monetization lever.
  • Services: +12% with records in every category and all-time records in cloud and payments, but quality issues surfaced: mobile gaming softness in the App Store, prior-year F1 theatrical revenue not repeating, U.S. court ruling on link-out transactions (Supreme Court to hear appeal), and FX hitting Services harder than total company (~5pp headwind March→September).
  • Apple Upgrade: U.S.-only, retail-only, Klarna-partnered leasing; no adoption targets given — "we'll see what the customer uptake is."

Sell-side read-through

  • The 500bps September deceleration dominated: Daryanani and Reitzes both decomposed it. Concession extracted: FX (~2.5pp) plus "significantly increasing" constraints gets "pretty close" to the full decel — implying little organic demand deceleration, but Parekh flatly refused any December color even as Reitzes flagged Street consensus decelerating to 8–9%.
  • Constraint attribution pinned down: advanced-node SoC availability remains the root cause; Cook admitted a demand forecast miss on both iPhone and Mac. Reitzes's Arizona-fab diversification question (an oblique TSMC-capacity probe) was deflected — "not a partner or supplier issue" — while noting 100M+ components already sourced from Arizona.
  • Memory interrogation produced real news: Daryanani got confirmation of active sourcing-flexibility evaluation (DRAM's three-supplier structure named); Woodring got the pricing disclosure — Apple has raised iPad and Mac prices, "reluctantly," citing "100-year flood" memory inflation. But Woodring's margin-dollars-vs.-margin-percent question got the same non-answer as Mohan's last quarter ("we look at units, revenue, and margin… not a mathematical formula"). LTA intentions went unanswered.
  • Services finally got forensic treatment (Woodring): Parekh provided the fullest Services bridge to date — FX main driver, F1 theatrical compare, mobile gaming softness, App Store model changes from court rulings — while insisting June "roughly met" expectations with "a bit more softness on mobile gaming."
  • Demand pull-forward risk raised (Rakers): Cook said it's "not obvious in the data" for iPhone but conceded iPad/Mac price elasticity is unknowable until channel inventory clears — an honest admission of a live risk.
  • Ternus's debut was content-free: Mohan's competitive-landscape question (OpenAI device, SpaceX phone) got a two-sentence deflection. Analysts got no read on the incoming CEO.

Management credibility

  • Guide was met, not beaten: revenue +16% inside +14–17%; GM ex-refunds at the guided midpoint (48.1%). The tariff refund ($0.11 EPS, ~2pp GM) flattered the headline — credit to Parekh for decomposing it explicitly rather than letting the 50.1% stand unqualified.
  • The Mac constraint call was validated: last quarter's "several months to balance" on Mac preceded a +29% quarter and records for new-to-Mac — the demand-undercall narrative now has a delivered proof point.
  • iPad compare warning also validated (−6%). Services "similar ex-FX" guidance roughly met, by Parekh's own framing.
  • Memory chronology honored exactly: "significantly higher in June" happened; "increasing impact beyond" reiterated with carry-in offsets now explicitly diminishing after September. This is consistent, escalating, and still without a mitigation framework.
  • New disclosure quality improved: the sequential GM bridges (March→June→September, in bps, memory ">100%" of the change) are the most precise margin mechanics Apple has given on this issue — credibility-enhancing specificity under Chatterjee's pressure.
  • Credibility dents: the September constraint broadening (now iPhone, Mac, and iPad) was not previewed — last quarter's framing implied iPhone was past its constraint ("to a lesser extent" this quarter, then "increase significantly" next). The pricing action (iPad/Mac increases) surfaced only under questioning, not in prepared remarks. OpEx again landed at the very top of guide. Ternus's content-free cameo is a missed chance to establish credibility with the Street.
  • Still unresolved evasions: memory mitigation specifics, LTA posture, December guidance, Siri AI unit/engagement metrics, Google relationship (now entirely absent — not even mentioned this quarter), tariff refund dollar amounts.

What changed versus the prior quarter

  • Supply constraints broadened: from Mac-primary (June) to all three hardware lines (iPhone, Mac, iPad) in September, with impact "significantly increasing" — the guide decelerates ~500bps partly on this.
  • Memory went from cost headwind to pricing action: Apple "reluctantly" raised iPad and Mac prices — the first concrete mitigation lever disclosed, revealed only in Q&A. Carry-in inventory offset now has an expiry date ("decreasing benefit… beyond September").
  • Tariff refunds moved from application to receipt: ~2pp of GM and $0.11 of EPS this quarter, ~1pp embedded in the September guide; pledged for U.S. reinvestment. Dollar totals still undisclosed.
  • Capital return re-anchored: buybacks snapped back to $25.8B from $11B, quieting (for now) the post-net-cash-neutral discretionary-return concern.
  • Services decelerated hard: 16% → 12%, with newly named idiosyncratic drags (F1 theatrical compare, mobile gaming softness, App Store court-ruling changes) on top of FX.
  • Mac flipped from constrained +6% to +29%; iPad flipped +8% → −6%; iPhone held 22% but lost its "constraint receding" status.
  • Siri AI went from "coming this year" to shipped-in-beta with named regional exclusions (EU, China) — progress plus a new regulatory risk disclosure.
  • Cook's tenure formally ended on this call; transition is now the standing fact, not the announcement.

Bull case

  • Three straight quarters of 22% iPhone growth with June records in every geography, share gains, and record upgraders; the September decel is arithmetically explained by FX plus supply, not demand — Cook's "scrambling on the supply side" and "demand forecast issue" candidness implies upside leverage if nodes free up.
  • Mac is now a demonstrated growth engine: +29% with Neo converting Windows/Chromebook fleets at scale (25K, 8K, 6K unit wins) and enterprise on-device AI case studies quantifying value (Crédit Agricole 80% processing-time cut, Disney token-cost reduction).
  • Siri AI is real and in users' hands with strong early reception — the AI narrative shifts from "unshipped promise" to "beta with phenomenal reviews," with an identified (if unpriced) monetization path via iCloud+.
  • Pricing power demonstrated: Apple raised hardware prices and still guided mid-teens iPhone growth — elasticity untested but the willingness to price through the memory shock protects dollar profit.
  • Margin ex-refunds (48.1%) still at guided midpoint with named offsets (BOM deflation, mix) partially countering memory; tariff refunds continue (~1pp) into September.
  • Buybacks restored to $25.8B; net cash ~$63B; the $600B U.S. commitment now has a flagship $30B+ Broadcom program.

Bear case

  • The September guide embeds a "significant" sequential worsening of constraints across all three product lines in the most important pre-holiday ramp quarter — and management refused to rule out December deterioration while Street models 8–9%.
  • Memory trajectory is now explicit and worsening: −120bps GM in June, −160bps more guided into September, "increasing impact" beyond, carry-in offsets expiring. Ex-refund GM of ~46.5% would be the lowest in years, and management still won't frame the endgame (share vs. price vs. margin).
  • Services quality is eroding at the margin: 12% growth (high-single-digit reported next quarter), gaming softness, a U.S. court ruling already changing App Store economics, and FX exposure worse than the total company (~5pp March→September). The 16% era may have been FX-flattered.
  • Price increases on iPad/Mac introduce elasticity risk precisely when Cook admits the answer is unknowable ("too early to come to a definitive conclusion") — a demand-air-pocket scenario exists for H2.
  • Siri AI is gated in the EU and China — Apple's two most regulatorily contested regions — with no launch timeline; compute-cost plans are admittedly "not complete," and OpEx (+23% y/y, guided higher) is already outgrowing revenue.
  • Transition risk is unpriced-in either direction: Ternus gave the Street nothing to evaluate, and the first Ternus-led quarter opens with the weakest growth guide in two years.

Next-quarter watchlist

  • Supply: do constraints peak in September as implied, or does Cook's "scrambling" extend into December? First Ternus-era guidance on December will be the key tell — any continued refusal to comment will read as caution.
  • Memory: does GM land at/above the ~46.5% ex-refund midpoint? Concrete mitigations owed: LTAs (Woodring's unanswered question), alternative DRAM sourcing (Daryanani's), further pricing. Watch carry-in inventory depletion post-September.
  • Price elasticity: first read on iPad/Mac demand post-increases — channel inventory normalization timing is Cook's own stated gating factor ("weeks ahead").
  • Services: reported growth vs. the implied high-single-digit; whether gaming softness and App Store model changes persist; any Supreme Court link-out developments; iCloud+ AI upsell attach commentary.
  • Siri AI: fall OS launch execution, EU/China resolution progress (China baseline Apple Intelligence rollout just beginning), beta-to-GA quality, and any quantified engagement or monetization.
  • Tariffs: refund dollar amounts and cadence (two straight quarters of guided benefit now); whether rates hold per guide assumptions.
  • Ternus era: his first call as CEO, any shift in guidance philosophy, capital-return posture, or disclosure practices; whether buyback pace holds near $25B.
  • iPhone 17 cycle: does mid-teens September guidance prove conservative given the demand-forecast miss admission, and does the iPhone 18 launch land against a 22%-growth comp?
