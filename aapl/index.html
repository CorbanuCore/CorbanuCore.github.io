Quarter in one view

  • Q3 FY2026 (June): revenue $109.4B, +16% YoY — June-quarter record, within the +14–17% guide, achieved despite supply constraints and sequential FX headwinds. Diluted EPS $2.02 (+29%, June record); net income $29.8B; OCF $34.4B — all June records even excluding tariff-refund benefit.
  • Products $78.7B (+18%); Services $30.7B (+12%, June record — a deceleration from 16% and slightly below the "similar to March ex-FX" guide; management says it "roughly met" expectations with mobile gaming softer).
  • Segments: iPhone $54.3B (+22%, June record); Mac $10.4B (+29%, June record, constrained); iPad $6.2B (-6%, the flagged A16 compare landed); Wearables $7.9B (+6%); Services $30.7B (+12%).
  • Gross margin 50.1% (+80bps QoQ) — but flattered by ~2pts of tariff refunds; ex-refund 48.1%, the midpoint of the 47.5–48.5% guide. Products GM 40.1% (+140bps QoQ, incl. >2.5pts refund benefit); Services GM 75.6% (-110bps QoQ on mix).
  • OpEx $19.1B (+23% YoY), at the top of the $18.8–19.1B guide, driven by R&D/AI investment.
  • Tariff refunds received and flowing through the P&L: ~2pts to company GM, $0.11 to EPS; proceeds earmarked for US reinvestment.
  • Capital: cash $147B, debt $84B, net cash ~$63B. Returned $33B ($25.8B buybacks, $4B dividends) — a sharp re-acceleration from last quarter's $11B buyback.
  • Structural events: Cook's final earnings call (Ternus takes over; transition "seamless"); Siri AI unveiled at WWDC and in developer/public beta; Apple "reluctantly" raised prices on iPad and Mac due to memory costs — a first in this run.
  • FQ4 (September) guide: revenue +9–11% YoY (FX -2.5pts sequential; supply constraints "increase significantly" and now hit iPhone, Mac, AND iPad); iPhone mid-teens growth; Services similar to June ex-FX; GM 47–48% (incl. ~1pt refund benefit → ~46.5% underlying); OpEx $19.1–19.4B; OI&E ~$350M; tax ~16.5%.

What management is focused on

  • The handoff: Cook's farewell remarks dominated framing; Ternus was on the call but said almost nothing — his only answer (competitive landscape/OpenAI-device question) was a two-sentence deflection to "our plans." No strategic content from the incoming CEO.
  • Supply constraints as a demand problem: Cook repeatedly reframed constraints as a "demand forecast issue, to be candid" — iPhone +22% YTD and Mac +29% exceeded even high internal expectations. Advanced-node SoC capacity remains the root gate; "we've been pulling supply ahead. At some point, there's a limit to that. We've got a quarter that we're going to be scrambling on."
  • Memory "100-year flood": Cook's strongest language yet — "exponential increases in memory prices." June costs were "significantly higher" (as forecast); September higher still, partly offset by carry-in inventory (benefit decreasing beyond September) and lower non-memory BOM costs; market pricing "continuing to increase" beyond September with "increasing impact" possible.
  • Pricing philosophy disclosed for the first time: prices raised "reluctantly" on iPad and Mac; framework is units + revenue + margin judged holistically over the long term, "not a mathematical formula" — a partial answer to the question flatly refused last quarter.
  • Siri AI as the centerpiece: "completely reimagined," private, personal-context, cross-platform; developer and public beta feedback "overwhelmingly positive." Monetization thinking is nascent: heavy users could "buy up the stack on iCloud+"; no complete compute-cost plan yet.
  • US manufacturing escalation: new Broadcom multi-year agreement expected to exceed $30B (largest-ever American Manufacturing Program commitment) for custom silicon/wireless; Houston Advanced Manufacturing Center opening soon, Mac mini production there later this year; 100M+ components from Arizona this year; tariff refunds to be reinvested in the US.
  • Apple Upgrade: new US-only hardware leasing program with Klarna, retail-channel only; positioned around high residual values; no adoption targets given.

Key numbers and quarter mechanics

  • iPhone $54.3B (+22%): June records in every segment; June upgrader record; installed base all-time high; IDC global share gain; top-selling model in US, urban China, UK, France, Australia, Japan (Worldpanel); 99% US satisfaction. Third consecutive ~22% quarter; +22% YTD.
  • Mac $10.4B (+29%): driven by MacBook Pro and MacBook Neo; all-time records for upgraders and new-to-Mac; all-time emerging-markets record; Greater China all-time record; 95% US satisfaction (down from 97% last quarter's cited figure).
  • iPad $6.2B (-6%): the pre-announced A16 compare; installed base all-time high; >half of buyers new.
  • Wearables $7.9B (+6%): wearables and accessories strength; Watch June upgrader record; >half of Watch buyers new.
  • Services $30.7B (+12%): records in every category; all-time records in cloud and payments; June records in advertising, App Store, AppleCare, Music, TV+; 1.5B+ paid subscriptions; transacting/paid accounts all-time highs. Deceleration bridge from 16%: FX (main driver), prior-year F1 theatrical release, mobile gaming softness, App Store business-model changes in certain countries, US link-out court ruling (Supreme Court appeal being heard). FX is a ~5pt headwind to Services YoY growth March→September.
  • GM bridge: 50.1% reported; ex-refund 48.1%. March→June underlying decline of 120bps is "more than 100%" explained by memory, partly offset by carry-in inventory, lower non-memory BOM, and mix. June→September guide midpoint (46.5% ex-refund) is another ~160bps decline, again memory-driven; FX minimal on GM.
  • EPS $2.02 (+29%) includes $0.11 tariff-refund benefit; underlying ~$1.91.
  • Cash $147B; debt $84B; net cash ~$63B (roughly flat QoQ). Buybacks re-accelerated to $25.8B from $11B — no explanation offered for either the prior slowdown or the resumption.
  • Installed base: 2.5B+ active devices, all-time highs across categories and geographies.

Product and launch scorecard

  • MacBook Neo: now a proven share-taking weapon — ~half of US education large purchases displaced Windows/Chromebooks; Pinellas County (25,000 students from Windows), Peninsula SD 401 (8,000 from Chromebooks), Midwest City-Del City (6,000+, all-Apple district); enterprise traction in bank branches/retail. Still no revenue or margin disclosure — and it now carries a price increase.
  • Mac as AI platform: evidence deepening — Mac mini as agentic-AI platform, Mac Studio clusters running "frontier class models locally"; Disney using Mac on-device AI to cut cloud token costs and protect IP; Crédit Agricole cutting manual processing 80%+ on MacBook Pro; Morgan Stanley deployed 20,000+ iPhone 17s. The most concrete enterprise-AI proof points Apple has given.
  • Siri AI: the quarter's biggest product event — unveiled at WWDC, developer beta at keynote, public beta weeks ago; feedback "phenomenal"/"overwhelmingly positive" per Cook (no independent metrics). Not launching initially in EU (DMA — Mac version will be offered there; working with the Commission) or China (original Apple Intelligence features approved last week; Siri AI needs "more work," "front end" of the process). The two-year-old "coming this year" commitment appears on track for the US fall launch — but two of the largest markets are excluded at launch.
  • Apple Upgrade (Klarna leasing): launched this week, US-only, retail-only; early feedback "quite positive"; no adoption or replacement-cycle data — a watch item, not yet a driver.
  • iPad: -6% as pre-flagged; price increase now applied with elasticity "too early" to judge.
  • Wearables: +6%, second consecutive growth quarter; AirPods live translation via Apple Intelligence highlighted.
  • Services: Apple TV+ record viewership, 89 Emmy nominations; Apple Sports expanded to 170+ countries for the World Cup; F1 season engagement; mobile gaming softness is the one disclosed weak spot.

Sell-side read-through

  • September guide deconstruction (Daryanani, Reitzes): the +9–11% guide vs. Street at ~12% — management attributes the gap to FX (-2.5pts sequential) plus "significantly" increasing constraints across iPhone, Mac, and iPad. Reitzes pressed on December (Street ~8–9%): Parekh flatly refused any color beyond September — the same refusal pattern as post-June last quarter.
  • Memory (Daryanani, Woodring, Chatterjee): the dominant theme. New disclosures: DRAM is a three-supplier market; more suppliers "would help on supply, perhaps pricing — unclear"; "evaluating all options" on sourcing flexibility. No LTA commitment given despite Woodring's direct multi-year-LTA question. Chatterjee extracted the cleanest GM math: memory explains >100% of both the March→June (-120bps) and June→September (-160bps) underlying declines.
  • Pricing (Woodring): the quarter's biggest disclosure shift — Apple confirmed it raised prices on iPad and Mac ("reluctantly," "100-year flood" memory pricing) and gave a units/revenue/margin judgment framework. Elasticity on the increases is explicitly unknown ("weeks ahead" to read).
  • Services deceleration (Woodring): pressed on App Store/third-party data and even AI cannibalizing App Store time; Parekh gave the fullest Services bridge ever (FX, F1 theatrical compare, mobile gaming, business-model changes, link-out ruling) but no quantification of each factor. September implies below-10% reported Services growth per Woodring's math — not disputed.
  • Siri AI economics (Ng, Mohan): compute-cost recovery via iCloud+ tier upsell is the only monetization lever named; "I don't want to say that we have a complete plan." Capital-intensity question deflected to the hybrid cloud model and "we'll see."
  • Demand pull-forward (Rakers): Cook says 22% YTD iPhone growth shows no obvious pull-forward; iPad/Mac price-increase elasticity unresolved.
  • Ternus's debut (Mohan): a content-free answer on AI-device competition — the Street got no strategic signal from the incoming CEO on his first call.

Management credibility

  • Delivered the guide: +16% within +14–17%; GM ex-refund (48.1%) at the guided midpoint; OpEx $19.1B at the top of the range; tax and OI&E in line. The tariff-refund benefit (~2pts GM, $0.11 EPS) was transparently broken out — good disclosure practice.
  • Services modestly missed the spirit of the guide: 12% vs. "similar to March ex-FX" (~13% underlying); management conceded "a bit more softness on mobile gaming" but claimed "roughly met" — a small credibility dent, honestly handled.
  • Memory forecasting remains accurate: last quarter's "significantly higher June" confirmed; the escalation chronology (December → March → June → September → beyond) has been consistent and is now four quarters deep. The "100-year flood" / "exponential increases" language is new and more alarmed than prior framing.
  • The pricing refusal broke: after flatly refusing Mohan's pricing-framework question last quarter, Cook volunteered the price increases and the philosophy this quarter — candor improved under pressure, though only after the increases were already in the market.
  • Siri: the "coming this year" commitment was converted into a shipped beta — a major credibility deposit. But the EU/China exclusions were disclosed at WWDC, not emphasized on this call until Chatterjee asked; China Siri AI timing remains genuinely open.
  • Constraint narrative consistent: Mac-led June constraints confirmed; September broadening to all three hardware lines is new and was disclosed proactively in the guide. The "demand forecast issue" admission is notable self-critique.
  • Capital return whiplash unexplained: $25B → $11B → $25.8B buybacks across three quarters with no rationale either time; net-cash-neutral replacement framework still absent.
  • New verifiable claims: Broadcom >$30B agreement; Mac mini in Houston later this year; 100M+ Arizona components this year; ~50% Windows/Chromebook displacement in US education Neo purchases; 1.5B paid subscriptions; Supreme Court hearing the link-out appeal.
  • Persistent refusals: foregone-revenue quantification, December-quarter color, LTA specifics, Siri AI compute-cost plan, Neo economics, iPhone mix, Google deal economics (never mentioned this quarter — notably absent even as Siri AI shipped).

What changed versus the prior quarter

  • Pricing regime shift: Apple raised hardware prices (iPad, Mac) for the first time in this cycle — memory costs moved from margin-absorbed to partially passed through. This is the single biggest strategic change.
  • Constraints broadened: from Mac-led (June) to iPhone + Mac + iPad (September), with impact "increase significantly" — the first time all three hardware lines are constrained simultaneously, and the first guide where constraints (not demand) cap growth at 9–11%.
  • Siri went from promise to product: WWDC unveiling, developer + public betas shipped — but with EU/China carve-outs now explicit.
  • Tariffs became a cash event: refunds received, ~2pts GM / $0.11 EPS benefit in Q3, ~1pt embedded in the Q4 GM guide, proceeds pledged to US reinvestment.
  • Buybacks re-accelerated: $11B → $25.8B, total return $33B — the prior quarter's slowdown reversed without explanation.
  • Services decelerated for real: 16% → 12%, with mobile gaming softness and App Store model changes now named as headwinds beyond FX; September implies further reported deceleration.
  • Mac inflected hard: +6% → +29% (best June ever) on Neo + Pro, despite constraints; iPad flipped +8% → -6% as pre-flagged.
  • GM trajectory clarified: reported 50.1% is a refund-inflated peak; underlying 48.1% → guided ~46.5% — two consecutive ~120–160bps underlying steps down, all memory.
  • Cook era closed on the call itself; Ternus present but silent on strategy — leadership transition moved from announcement to execution.

Bull case

  • Demand is extraordinary and self-confessed underforecast: iPhone +22% for three straight quarters (+22% YTD), Mac +29%, constraints caused by a "demand forecast issue" — the September 9–11% guide is supply-capped, not demand-capped, and Cook says the company will be "scrambling" for supply.
  • Mac is a genuine second growth engine: +29% with Neo taking share from Windows/Chromebooks at scale (~50% displacement in US education large purchases) and enterprise AI workloads (Disney, Crédit Agricole) creating a new demand category.
  • Siri AI shipped in beta to strong early reception — the AI narrative now has a product, a privacy differentiator, an on-device cost advantage, and a named monetization path (iCloud+ upsell) on 2.5B devices and 1.5B paid subscriptions.
  • Pricing power demonstrated: Apple raised prices and still guides iPhone to mid-teens growth; high residual values underpin the new Upgrade leasing program, which could shorten replacement cycles.
  • Margin management is working: despite "exponential" memory inflation, underlying GM steps down only ~120–160bps per quarter with offsets (carry-in inventory, non-memory BOM, mix) actively managed; reported GM still 50.1%.
  • Capital return is back at full throttle: $33B returned, $147B cash, $34.4B quarterly OCF.
  • Services records in every category with cloud, payments, video, and advertising all growing double-digit — the deceleration is FX/compare/gaming-specific, not broad-based.

Bear case

  • Underlying GM is in a clear downtrend: 49.3% → 48.1% → ~46.5% guided, memory explaining >100% of each step, carry-in inventory benefit "decreasing over time beyond September," and market memory prices "continuing to increase" — no bounded end state offered.
  • Constraints are worsening, not resolving: from one product line to three, "significantly" larger impact in September, no December color, and the root cause (advanced-node capacity) has no near-term fix — foregone revenue is compounding and still unquantified.
  • Services growth is decelerating toward single digits: 16% → 12% → implied <10% reported in September, with structural drags (link-out ruling, business-model changes, gaming softness) layered on FX; the App Store's regulatory exposure is now a named revenue factor.
  • Price increases cut both ways: elasticity on iPad/Mac is untested ("weeks ahead"), and further memory inflation could force either more increases (demand risk) or margin absorption (earnings risk) — management's own framework offers no anchor.
  • Siri AI launches without the EU and China — two of Apple's largest markets — with China timing genuinely uncertain and the EU gated by regulators; the flagship AI feature is geographically incomplete at birth.
  • AI economics are unplanned: no complete compute-cost recovery plan, capital-intensity question unanswered, Google arrangement economics still never disclosed even as the product it presumably powers shipped.
  • Leadership risk at the inflection point: the incoming CEO gave a content-free first answer while the company faces its tightest supply quarter, a memory cost flood, and a Services deceleration simultaneously.
  • Reported optics will deteriorate: Q4 prints will show ~46.5% underlying GM and <10% Services growth against a 50.1%/12% Q3 — headline momentum breaks even if execution holds.
