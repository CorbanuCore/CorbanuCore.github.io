Quarter in one view

  • Q2 FY2026 revenue of $1.935B, +93% YoY and +19% sequentially — the highest YoY growth rate in company history, extending the streak to 12 consecutive quarters of accelerating growth (per management's Rule of 40 framing).
  • U.S. business now 81% of revenue ($1.573B), +115% YoY / +23% sequentially; U.S. commercial +149% YoY / +28% sequentially to $764M; U.S. government +90% YoY / +18% sequentially to $809M.
  • Rule of 40 score of 155 (up 10 points QoQ from 145); adjusted operating margin 62%; GAAP net income $1.062B (55% margin); adjusted FCF $1.22B (63% margin, +115% YoY) — first quarter above $1B on GAAP net income, adjusted FCF, and adjusted operating income simultaneously.
  • Largest-ever full-year guidance raise again: FY2026 revenue midpoint lifted to $8.154B (+82% YoY), an 11-point growth-rate increase over last quarter's guide; U.S. commercial FY guide raised to >$3.424B (≥+134%).
  • Record bookings: U.S. commercial TCV $2.132B (+153% YoY, +81% QoQ, ~$800M above prior best); government TCV $3.4B (+49% YoY); NDR 157% (+700 bps QoQ).

What management is focused on

  • "Sovereign AI" as the organizing frame: the dominant theme across all four executives — enterprises must own their data, logic, actions, security, and increasingly their model weights, rather than renting frontier models that absorb their IP as training data. This supersedes last quarter's "no-slop zone" language (though "slop" rhetoric persists).
  • Post-training / customer-owned weights: Sankar introduced the "benchmaking vs. benchmaxing" thesis — customer-specific benchmarks plus supervised fine-tuning/RL inside the customer security boundary, compounding alpha into weights the customer controls. Claimed vanilla Nemotron Ultra beat frontier models on 5 production tasks within 24 hours of integration.
  • NVIDIA partnership and classified fine-tuning: Karp disclosed a partnership with NVIDIA and entry into classified-space model fine-tuning, claiming Palantir-fine-tuned models on an NVIDIA stack outperform frontier models. No economics, timing, or revenue detail given.
  • FDE tradecraft as moat: a bake-off anecdote — a frontier lab's deployment team failed on a ticketing automation problem while Palantir's FDEs converted the work into a $10M ACV contract. "Only Palantir has FDEs. Everyone else has sparkling sales engineers."
  • Maven as platform, not just program: first government program of record launched *on* the Maven platform; 25,000+ builders developing on it; DoW TTM revenue still <25 bps of the Pentagon budget (framed as headroom).
  • American Tech Fellowship / labor narrative: 1,000+ ATF grads; factory-floor worker cut production planning from 30–40 days to <1 — AI-as-job-creator messaging via the first American Builder Summit.
  • Karp's 18-month ambition: driving the business to grow at or above the U.S. commercial rate (~149%) for the next 18 months — an informal stretch goal, not guidance, and well above the FY guide.

Key numbers and quarter mechanics

  • Revenue: $1.935B total (+93% YoY, +19% QoQ); U.S. $1.573B (+115%); international implied ~$362M.
  • Commercial: $945M (+110% YoY, +22% QoQ); U.S. commercial $764M (+149% YoY, +28% QoQ); international commercial $182M (+26% YoY, +2% QoQ) — still the laggard, unchanged growth rate vs. Q1.
  • Government: $990M (+79% YoY, +15% QoQ); U.S. government $809M (+90% YoY, +18% QoQ); international government $181M (+42% YoY, +5% QoQ).
  • Customers: U.S. commercial 653 (+35% YoY, +6% QoQ); total customer count not disclosed on the call. Top-20 customer TTM revenue $124M each (+67% YoY, up from $108M/+55% in Q1).
  • Deals: 220 deals ≥$1M, 98 ≥$5M, 73 ≥$10M — all records. Notable conversions: multinational tech company expanded from one operating company (started Q4 last year) to a 3-year ~$370M deal; global asset manager Q1 start → 3-year $35M deal; software/services company post-"agent camp" → $15M 5-month initial deal; nonprofit health system pilot (end-2025) → 3-year $37M deal.
  • Bookings/backlog: commercial TCV $2.337B (+118% YoY); U.S. commercial TCV $2.132B (+153% YoY, +271% dollar-weighted duration); TTM U.S. commercial TCV $5.964B (+117%); government TCV $3.4B (+49% YoY, +129% duration-weighted); total remaining deal value $13.1B (+83% YoY, +11% QoQ); RPO $4.9B (+103% YoY, +10% QoQ); NDR 157%.
  • Profitability: adj. gross margin 86% (down from 88% — attributed to assuming cloud hosting costs for one government customer; management frames it as enabling faster time-to-value and workflow expansion); adj. operating income $1.194B (62% margin); GAAP operating income $912M (47%); GAAP net income $1.062B (55%).
  • SBC: $265M disclosed this quarter (resolves Q1's inaudible figure); equity-related payroll tax $17M. GAAP EPS $0.41 = adjusted EPS $0.41; SpaceX unrealized gains contributed +$0.03 GAAP / +$0.02 adjusted EPS tailwind — i.e., underlying adjusted EPS ~$0.39.
  • Expenses: adjusted expense $741M, +14% QoQ and +37% YoY (technical hiring, AI platform); significant Q3 expense ramp flagged for seasonal new-hire starts and marketing.
  • Cash: $1.216B operating cash flow; $1.22B adj. FCF; $9.2B cash/Treasuries (up from $8.0B).
  • Guidance: Q3 revenue $2.160–2.164B (implies ~12% sequential at midpoint, a deceleration from Q2's 19%); Q3 adj. op income $1.292–1.296B (~60% margin, slight compression); FY26 revenue $8.15–8.158B; U.S. commercial >$3.424B (≥+134%); adj. op income $4.889–4.897B; adj. FCF $4.5–4.7B; GAAP profitability every quarter.
  • Strategic commercial contracts now ~$400K (0.02% of revenue), <$500K per remaining quarter — fully wound down.

Product and launch scorecard

  • AIP / sovereign AI stack: Core engine. New layers emphasized: agent SDKs, orchestration with telemetry/observability, evals and customer-specific benchmarks, AIP Evolve, and post-training (SFT + RL) — the "automated model factory" running inside the customer security boundary. Evidence: Kirkland & Ellis legal operating-model rebuild (days → minutes for analysis/drafting); the $370M portfolio-wide tech-company expansion; bake-off win converting to $10M ACV.
  • Post-training / fine-tuning (new): First quarter this is a headline product claim. Evidence is anecdotal (Nemotron Ultra beating frontier on 5 tasks, unverified; classified-space fine-tuning "already entering" per Karp). No revenue attribution, customer names, or GAAP-relevant milestones disclosed. This is the key new product bet to track.
  • Maven Smart System: First program of record launched on Maven as a platform (open data standards, ontology, dev tooling, peering); 25,000+ builders. Adoption evidence remains strong; no updated usage-growth metric this quarter (Q1 had 4x YoY).
  • NVIDIA partnership: Announced by Karp with no terms, scope, or timing — a disclosure gap given its centrality to the fine-tuning story.
  • Ship OS / Warp Speed / Apollo / USDA: Not mentioned on this call — notable absences given Q1 prominence. No evidence of setback, but no progress updates either.
  • Agent camps / sovereign boot camps: Functioning as demand-generation — $15M deal signed after a May agent camp; boot camp drew unsolicited CEO/operational-lead attendance and reactivated dormant Foundry-only customers (management ties this to NDR strength).

Sell-side read-through

  • Only three questioners again (Dan Ives, Mariana Perez Mora/BofA, Gil Luria/D.A. Davidson) — remarkably light Q&A for a 93%-growth quarter, and zero challenges to numbers, margins, or guidance.
  • Ives asked a soft question about sovereign boot camp takeaways; the useful extraction was Karp's comment that legacy Foundry-only customers are now migrating across the stack (Ontology, sovereign AI) — supporting the NDR 157% print — and that Palantir is actively seeking "technically exceedingly competent partners" to scale, with partners permitted to occasionally compete.
  • Perez Mora asked why Palantir saw the sovereignty shift early — a reflective question, not a probe. Sankar's answer introduced a substantive claim: reasoning traces, metadata, and usage exhaust are "probably more valuable than just the data," and customers now lack mechanisms to control them — the intellectual core of the sovereign AI pitch.
  • Luria's question was the most analytically pointed: model lock-in/rug-pull risk (frontier models pulled by government or vendor this year). Management confirmed a model-switching product already deployed across the U.S. government — a concrete capability disclosure, though unnamed and unquantified.
  • Not asked: international commercial stagnation (+26% for the second straight quarter), gross margin decline mechanics and durability, Q3's implied sequential deceleration and margin step-down, SBC trajectory ($265M), SpaceX gains flattering EPS, CR/appropriation risk (last quarter's biggest open risk — now fully unaddressed), and the absence of Apollo/Ship OS/USDA updates. Analysts again left the hard questions on the table.

Management credibility

  • Delivery vs. claims: 12 straight quarters of accelerating growth and expanding Rule of 40; second consecutive largest-ever FY guidance raise; Q2 revenue ($1.935B) beat the Q2 guide midpoint ($1.799B) by ~7.5%. Near-term numerical credibility is very strong.
  • Improved disclosure: SBC explicitly disclosed ($265M) after last quarter's inaudible figure; GAAP and adjusted EPS both $0.41 with the SpaceX tailwind quantified — resolves Q1's unexplained EPS inversion.
  • Specificity: CFO section remains highly granular (deal-count tiers, duration-weighted bookings, TTM bookings, gross-margin bridge for the cloud-hosting change — proactively explained, a credibility positive).
  • Weak points: (1) Karp's 18-month "grow at or above U.S. commercial" ambition is informal and far above guidance — sets an unofficial bar management may be held to; (2) Nemotron-beats-frontier and bake-off claims are single-anecdote, unverifiable, and self-serving; (3) NVIDIA partnership announced with zero detail; (4) Karp's political/cultural monologue ("token self-pleasuring," colonization framing) is escalating in rhetorical intensity — a governance/optics consideration even if shareholders currently tolerate it; (5) gross margin decline was explained but not quantified forward — unclear whether 86% is the new base.
  • Consistency: sovereign AI messaging was uniform across all four speakers — disciplined, but like last quarter it functioned more as marketing than disclosure; product-level proof points (Ship OS, Apollo) quietly dropped from the script.

What changed versus the prior quarter

  • Acceleration across the board: total growth 85% → 93%; U.S. 104% → 115%; U.S. commercial 133% → 149%; U.S. government 84% → 90%; Rule of 40 145 → 155; NDR 150% → 157%.
  • Guidance raised again: FY26 revenue midpoint $7.656B → $8.154B (growth +71% → +82%); U.S. commercial guide >$3.224B (≥120%) → >$3.424B (≥134%); adj. FCF guide $4.2–4.4B → $4.5–4.7B.
  • Narrative shift: "no-slop/governance" framing evolved into "sovereign AI / own your weights," with post-training and fine-tuning (plus an NVIDIA partnership) as the new product frontier — a meaningful expansion of the claimed addressable stack.
  • Margins mixed: adjusted operating margin 60% → 62%, but adjusted gross margin 88% → 86% (cloud hosting assumption for a government customer); Q3 guide implies ~60% operating margin — modest compression.
  • Q1 watchlist items resolved: SBC disclosed; EPS inversion explained (SpaceX gains); Q2 delivered well above guide. Unresolved: CR/appropriation sensitivity (not mentioned), Apollo shipping milestones (not mentioned), international commercial (still +26%), agent engine SDK availability (referenced as stack layer, no launch status).
  • Segment reclass noise gone: no commercial-to-government transition disclosed this quarter; growth rates appear clean.

Bull case

  • Acceleration at scale: 93% growth on a ~$8B run-rate with 62% adjusted operating margin and 63% FCF margin; Rule of 40 of 155 with 12 straight quarters of expansion — a financial profile with few precedents.
  • Bookings momentum is extraordinary: U.S. commercial TCV +153% YoY and +81% sequentially, $800M above the prior record; duration-weighted +271% — customers signing longer, larger deals; $13.1B remaining deal value and NDR 157% give multi-year visibility.
  • Land-and-expand is compounding: top-20 customer revenue +67%; multiple pilot-to-enterprise conversions within 2–3 quarters ($370M, $37M, $35M deals); dormant Foundry customers reactivating into the full stack.
  • Sovereign AI / post-training expands the TAM narrative from governance software into model customization — if the Nemotron/fine-tuning claims hold, Palantir captures value previously accruing to the labs, with NVIDIA partnership as potential distribution/compute leverage.
  • Maven's evolution into a platform-of-record for other programs (25,000 builders) deepens the defense moat structurally, not just contractually.
  • Guidance pattern (two consecutive largest-ever raises, Q2 beat by ~7.5%) suggests the Q3 sequential step-down is conservatism, not demand softness.

Bear case

  • Q3 guide implies deceleration: ~12% sequential vs. 19% in Q2, and ~60% implied operating margin vs. 62% — the first guide that embeds both slower growth and margin compression; if the beat pattern breaks, the acceleration narrative (the stock's core premise) is dented.
  • Gross margin slipped 2 points on cloud-hosting absorption for one government customer; if hosting-assumption becomes a pattern in large government deals, the 86–88% gross margin band erodes.
  • International commercial remains flat (+26% YoY, +2% QoQ — second straight quarter); the growth story is now 81% U.S.-concentrated and narrowing further.
  • Government budget risk went unmentioned: last quarter's CR concern received no update; U.S. government is ~42% of revenue growing 90% into an unresolved appropriations environment.
  • Unverifiable product claims are stacking up: Nemotron-beats-frontier, bake-off wins, classified fine-tuning, NVIDIA partnership — none with terms, third-party validation, or revenue attribution. The gap between rhetoric and disclosure is widening.
  • Karp's informal 18-month ≥149% growth ambition creates an unofficial expectation management cannot meet through guidance math (FY guide is +82%); any normalization toward the guide could be read as failure against the CEO's own framing.
  • EPS quality: SpaceX mark-to-market gains contributed $0.02–0.03; SBC of $265M (~14% of revenue) remains the GAAP/adjusted wedge.
  • Dropped topics: Apollo, Ship OS, USDA conversion — Q1's proof points vanished from the script without explanation.
