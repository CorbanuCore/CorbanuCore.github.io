Quarter in one view

  • Demand-led quarter, margin-normalizing quarter: record Q2 deliveries with sequential growth of 60% Americas / 27% APAC / 12% EMEA; largest order backlog since 2023; Model Y records in Netherlands, Australia, New Zealand. Auto gross margin ex-credits fell 19.2% → 16.3%, but CFO bridges it: Q1's ~$230M warranty true-downs and tariff relief didn't repeat, so margins were "approximately flat" controlling for those.
  • Energy whipsawed both ways: deployments rebounded 53% QoQ to 13.5 GWh (second-largest quarter ever), but gross margin collapsed 39.5% → 20.4% on a ~$240M warranty true-UP (vendor cell issues in legacy deployments), non-repeating Q1 tariff benefits (>$200M), and guided ASP compression. New long-term guide: energy normalizes at mid-to-low 20% gross margin.
  • FCF went negative as guided — CapEx more than doubled sequentially and will rise further in H2; >$25B FY guide reiterated, now with up to $30B of debt facilities secured to accelerate the buildout. CapEx growth guided for "the next two to three years."
  • Robotaxi got its first real metrics: >380,000 unsupervised miles across six cities in two states, zero notable incidents, >10% weekly miles growth, fleet running early v15 builds (40% of seven improvement tracks merged). Seven US markets total per CFO. Still no fleet count.
  • FSD disclosure stepped up: ~1.5M paid customers (from ~1.3M), 55% upfront / 45% subscription mix, and a striking ~55% of North American Q2 deliveries had FSD subscription enabled at delivery.
  • Net income flattered by a $1B mark-to-market gain on SpaceX holdings (a new disclosure — Tesla holds SpaceX equity), offset by ~$300M FX and ~$100M Bitcoin losses.

What management is focused on

  • Speed over capital efficiency: Musk explicitly instructed the team to spend "as fast as we can without it being too wasteful" — accepting lower capital efficiency for time, claiming "higher NPV outcome." Calls it "the fastest industrial scale-up since World War II in America."
  • Funding the supercycle with leverage: $30B debt capacity framed as "opportunistic" acceleration, with a "capital-efficient manner" assurance. This is new — prior quarters were cash-funded.
  • FSD as the demand engine, now with numbers: Musk's "buying FSD with a car attached" framing is backed by the 55% NA delivery attach rate — the first hard attach disclosure.
  • Robotaxi scaling discipline: multi-city expansion deliberately chosen over density in one city to prove generality of the stack and work through city-by-city TCO/regulatory requirements (Lars); miles-per-vehicle efficiency emphasized over fleet size (Ashok).
  • Optimus realism campaign: Musk repeatedly calibrates — "hardest product to scale manufacturing that we've ever made," no supply chain exists, initial S-curve "quite flat and long." Simultaneously reveals Optimus 4 (Austin, ~10M units/yr aspirational vs 1M/yr for Optimus 3, far more vertically integrated).
  • Power as the AI bottleneck: energy pitch pivoted toward data-center power smoothing (training runs can drop 70% power in 100ms; SpaceX buying Megapacks for this); US could "double energy output just with batteries" (1.2–1.3 TW capacity vs 0.5 TW average use).
  • New compute distribution concept — "megapod": AI4+x86 boxes ("Digital Optimus in a box") deployable at Superchargers, citing ~7 GW of Supercharger power. Digital Optimus (screen-driving agent) run in partnership with SpaceX as the orchestrating "manager" model.

Key numbers and quarter mechanics

  • Auto gross margin ex-credits: 16.3% vs 19.2%; bridge = non-repeat of ~$230M warranty true-downs + tariff relief; "approximately flat" ex-those. Headwinds called out: commodity prices and interest-rate subvention (recognized upfront as revenue offset; rising rates raised the cost).
  • Energy: 13.5 GWh (+53% QoQ); margin 20.4% vs 39.5%; drivers = ~$240M warranty true-up (vendor cell issues, legacy deployments), >$200M Q1 tariff benefit not repeating, industrial-storage ASP declines. New normalized guide: mid-to-low 20% gross margin — a material reset from the >39% record.
  • Services & other margin: 9.2% → 14.1%, all-time high — fleet cost management (used car, Supercharging, service, insurance) plus volume; still absorbs deliberate robotaxi infrastructure spend.
  • FSD: ~1.5M paid customers (from ~1.3M, ~+200K net adds); mix 55% upfront / 45% subscription; purchase option removed in most markets; ~55% of NA Q2 deliveries had FSD subscription enabled at delivery.
  • Robotaxi: >380,000 unsupervised miles, six cities, two states, zero notable incidents; >10% weekly miles growth; seven US markets (CFO); fleet on early v15 (40% of seven tracks merged).
  • FCF: negative, as guided; CapEx more than doubled QoQ, rising further in H2; FY guide >$25B reiterated; CapEx growth for 2–3 more years; $30B debt facilities secured.
  • Net income mechanics: +$1B SpaceX mark-to-market gain, −$300M FX, −$100M Bitcoin.
  • OpEx up sequentially: preproduction ramp costs (Semi, Optimus, Cybercab, AI), new compute depreciation, litigation charges; R&D-led OpEx growth guided through 2026 and beyond.
  • Backlog: largest since 2023 (vehicles); energy backlog "robust."

Product and launch scorecard

  • Semi: production started (Musk's opening) — the Q1 "soon" converted. Autonomy for Semi targeted "end of this year or early next year," deliberately deprioritized behind 3/Y/Cybercab for ~6 months.
  • Optimus: "will soon start production" — note this is softer than Q1's "late July, August" Fremont start assumption; the call gives no confirmation production has begun. Optimus 4 pre-announced (Austin, 10x volume aspiration, deeper vertical integration). Training strategy detailed: factory-worker observation, dedicated demonstration team, internet video, "Optimus Academy" RL flywheel. V3 reveal timing not revisited on the call.
  • Cybercab: production started (reiterated); factory rides underway in Austin; manufacturing targets aligned to projected unsupervised-miles growth; new disclosure — Cybercab needs chassis-specific driving data accumulated via wheel/pedal-retrofitted units before volume deployment; Starlink integration confirmed for Cybercab and eventually all vehicles.
  • Robotaxi: first quantitative safety disclosure (>380K miles, zero notable incidents) — a real response to three quarters of pressure, though still no fleet count, no intervention rates, no per-mile economics. v15 progress disclosed (40% of tracks merged). New Jersey state-level regulation flagged as "disheartening" (Lars) — first named regulatory friction.
  • FSD v15: robotaxi fleet already on early builds; consumer timing not revisited this call (Q1: "probably Q4" for unsupervised to customers).
  • Hardware upgrades: Musk now says it will be "financially sensible at some point" to upgrade essentially all camera-equipped cars below HW4 — broader than Q1's HW3 retrofit framing; targets a moderate AI4 upgrade (~mid-2027 production) or AI5.
  • AI5: "hopefully in volume production around the middle of next year," initially for Optimus; claimed future "best edge computing chip in the world." Note tension: Q1 said AI5 not for vehicles near-term; this call floats AI5 as an upgrade destination for older cars.
  • TerraFab: location announcement deferred to a standalone event ("day in the spotlight"); development fab in Austin has equipment orders placed (consistent with Q1); mask+logic+memory+packaging+testing under one roof reiterated. Samsung (Texas) and TSMC (Arizona) fabs cited as building AI compute for Optimus/robotaxi; Micron thanked for memory allocation.
  • Solar: "massive" silicon-to-panel buildout; US solar manufacturing to be multiplied "an order of magnitude" (CFO).
  • Lithium refinery and cathode refinery: production started (new); LFP factory in operation earlier this year (CFO).
  • Megapack 3: "will soon start production" — consistent with Q1's "later this year."

Management credibility

Bull case

  • Record quarter across revenue, deliveries, energy, and FCF with $41B+ liquidity and ~$9B 2025 CapEx self-funded — the AI buildout is financed from operations, not dilution.
  • If unsupervised FSD ships (Austin driverless by year-end, 8–10 metros), Tesla converts a software update across millions of cars into a robotaxi network with no incremental hardware cost — and management argues the "text while driving" unlock drives consumer demand without margin sacrifice.
  • Cybercab (Q2 2026) plus existing capacity gets to ~3M annualized units within ~24 months; AI5's claimed 10x+ performance-per-dollar advantage and dual US foundries de-risk the compute roadmap, with datacenter demand as an outlet for chip oversupply.
  • Energy is a second growth engine with record margins, hyperscaler/datacenter demand, Megablock shipping next year, and MP4 removing the substation bottleneck; Shanghai megafactory mitigates tariffs.
  • Optimus, if the 1M-unit line ramps, leverages existing manufacturing/AI talent; management's candor about difficulty is arguably a sign the engineering is real.

Bear case

  • The entire expansion thesis rests on an autonomy claim management declined to substantiate: no intervention rates, fleet size, or ride counts were disclosed despite a direct question. "100% confident" is not data.
  • Paid FSD penetration is only ~12% of the fleet after years of availability — evidence that consumers are not yet paying for the autonomy story at scale.
  • Margin structure is thin for a volume-max strategy: 15.4% ex-credit auto margin, declining credit revenue, >$400M/quarter tariff drag, rising R&D comp, and "substantially" higher 2026 CapEx — FCF could compress sharply if demand needs incentives.
  • Timeline risk is concentrated in 2026: Cybercab, Semi ramp, Optimus production start, AI5 ramp, Megablock, and reasoning-enabled FSD all land within ~12 months; Musk's own hedges (40x→10x, "maybe less than 24 months," design not frozen at production start) suggest slippage risk.
  • Governance: the comp/vote fight, Musk's stated desire for ~mid-20s voting control, and attacks on proxy advisors create headline and vote-outcome risk on Nov 6; a failed vote introduces leadership-uncertainty tail risk Musk himself raised ("can I just be ousted?").
  • Energy faces rising competition and tariff exposure; robotaxi economics are buried in Services & other, obscuring losses as the fleet scales.
