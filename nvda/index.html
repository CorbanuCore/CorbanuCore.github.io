Quarter in one view

  • Revenue $82B, +85% y/y, +20% q/q — third consecutive quarter of y/y acceleration, 14th straight sequential quarter, record $13.5B sequential increase. Beat the $78B ±2% guide (~$79.6B top of band) by ~$2.4B at midpoint.
  • Data Center $75B, +92% y/y, +21% q/q: compute $60B (+77% y/y), networking $15B (nearly tripled y/y, up from $11B). GB300/NVL72 called "the fastest product ramp in our company's history."
  • Q2 FY27 guide: $91B ±2% — an ~$9B sequential step, again with zero China data center compute revenue. GM guided 74.9%/75.0% ±50bps; FY27 "mid-70s" reiterated.
  • The $500B frame is now $1 trillion: Kress expressed "full confidence in $1 trillion in Blackwell and Rubin revenue we foresee from 2025 through calendar 2027" — the first hard successor number, though still undefined in composition.
  • New reporting framework: two platforms (Data Center, Edge Computing); DC split into Hyperscale $38B (~50% of DC, +12% q/q) and ACIE $37B (+31% q/q, AI cloud revenue >3x y/y). Nine quarters of recast history posted.
  • Vera CPU elevated to a headline growth driver: "nearly $20 billion in total CPU revenue this year" visibility (clarified as standalone Vera, excluded from the $1T), framed as a new $200B TAM.
  • Capital return step-change: dividend raised from $0.01 to $0.25/quarter (Kress misspoke $0.20; Jensen corrected on the call), new $80B buyback authorization on top of $39B remaining, ~50% of FCF return targeted for the year. Record $20B returned in Q1; record FCF $49B (vs. $35B in Q4).
  • China unchanged: H200 licenses approved, zero revenue, import permission unknown, zero in outlook.
  • Rubin production shipments confirmed for 2H26 starting Q3; claims now "up to 35x higher inference throughput and up to 10x greater AI factory revenue vs. Blackwell."

What management is focused on

  • "Compute is revenues, compute is profit" — Jensen's master narrative continues, now sharpened: "Demand has gone parabolic. Agentic AI has arrived. Tokens are now profitable." The closing remarks organized the entire company around five demand pillars (frontier models, hyperscalers, AI-native/sovereign/enterprise, edge/physical AI, Vera CPU).
  • Segmentation as strategy communication: the Hyperscale/ACIE split is designed to show NVIDIA is more than hyperscaler CapEx. Jensen repeatedly emphasized the "second category" (AI natives, enterprise, industrial, sovereign — "250,000 companies") as larger long-term and growing faster, and told Reitzes directly: "we should be growing faster than hyperscale CapEx."
  • Inference share offense: "We are growing share in inference very, very quickly" — driven by Anthropic onboarding ("coverage of Anthropic has been largely zero until just recently"), new frontier labs (Cursor, Perplexity, TML, Reflection), and claims that "every single frontier model company will jump on Vera Rubin from the get go."
  • Vera CPU as the new TAM story: agentic AI needs CPUs for orchestration/tool-use ("harnesses"); Vera positioned as "the world's first CPU purpose built for agentic AI" — 1.5x perf/core, 2x perf/watt, 4x rack density vs. x86; four use cases (in Vera Rubin, standalone, storage with CX9, security/confidential computing with CX9). Jensen: "we will be supply constrained throughout the entire life of Vera Rubin."
  • GPU rental pricing as demand evidence: H100 rental price +20% YTD, A100 +~15% — the "sold out with rising pricing" claim from last quarter now has quantified data attached.
  • Capital allocation reframed: after declining Arcuri's mega-buyback invitation last quarter, management pre-empted the debate with the dividend raise, $80B authorization, and a ~50%-of-FCF return commitment — while reiterating R&D and ecosystem investment remain the first priority.

Key numbers and quarter mechanics
