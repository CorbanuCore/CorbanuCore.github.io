Quarter in one view

  • Record Q1 FY27 revenue of $82B, +85% y/y and +20% q/q — third consecutive quarter of y/y acceleration, 14th straight sequential quarter; record +$13.5B sequential add. Beat the $78B ±2% guide by ~$4B.
  • Data center $75B (+92% y/y, +21% q/q): compute $60B (+77% y/y), networking $15B (~3x y/y). New segmentation introduced: Hyperscale $38B (~50% of DC, +12% q/q) vs. ACIE (AI clouds/industrial/enterprise) $37B (+31% q/q, AI cloud revenue >3x y/y).
  • Q2 FY27 guided to $91B ±2% (~+$9B sequential), again with zero China data-center compute revenue; management now expresses "full confidence in $1 trillion in Blackwell and Rubin revenue... from 2025 through calendar 2027" — a doubling of the prior $500B frame.
  • GAAP GM 74.9% / non-GAAP 75.0%; FY27 "mid-70s" reiterated. Record FCF $49B (vs. $35B in Q4); record $20B returned to shareholders; dividend raised from $0.01 to $0.25 (Jensen corrected Colette's $0.20 on the call); new $80B buyback authorization on top of $39B remaining; ~50% of FCF to be returned this year.
  • Major new disclosure: Vera standalone CPU — claimed $200B new TAM and "visibility to nearly $20 billion in total CPU revenue this year," with ambition to become "the world leading CPU supplier."

What management is focused on

  • "Demand has gone parabolic" / agentic AI as settled fact: Jensen's close — "Agentic AI has arrived... Tokens are now profitable, so model makers are in a race to produce more." The "compute equals revenues" doctrine is now the load-bearing claim for all forward visibility.
  • The $1 trillion visibility claim: Blackwell + Rubin revenue 2025 through CY2027, up from the $500B-through-CY2026 frame two quarters ago. Notably, per Jim Schneider's question, this excludes Vera standalone CPU, LPX, and Rubin CPX — management framed those as upside.
  • Vera CPU as a new pillar: positioned as "the world's first CPU purpose built for agentic AI" — 1.5x perf/core, 2x perf/watt, 4x rack density vs. x86; four use cases (in Vera Rubin, standalone, storage with CX9, security/confidential computing with CX9); ~$20B CPU revenue visibility this year; Jensen expects to be "supply constrained throughout the entire life of Vera Rubin."
  • Segmentation overhaul: two market platforms (Data Center, Edge Computing) with DC split into Hyperscale and ACIE. Jensen's rationale: the non-hyperscale "second category" (AI natives, enterprise, industrial, sovereign — "250 thousand companies") is structurally different, poorly understood, and will grow faster than hyperscale long term.
  • Inference share gains: explicit claim of "growing share in inference very, very quickly," anchored on the Anthropic partnership (coverage "largely zero until just recently") plus new frontier labs (Cursor, Perplexity, TML, Reflection). MLPerf sweep cited; GB300 throughput +2.7x and cost/token -60% vs. six months ago.
  • Capital allocation finally quantified: after two quarters of deflecting, management delivered — $20B returned in Q1, dividend to $0.25, $80B new authorization, ~50% of FCF targeted for return this year.

Key numbers and quarter mechanics

  • Revenue $82B (+85% y/y, +20% q/q); DC $75B (+92% y/y); DC compute $60B (+77% y/y); DC networking $15B (~3x y/y). InfiniBand >4x y/y on XDR deployments; Spectrum-X claimed "larger than all Ethernet network peers combined."
  • Hyperscale $38B (+12% q/q, ~50% of DC); ACIE $37B (+31% q/q); AI cloud revenue >3x y/y; sovereign +80% y/y; ~40 countries, "$50 trillion in GDP"; partner data centers >10MW nearly doubled in a year to 80+ sites.
  • Edge computing $6.4B (+10% q/q, +29% y/y); Blackwell workstations strong; consumer demand "fell modestly due to higher memory and system prices." Physical AI >$9B LTM (vs. >$6B FY26 disclosed last quarter).
  • GAAP GM 74.9% / non-GAAP 75.0%, flat sequentially; GAAP and non-GAAP opex +12% q/q (compensation, compute/infrastructure). Non-GAAP tax 16% (below guide, geographic mix).
  • FCF record $49B (from $35B in Q4). DSO 45 days on collection timing; guided back to mid-50s in Q2 — a working-capital normalization headwind to Q2 cash flow.
  • Total supply (inventory + purchase commitments + prepaids) raised to $145B — the balance-sheet backing for the $1T claim.
  • Q2 FY27 guide: revenue $91B ±2%; GM 74.9%/75.0% ±50bps; opex ~$8.5B GAAP / ~$8.3B non-GAAP; FY27 opex growth now "upper forties" % y/y (raised from "low 40s"); FY27 tax 16–18% (lowered from 17–19%, geographic mix — also clarifies last quarter's garbled "7–19%").
  • Pricing datapoints: H100 rental price +20% YTD; A100 cloud pricing +~15% — management's evidence that aging GPUs still generate "profitable revenue beyond the depreciable life."
  • Hyperscaler capex: analysts now forecasting >$1T by 2027 (per Colette); Jensen referenced "a trillion dollars this year" for hyperscale capex. $3–4T annual AI infrastructure spend by end of decade reiterated.
  • Transcript artifacts: "StacyX AI" (likely xAI), "Farweave" (likely Microsoft Fairwater), "codec"/"Codex" around GPT-5.5, "GV300" (GB300), "XGS" (Google instance type), "OpenClaw," "Hermes," dividend initially stated as $0.20 then corrected to $0.25.

Product and launch scorecard

  • Blackwell/GB300: "fastest product ramp in our company's history"; frontier model builders and hyperscalers each cumulatively deployed "hundreds of thousands" of Blackwell GPUs. GPT-5.5 "co-designed for, trained with, and served on Blackwell." Microsoft Fairwater live "ahead of schedule" with hundreds of thousands of Blackwell GPUs. AWS to add >1M Blackwell and Rubin GPUs starting this year.
  • Vera Rubin: production shipments on track for 2H CY2026, starting Q3 (fiscal Q3, i.e., calendar Q3); Colette: POs in hand, "almost all of our major customers ready to go," ramp continues into fiscal Q4 with Q1 of next year "very big as well" — but still "hard to say" whether it out-ramps GB300. Now described as 7 chips across 5 racks (vs. 6 chips last quarter — the counting inconsistency persists unreconciled). Claims: up to 35x inference throughput and up to 10x AI factory revenue vs. Blackwell. Google early adopter: bare-metal instances supporting up to 960K Rubin GPUs across sites.
  • Vera standalone CPU: the quarter's biggest new vector — $200B TAM claim, ~$20B revenue visibility this year, every major hyperscaler and system maker partnering. Jensen's agentic-CPU thesis: agent harnesses (Codex, Claude Code-style) run orchestration/tool-use on CPUs; "billions of agents" each effectively needing a PC. Economics framed as tokens-per-dollar vs. legacy dollars-per-core.
  • LPX (low-latency decode): Jensen downgraded near-term relevance — "today, it is a lot less than 20%" of the market; SRAM-based decode accelerators "will be a niche product for some time to come." This is a notable tempering of the Groq-derived low-latency thesis from last quarter.
  • Networking: $15B quarter (~3x y/y); Spectrum-X now claimed larger than all Ethernet peers combined; InfiniBand >4x y/y; AWS Spectrum collaboration confirmed.
  • Edge/physical AI: $9B+ LTM physical AI revenue; Uber robotaxi partnership (~30 cities, 4 continents by 2028); consumer/gaming soft on memory and system pricing — the supply constraint flagged last quarter is now visible in demand destruction language.

Jensen answered cleanly: $20B is standalone; CPUs run harnesses/tool-use, GPUs run thinking — incremental, not cannibalistic. Best-substantiated answer of the call.

  • Josh Buchalter (TD Cowen): Rubin ramp slope vs. GB300. Colette: starts fiscal Q3, ramps into Q4, fiscal Q1 next year "very big," but "hard to say" if faster than GB300 — consistent with prior "too early" candor, now with POs and customer readiness added.
