Quarter in one view

  • Q2 FY2026 total revenue $60.8B, +28% YoY (+27% cc) — above the guided $58–61B range midpoint and top end ($61B), even with FX flipping from the assumed ~2% tailwind to a much smaller actual one. FoA revenue $60.4B (+28%); FoA ads $59.4B (+27%, +26% cc). Growth decelerated from Q1's +33% as guided, but the guide was beaten.
  • Ad mechanics: impressions +14% (down from +19% in Q1), price per ad +12% (held at Q1's level) — the growth mix shifted toward price; impression growth now explicitly partly attributed to "lower monetizing surfaces and regions."
  • GAAP operating income $18.8B, down 8% YoY, 31% margin (vs. 41% in Q1) — hit by $2.4B in legal-proceedings charges and $1.2B in severance from the May headcount reduction. Ex-charges, OI grew 9% YoY. Net income $15.8B / $6.18 EPS; tax rate 16% (Q1's was distorted by an $8.03B benefit).
  • CapEx (incl. finance leases) $31.1B — up ~57% QoQ; FCF collapsed to $784M. Cash $90.3B vs. debt $83.7B → net cash only ~$6.6B (from ~$22.5B in Q1). FY26 CapEx narrowed to $130–145B (from $125–145B); expenses raised at the low end to $165–169B (from $162–169B) on the legal charge. Q3 guided to $61–64B with ~1% FX headwind; tax guide raised to 15–17%.
  • Layoff now quantified: ~8,000 employees (≈10% — confirming Squali's Q1 figure), $1.2B severance; headcount 75,000+, down 3% QoQ, with most of the impact exiting headcount by end of Q3.
  • Reality Labs revenue $431M, +16% YoY — first growth quarter in the supplied record, driven by AI glasses, offset by lower Quest sales. Still no units or segment split.
  • New: BlackRock strategic venture for a 1GW El Paso data center (announced yesterday); Muse Spark 1.1 + Muse Image shipped with a public API; Meta One subscription launched; business agents went global with >1M businesses using weekly.

What management is focused on

  • Expanding the monetization narrative beyond ads: Zuckerberg laid out a three-tier opportunity stack — AI accelerating the core (recommendations, ads, creative tools), personal agents as the next consumer platform, and a new enterprise business (APIs, business agents, and explicitly "potentially selling compute directly" — Zuckerberg disclosed "quite a number of offers at a meaningful premium over what we paid for the compute").
  • MSL scaling ladder: Muse Spark 1.1 (agentic/coding, efficient, computer use) and Muse Image shipped in the last month; larger, more advanced models "in training." Zuckerberg conceded Spark 1.1 sits at the efficient/low-cost end of the intelligence spectrum (prompted by Sandler's Pareto-frontier question) and framed efficiency as deliberate for billion-user serving.
  • Capacity strategy with a financing overlay: Li framed Meta as "demand constrained" today, plans geared to maximize 2026–27 capacity, and 2028+ decisions deliberately deferred (land/power now, chips later). The BlackRock JV is presented as the template for supplementing operating cash flow with external capital; Li noted an evolving capital structure with more long-duration debt.
  • Recommendations as the near-term ROI proof: Meta Generative Recommender (LLMs reasoning jointly over ads and users) deployed into ads retrieval; 8.3% ad-click increase and 15.7% conversion uplift on Facebook; largest single Reels ranking release (+15bps sessions); foundation models for combined organic+ads recommendations hit first research milestone.
  • Agents everywhere: business agents global on WhatsApp/Messenger (>1M businesses weekly), rolling out on Instagram; Meta Business Agent platform for enterprises launched; personal agents teased ("we're going to have more to share soon") with WhatsApp as the key surface and incognito mode as a privacy differentiator.
  • Open-source posture clarified: return to open-weight releases "at some point soon," but frontier models will not be ceded to reliance on others' open weights (Gawrelski question) — Zuckerberg framed model sovereignty as core to Meta's full-stack identity.

Key numbers and quarter mechanics

  • Revenue: total $60.8B (+28%, +27% cc); FoA $60.4B (+28%); FoA ads $59.4B (+27%/+26% cc); FoA Other $1.0B (+73%, first $1B quarter; WhatsApp paid messaging + subscriptions); RL $431M (+16%).
  • Ads: impressions +14%; price +12%. Impression deceleration (19% → 14%) is the key mechanical change; price held. Advantage+ end-to-end solutions now >$75B annual run rate.
  • Engagement/user metrics: 3.6B DAP; Instagram hit 2B DAU; Threads crossed 500M MAU ("fastest-growing conversation app ever"); WhatsApp peaked at 30M messages/sec during the World Cup final. Instagram time spent +double digits YoY; Facebook video time +9% globally, >10% US/Canada.
  • P&L: expenses $42B, +55% YoY (includes $2.4B legal charges, $1.2B severance; also "third-party AI token costs" — a notable new expense line); OI $18.8B (-8%, 31% margin; ex-charges +9%); tax 16%; net income $15.8B / $6.18.
  • Cash/capital: CapEx incl. finance leases $31.1B; FCF $784M; cash $90.3B vs. debt $83.7B. Debt grew ~$25B QoQ (from $58.7B) — financing the build-out is now visibly leverage-driven. No buyback mentioned for a third consecutive call.
  • Headcount: 75,000+, -3% QoQ; ~8,000 impacted by May reduction, largely rolling off by end of Q3.
  • Recommendation/ads proof points: 8.3% click / 15.7% conversion gains on Facebook from GEM + user-understanding models; +1% app-event conversions on Instagram from LLM pilots; +15bps IG sessions from largest Reels ranking release; >50% of IG Feed recommended content now <1 day old (2x YoY); all public IG Reels/Feed posts now LLM-processed; "Your Algo" / "Shape Your Feed" user-tuning features with >80% retention among engagers.
  • Business AI: >1M businesses using business agents weekly; Movida (Brazil) case study: +44% daily WhatsApp bookings, 85% of conversations fully AI-resolved. GenAI creative tools at >9M small businesses (up from >8M in Q1); image generation adoption more than doubled in the quarter.
  • Guidance: Q3'26 $61–64B (~1% FX headwind — first headwind in the supplied record); FY26 expenses $165–169B (low end +$3B on legal charge); FY26 CapEx $130–145B (low end +$5B); tax 15–17% (up from 13–16%); "operating income above 2025" reiterated.

Product and launch scorecard

  • Muse Spark 1.1 + Muse Image (MSL): shipped in the last month; Spark 1.1 framed as strong agentic/coding model "very efficient," distributed via new public API, OpenRouter (US developers), partner channels and coding agents "over the coming weeks." Zuckerberg acknowledged it is not at the frontier end ("lower cost end" per Sandler's framing, not disputed). Meta AI daily interactors +60% since the Muse Spark rebuild. Verdict: velocity improved (two model releases in a month), but frontier standing still unproven externally; enterprise adoption features still being built.
  • Muse Image: positioned across consumer content ("nearly infinite universe of personalized content") and ad creative; "great feedback so far" — no quantified adoption yet.
  • Meta One subscription: just launched; tiers/pricing to expand. Explicitly new revenue stream #1 alongside API. No subscriber or revenue data.
  • Model API: "competitive price," "encouraged by initial results" — no revenue figures; enterprise rollout and more countries pending.
  • Business agents: global rollout done on WhatsApp/Messenger, Instagram rollout underway; >1M businesses weekly; Movida case study (44% bookings lift, 85% autonomous resolution) is the strongest third-party-flavored proof point Meta has offered in this category. Monetization still evolving: "subscriptions, volume-based pricing," and outcome-based auction model aspirational.
  • Glasses: new Meta-branded line with EssilorLuxottica (including a Kylie Jenner style), first to ship Muse Spark out of the box; "early sales strong, exceeding expectations"; RL revenue +16% — first quarter glasses growth is visible in segment revenue. Still no units; more lineup detail promised at Connect (Sept 23).
  • New consumer apps: Instagram Instants, Forum (Groups), Seller (Marketplace) shipped — evidence of the "ship more apps faster" claim, but no traction metrics.
  • Threads ads: global expansion completed in Q2; WhatsApp Status ads "on track toward global rollout."
  • Personal agents: still not shipped — "we're going to ship this at some point soon… there's only so much I can say on an earnings call."

Sell-side read-through

  • Nowak again led with ROIC — this time asking which opportunity delivers "quantifiable material ROIC" in '26–27 — and again got no metrics: Zuckerberg said he's "optimistic that we're going to see meaningful growth in all of these areas." Third consecutive soft answer on returns discipline; the question is now a standing feature of the call.
  • Nowak's '27 CapEx probe: Li declined specifics but disclosed plans are geared to "maximizing 2026 and 2027 capacity," later clarifying (Gawrelski) that this is demand-driven ("demand constrained… numerous ROI-positive places we would put compute") and that '28+ flexibility means land/power now, chip decisions later. Read: '27 spend still pointed up; no downward scenarios offered.
  • Sheridan's capital-mix question got the clearest financing philosophy to date: more long-duration debt to lower cost of capital plus partnerships like BlackRock — explains the ~$25B debt step-up and signals off-balance-sheet structures will grow.
  • Anmuth pressed the apparent contradiction of selling compute while buying third-party capacity; Zuckerberg's answer (scarcity, portfolio of monetize-now vs. build-intelligence) was rhetorically smooth but did not explain the simultaneous buy/sell at the unit level — the "significant premium" offers claim is unverifiable and unquantified.
  • Sandler introduced the only externally-anchored model-quality claim of the call ("Spark 1.1 close to the Pareto frontier at the lower cost end"); Zuckerberg did not contest the "lower intelligence spectrum" placement — a quiet concession that MSL is not yet at the frontier.
  • Gawrelski tested the open-source escape hatch (skip frontier model development); Zuckerberg rejected reliance on others' open weights as "very tricky" and risky — committing Meta to the expensive path.
  • Not asked: buybacks (third straight call of silence), EU less-personalized ads, Manus (a second consecutive non-update — the deal was not mentioned at all), and the youth-trial exposure that Li herself flagged as a potential "material loss."

Management credibility
