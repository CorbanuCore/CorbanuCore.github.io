    Quarter in one view

    • FQ4 FY2026 was a blowout on every line: revenue $54.2B (+31% q/q, +379% y/y, sixth consecutive record), gross margin 87% (+210 bps q/q), non-GAAP EPS $33.42 (+33% q/q) — all above the high end of guidance. FY2026 revenue $133.2B (+256%), GM 81.1% (+40 pts y/y), EPS $75.52 (+811%).
    • FQ1 FY2027 guide: record $61.5B ±$1.5B revenue, GM ~86.25% (explicitly the FY27 floor), EPS $38.15 ±$1.00, opex ~$2.06B, tax ~15.5%. FY27 guided to sequential revenue growth every quarter with higher GM after Q1.
    • SCA book scaled again: 26 signed (from 16), ~$32B customer financial commitments (from $22B+, "vast majority" cash), RPO ~$150B, estimated >35% of revenue through 2030, with extensions into 2031. New disclosure: three-quarters of SCA revenue has a defined pricing framework, a majority of that with floor/ceiling bands; the rest is periodically market-priced.
    • Tightness language hardened further: "much tighter" supply-demand in CY27 and CY28 than CY26; no line of sight to balance; >75% of CY2027 output already committed (SCA + non-SCA POs); HBM CY2027 "vast majority" sold with significant price increases narrowing the GM gap to conventional DRAM.
    • CapEx stepped up again: FQ1 ~$11.5B, 1H FY27 ~$25B, 2H higher (implies FY27 >$50B), construction-weighted, aimed at cleanroom availability late CY2028+. Capital return increase confirmed from December 9, 2026, with intent to return 100% of excess cash over time, primarily buybacks; new authorization to be sought (current $2.2B).

    What management is focused on

    • Framing FY27 as better than FY26 with margins still rising: Q1 GM guided down 75 bps sequentially but explicitly called the FY27 floor; the ~$1B Q1 cost headwind (incentive comp absorbed into inventory, startup costs) was proactively quantified by Murphy and framed as partly "variable or temporal."
    • SCA architecture as the structural-reset proof point: Mehrotra and Murphy repeatedly tied CapEx confidence, RPO conservatism ("revenue to well exceed RPO"), and margin durability ("even at floor prices, margins meaningfully above any prior cycle peak") to the SCA book.
    • Pre-empting the peak-pricing/despec bear thesis: Mehrotra answered Arya's 2028 pricing question and Sankar's HBM-despec question with the same argument — content optimizations let customers ship more units (high-teens server unit growth CY26 and CY27), don't reduce latent memory need, and have "diminishing" effect; supply-side headwinds (HBM trade ratios, lower node-transition productivity, slow cleanroom ramps) keep 2028 tight.
    • Capacity build-out with discipline language attached: six fab/packaging projects enumerated (NY first output CY2030, ID1 mid-CY2027, ID2 late CY2028, Japan late CY2028, Tongluo mid-CY2027, Singapore HBM packaging early CY2027, Singapore NAND 2H CY2028); equipment to be racked "as appropriate."
    • Organizational restructuring: Bhatia to President/COO (end-to-end demand-to-supply, operating P&L), DeBoer to President/CTPO — framed as execution acceleration.
    • Physical AI as the next demand leg: L4+ vehicles >200GB memory and multiple TB storage; humanoids comparable; "significant driver by end of this decade"; customers sampling next-gen products.

    • Inventory goes the wrong way again in FQ2 (DIO and dollars up) right after one quarter of improvement; the end-of-FY25 "below target" commitment for DRAM is the third iteration of a drawdown promise.
    • LTA cancelability cuts both ways: the disclosure that customers *can* cancel outside firm windows introduces a theoretical air-pocket risk if AI demand expectations reset, even if current behavior is "chasing supply."
