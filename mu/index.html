Quarter in one view

  • This was a post-earnings analyst call (no prepared remarks included; headline Q3 results vs. the $33.5B/81%/$19.15 guide are not stated in the transcript — that cannot be scored from this document). The call's disclosures are concentrated on SCAs, CapEx escalation, HBM TAM, and capital return.
  • SCA program went from one signed agreement to 16 signed, including hyperscalers with HBM in scope; structure disclosed for the first time: take-or-pay, uncancellable, ~5-year terms (outside auto), annual volume commitments, quarterly price resets within a ceiling/floor band, product premiums, plus $22B+ in customer cash deposits/letters of credit (~$18B cash), refundable on a back-end-loaded schedule.
  • Management now targets ~half of company revenue under SCAs (analyst framed it as 40% now, half eventually); CFO says Q4 FCF around $30B+ (analyst framing, not corrected) and commits to capital return ramp after Dec 9 CHIPS anniversary.
  • CapEx raised again: FY2026 to ~$27B (from >$25B), ~$10B in FQ4 alone, FY2027 "above mid-40s% of revenue" with majority construction.
  • HBM TAM raised: now "easily crossing $100 billion in 2027" (previously 2028); market tightness extended to "beyond 2027" (from beyond 2026); supply-demand "no intercept" claimed even with new fabs.

What management is focused on

  • SCAs as the business-model transformation: Sadana framed demand through CY2030, customer "urgency very, very high," and SCAs as covering DRAM, HBM, and NAND across markets. Terms disclosed this time — a material shift from four consecutive stonewalls last quarter.
  • HBM allocation discipline maintained: strategic decision to hold HBM share in line with DRAM share, reiterated; demand "far in excess" of supply through 2027–2028 across HBM3E/4/4E.
  • Supply-first worldview: bit shipment growth "determined by supply, not demand"; management declines to give forward bit CAGRs because supply is the binding constraint.
  • Diversification defense: AEBU+MCBU = almost 40% of revenue; DRAM/NAND mix oscillates 75–80%/20%; no desire to tilt further into HBM.
  • Capital return escalation: buybacks as principal return lever, ramping after the Dec 9 CHIPS two-year anniversary; dividend growth continues (30% raise done).

Key numbers and quarter mechanics

  • SCAs: 16 signed; $22B+ aggregate deposits/credit commitments; ~$18B cash alone. Deposits are not prepaid revenue; refundable on a predefined, back-end-loaded schedule (bulk in second half of term); decrement is one remedy, not the only remedy; customer liability for full volume survives.
  • Data center revenue: $25B in FQ3, of which enterprise SSD = $5B (new disclosure, implies ESSD ≈ prior quarter's total NAND revenue — notable scale-up).
  • FQ4 FCF: ~$30B+ (Reitzes' framing; CFO confirmed "cash flow growth will increase in the fourth quarter" and didn't dispute the figure; last two quarters' FCF ≈ company's entire prior history, per CFO).
  • CapEx: FY2026 ~$27B (up from >$25B guided last quarter); FQ4 ~$10B; FY2027 above mid-40s% of revenue, majority construction; FY27 construction dollars "not producing bits in that time horizon"; greenfield bit contribution begins CY2028.
  • Startup costs: meaningful from FQ4 into H1 FY2027; $100–200M/quarter impact in FY2027 vs. prior run rates; prior 0.5–1+ pt GM effect now "much reduced" given larger revenue base.
  • Market cap context cited by analyst: ~$1.2T; Reitzes noted 10% of the company could be repurchased in a year at current FCF run-rate — CFO did not commit to pace.

Product and launch scorecard

  • HBM: demand above supply for all flavors (3E/4/4E) through 2028; SCAs include hyperscaler HBM; HBM TAM raised to >$100B in 2027 — the first TAM update after two quarters of withholding it, and it went up.
  • SOCAMM/LPDRAM-in-data-center: positioned as differentiation (first to market, "sole sourced for the longest time"); RAS (reliability) work ongoing; expected to grow as % of data center DRAM. Agentic AI driving CPU-server growth from multiple vendors (x86, NVIDIA, Qualcomm) — supports DDR5/LP attach thesis from prior quarters. No revenue or attach metrics given.
  • Enterprise SSD: $5B quarter inside data center; "record share after record share"; QLC leadership; Gen6 "first company" in volume; 245TB drives (new capacity figure — last quarter cited 122TB).
  • Competition (CXMT/YMTC): output "overwhelming majority" sold within China; minimal competition seen outside China. ~65,000 patents cited as IP moat.
  • No HBM4 yield update, no HBM revenue/share disclosure, no HBF update in this call.

Sell-side read-through

  • SCA mechanics (O'Malley, Barclays): finally answered — take-or-pay, uncancellable, price bands with quarterly negotiation, upfront deposits refundable back-half-weighted. This directly answers last quarter's biggest open item (downside margin floor = the price floor in the band; downside volume protection = take-or-pay). The unknown that remains: where floors sit vs. today's spot/contract prices.
  • Capital return (Reitzes, Melius): CFO commits to increased return post-Dec 9 CHIPS anniversary but refuses rate/pace — "determined based on a number of factors." The $30B FCF / 10%-of-market-cap framing went uncorrected.
  • Bit CAGRs (Sur, JPMorgan): management declined forward CAGR guidance entirely, reframing bit growth as supply-determined — reduces visibility but reinforces the tightness narrative.
  • Startup costs (Weathers, Deutsche Bank): quantified ($100–200M/qtr FY27) with explicit down-weighting of margin impact — pre-empting the "GM peak" debate.
  • CapEx (Schneider, Goldman): pressed twice for FY27 specificity; CFO bounded it (above mid-40s% of revenue; not 55–60% — "we'd owe you more specificity" if it were). Fulfillment-rate question deflected: no aggregate % given, though "extremely short" for some customers; auto/defense/medical prioritized — consistent with the 50–66% framework but not restated numerically.
  • NAND in SCAs (Rakers, Wells Fargo): NAND is in scope but DRAM is the constraint driving urgency; "DRAM far more constrained."

Management credibility

  • Major credibility event: last quarter management refused SCA terms four times; this quarter they disclosed structure, count (16), and deposit dollars ($22B+/~$18B cash) unprompted by new information — the terms are verifiable via balance-sheet deferred liabilities and customer disclosures (e.g., the referenced large customer's willingness to pay full price).
  • TAM update delivered with timing specificity (2027 vs. 2028) — consistent with the prior claim, now raised.
  • CapEx trajectory has now been raised in consecutive quarters ($20B → >$25B → ~$27B FY26; FY27 above mid-40s% of revenue) — each raise accompanied by a supply-still-short claim; the claim is internally consistent but the "disciplined" label is being stretched.
  • CFO hedges appropriately on buyback pace and FY27 CapEx ceiling; startup-cost guidance downgraded in margin terms with a stated reason (larger revenue base) — reasonable, but it also softens prior GM-impact guidance.
  • Remaining gaps: no Q3 actuals in this transcript (cannot verify beat vs. $33.5B guide); no HBM revenue/share disclosure persists; SCA price-floor levels undisclosed; no aggregate fulfillment rate.

What changed versus the prior quarter

  • SCA count: 1 → 16 signed, with hyperscalers and HBM included; structure fully disclosed for the first time (take-or-pay, price bands, deposits). Target: ~half of revenue under SCAs.
  • HBM TAM: >$100B crossed pulled forward from 2028 to 2027 — first update, upward.
  • Tightness horizon: "beyond CY2026" → "beyond 2027"; supply-demand intercept now described as not visible at all.
  • CapEx: FY26 >$25B → ~$27B with a ~$10B single quarter in FQ4; FY27 framed as above mid-40s% of revenue (a large step-up vs. the prior "construction +$10B y/y" framing).
  • Capital return: from dividend-raise-plus-modest-buyback to a committed post-Dec-9 buyback ramp against $30B/quarter FCF.
  • Startup costs: newly quantified ($100–200M/qtr FY27) with reduced claimed margin impact.
  • New data point: data center revenue $25B, ESSD $5B in FQ3.

Bull case

  • SCA economics de-risk the cycle: take-or-pay + price floors + ~$18B cash collateral across 16 agreements, targeting ~50% of revenue — the "other side of the cycle" question now has a structural answer, not just a narrative.
  • Demand visibility extended: HBM TAM >$100B in 2027, demand exceeding supply through 2028 across all HBM flavors, SCAs stretching to CY2030, no supply-demand intercept in sight.
  • Cash machine: ~$30B FCF in FQ4 alone against a stated buyback ramp; FY26–27 CapEx fully self-funded with $22B+ of customer deposits on top.
  • Data center breadth: $25B data center revenue with $5B ESSD and record share; SOCAMM/LP DRAM leadership into the agentic-AI CPU build-out.
  • Greenfield bits don't arrive until CY2028 — two-plus more years of pricing power even as Micron spends at record levels.

Bear case

  • CapEx is compounding into the peak: FY26 raised twice to ~$27B, FY27 above mid-40s% of revenue, with management conceding this is the largest construction program in history — the supply wave that eventually ends this is now substantially Micron-funded, arriving CY2028+.
  • SCA floors are untested and undisclosed: if price bands were set near today's extreme prices, floors protect; if not, take-or-pay obligates customers at prices that could sit above a future market — the deposit structure suggests customers bear that risk, but the floor levels are the whole ballgame and remain secret.
  • Deposit refundability: $18B cash is largely a liability (refundable, back-half weighted) — it is not the permanent capital the headline number implies.
  • Startup costs and HBM die-ratio inflation raise bit costs near-term (management's own words); bit growth stays supply-capped, so the model remains price-driven at extreme ASPs with consumer units already declining.
  • No Q3 results verifiable from this call; GM peak question deferred again with a softer caveat on startup-cost impact.

  • SCA progression: count toward the ~50%-of-revenue target, new deposit dollars vs. $22B+, any disclosure of price-band levels relative to current pricing, and whether deposits appear as expected on the balance sheet.

  • FY27 CapEx specificity: management owes a number given the "above mid-40s%" framing; watch construction vs. equipment split and any re-acceleration.
  • FQ4 delivery: ~$10B CapEx quarter and ~$30B FCF — both analyst-framed but uncorrected; treat as soft commitments.
  • HBM: TAM now testable (>$100B in 2027); HBM4 yield ramp vs. HBM3E, 16-Hi qualification, CY2027 pricing, and whether share/revenue disclosure ever resumes.

Quarter in one view

  • Massive Q2 FY2026 beat: revenue $23.9B (+75% q/q, +196% y/y, fourth consecutive record; +$10.2B q/q is largest sequential increase ever), gross margin 75% (+18 pts q/q, record), non-GAAP EPS $12.20 (+155% q/q, +682% y/y), record FCF $6.9B — all above the high end of guidance ($18.7B ±$400M, 68%, $8.42 ±$0.20).
  • Q3 FY2026 guide is another enormous step-up: record revenue $33.5B ±$750M, GM ~81% (+~6 pts q/q), EPS $19.15 ±$0.40, OpEx ~$1.4B, tax ~15.1%. Management notes Q3 revenue alone exceeds full-year revenue of every year through FY2024.
  • Key strategic events: first five-year Strategic Customer Agreement (SCA) signed with a "large customer"; HBM4 36GB 12-Hi in volume shipment for NVIDIA Vera Rubin; Tongluo fab acquisition from Powerchip closed ahead of schedule; new Singapore NAND fab announced.
  • FY2026 CapEx raised sharply to >$25B (from ~$20B); FY2027 construction CapEx guided up >$10B y/y, plus higher equipment spend. Board approved a 30% dividend increase to $0.15/share.
  • Balance sheet: record net cash $6.5B (cash $16.7B vs. debt $10.1B); debt cut $1.6B in the quarter, >$5B over three quarters; two credit upgrades to "solid triple-B."

What management is focused on

  • SCAs as the structural narrative: rebranding the prior "multiyear LTA" theme as "Strategic Customer Agreements" — multiyear, specific commitments, "robust provisions" for both sides. First five-year SCA signed; discussions with multiple customers across multiple markets. Zero specifics given on pricing mechanics, cancellation terms, margin floors, or capex linkage despite four separate analyst attempts.
  • 1γ execution: fastest ramp to mature yields in company history, ramping volumes faster than any prior node, majority of DRAM bit mix by mid-CY2026; framed as destined to be the highest-volume node ever. Increased EUV adoption planned at 1δ with latest-gen tools.
  • Capacity buildout acceleration: Tongluo closed early (shipments from existing fab FY2028; second cleanroom construction starting 2026); Idaho fab 1 mid-CY2027 on track, fab 2 ground prep begun; New York broke ground, ahead of plan; new Singapore NAND fab (first wafers 2028); India assembly/test now in commercial shipments; Singapore HBM packaging contributing meaningfully in CY2027.
  • AI-driven demand breadth: DRAM+NAND data center bits to exceed 50% of industry TAM for the first time in CY2026; server units low-teens growth CY2026; robotics framed as a 20-year growth vector; flagship phones with ≥12GB now ~80% of CQ4 mix (vs. <20% a year ago).
  • Allocation discipline: 50–66% key-customer demand fulfillment reiterated as unchanged; commitment to staying diversified across end markets despite data center priority.
  • FY2027 investment step-up: "meaningful" R&D increase flagged; return on capital cited at >30%, "headed towards 50%."

Key numbers and quarter mechanics

  • DRAM: record $18.8B (+207% y/y, +74% q/q), 79% of revenue; bits up mid-single digits; prices up mid-60s% q/q including favorable mix — price again did nearly all the work.
  • NAND: record $5.0B (+169% y/y, +82% q/q), 21% of revenue; bits up low-single digits; prices up high-70s% q/q including mix. Data center NAND revenue more than doubled sequentially to a record.
  • Segments (all records): CMBU $7.7B (32%, +47% q/q, GM 74% +9 pts); CDBU $5.7B (24%, GM 74% +23 pts); MCBU $7.7B (32%, +81% q/q on price despite lower bits, GM 79% +25 pts); AEBU $2.7B (11%, +57% q/q on price despite lower bits, GM 68% +23 pts; auto+industrial >$2B).
  • Operating income $16.5B; operating margin 69% (+22 pts q/q, +44 pts y/y). OpEx $1.4B (+$87M q/q, higher R&D). Taxes $2.5B at 15.1% ETR.
  • Cash flow: OCF $11.9B, CapEx $5.0B, FCF $6.9B (record, +77% vs. prior record). CFO said Q3 FCF could "roughly double sequentially" per Arcuri's framing math ($35–40B FY FCF implied by analyst).
  • Inventory $8.3B (+$62M), 123 days (−3); DRAM days still below 120.
  • Capital return: $350M buyback (CHIPS-limited); dividend +30% to $0.15; debt now $10.1B, weighted avg maturity Aug 2034.
  • CapEx: FY26 >$25B (up from ~$20B; majority of increase is cleanroom/facility, largest single factor Tongluo, then US fab construction); Q3 CapEx ~$7B; FY27 construction CapEx up >$10B y/y plus higher equipment; construction growth outpacing equipment growth in both FY26 and FY27.
  • Industry outlook: CY2026 DRAM bit shipments low-20s% (raised slightly from ~20%); NAND ~20%; both supply-constrained; tightness "beyond calendar 2026" reiterated. Micron supply to grow in line with industry.
  • Guidance mechanics: Q3 GM ~81% driven by higher price, lower cost, favorable mix; Q4 OpEx reflects 53rd week; FY27 OpEx rising on R&D; trade/geopolitical impacts excluded from guidance.

Product and launch scorecard

  • HBM4: volume shipments of 36GB 12-Hi underway for NVIDIA Vera Rubin (announced at GTC) — the CQ2 2026 ramp commitment from last quarter delivered on schedule; mature yields expected faster than HBM3E (still a claim, now testable). 16-Hi 48GB sampled (33% more capacity per stack). HBM share: management now says the 2025 target (share in line with DRAM share) was achieved — CEO had to correct himself mid-answer after misstating the year. No new HBM revenue or share metrics; no update to the $100B/2028 TAM.
  • HBM4E: development "well underway," CY2027 volume ramp, on 1γ; "HBM4D" customization options flagged for differentiation (first mention of this branding in the transcript).
  • Non-HBM vs. HBM margins: CEO confirmed non-HBM margins are currently higher than HBM margins — notable admission explaining mix management incentives and the HBM disclosure reticence.
  • LP server DRAM: sampled industry's first 256GB LP SoC-M2 (1γ-based, 2TB per CPU — quadrupling content y/y). NVIDIA "Grok 3 LPX" rack-scale architecture cited using up to 12TB DDR5/rack as proof of DDR5/LP durability alongside HBM.
  • Data center SSD: PCIe Gen6 G9 SSDs now in high-volume production (up from qualification stage last quarter); 122TB SSD "strong adoption" (16x sequential read/watt vs. capacity-matched HDD); data center SSD share up for the fourth consecutive calendar year (2025 record); data center NAND revenue >2x sequentially.
  • Mobile: strong interest in 1γ LPDDR6 samples; mass production of 10.7 Gbps 1γ LPDDR5X 16Gb. PC: LPCAM2 qualified at a major OEM; industry's first Gen5 QLC client SSD launched; LPDDR5X designed into personal AI workstations (DGX Spark-class) with high volumes shipping.
  • Auto: sampled industry's first automotive-grade 1γ LPDDR5; first G9-based UFS 4.1 automotive solution; L4 vehicles cited at >300GB DRAM vs. ~16GB today.
  • High-bandwidth flash (HBF): management acknowledged the concept, cited NAND limitations (write speed, power, retention), said it's early and still being studied — not a committed program.

Sell-side read-through

  • SCA structure (Sankar, TD Cowen; Arcuri, UBS; Muse, Cantor): three analysts probed pricing/cancellation mechanics, downside margin floors, and capex/ROIC linkage. All were refused on confidentiality grounds. Management confirmed only: multiyear, specific commitments, "robust terms," large customer, discussions across multiple markets, and R&D/roadmap collaboration as an embedded benefit (prompted by Sur). The lack of any answer on a downside margin mechanism — the key question for "the other side of the cycle" — is the biggest open item.
  • Cash deployment (Arcuri): floated $35–40B FY26 FCF and >$50B cash by end of CY2026; CFO didn't dispute, said Q3 cash flow could roughly double sequentially, reiterated balance-sheet strength plus organic investment as priorities, buybacks as dilution offset plus opportunistic repurchase. No commitment on CHIPS buyback restriction relief.
  • HBM mix and TAM (Muse): asked whether the ~40% CAGR implies ~$50B CY2026 HBM TAM and whether players shift bits to higher-margin DDR5. Management didn't update TAM, confirmed non-HBM margins exceed HBM margins, and framed it as active mix management — an implicit confirmation that HBM share gains are being traded against margin.
  • ESSD trajectory (Sur): management endorsed continued growth ("further growth in the quarter ahead," robust design wins) without confirming Sur's doubled-sequentially framing or 50%-of-NAND-mix estimate.
  • SRAM/LPU threat (O'Malley, Barclays): management positioned LPU architectures (Grok LPX, Vera Rubin ecosystem) as complementary — 12TB DDR5 per rack — and AI deployment penetration as still very low. No acknowledgment of any cannibalization risk.
  • HBM share (Arya, BofA): asked directly about 20–25% share "right off the bat" for Vera Rubin; management declined share guidance, cited achieved 2025 parity target, and reiterated no quarterly share disclosure. The CEO's verbal slip (saying 2026 then correcting to 2025) added noise but the substance is unchanged retrenchment.
  • Margin durability (Arya): pressed on historical margin peaks (low-60s) vs. 81% guide. CFO's answer: AI as secular driver, supply constraints are durable (greenfield-only new capacity, HBM trade ratio, declining bits-per-wafer), "reversion to historical mean should be revisited" — a full-throated structural-margin claim that is the cycle's central debate.
  • Demand fulfillment (Moore, Morgan Stanley): 50–66% key-customer fulfillment confirmed unchanged; consumer demand destruction acknowledged only as "some" impact.

Management credibility

  • Delivery on guidance: Q2 beat the high end of what was already the largest sequential guide in company history ($18.7B/68%/$8.42 → $23.9B/75%/$12.20). Beat-and-raise cadence now extends to an even larger Q3 guide — verifiable next quarter.
  • Delivery on open items from last quarter: HBM4 ramp on schedule (volume shipments for Vera Rubin); first SCA signed (only one, vs. "several key customers" in discussion last quarter — progress but slower breadth than teased); Gen6 SSD moved from qualification to HVM; Tongluo closed ahead of schedule.
  • New disclosure: non-HBM > HBM margin admission is candid and economically important, though it partially explains (rather than excuses) continued HBM revenue/share opacity. HBM TAM not updated — neither raised nor lowered.
  • Weaker spots: (1) SCA questions were stonewalled four times on every material term — price reset cadence, cancellation, margin floors, capex linkage all undisclosed; (2) CEO's on-air slip on the HBM share year required correction; (3) HBM revenue dollars still withheld; (4) guidance still excludes tariff/geopolitical impacts; (5) the 50–66% fulfillment figure is now three-plus months old with no improvement — consistent messaging, but also confirmation supply response is slow.
  • Consistency: "tight beyond CY2026," "specific commitments," "disciplined" capital framing, and in-line-with-industry supply growth all repeated coherently by CEO and CFO.

What changed versus the prior quarter

  • Scale of the beat/guide: Q2 came in ~$5.2B above the high end; Q3 guide (+40% q/q at the midpoint, 81% GM) dwarfs last quarter's step-up. The earnings model has been reset again within 90 days.
  • CapEx: from ~$20B to >$25B FY26, with FY27 construction spend guided up >$10B y/y — the prior "disciplined under-build" posture has shifted to aggressive expansion, anchored by the Tongluo acquisition (new fab asset, closed early) and a brand-new Singapore NAND fab.
  • Contracting: from "full CY2026 HBM contracted + LTAs in discussion" to one signed five-year SCA — real but narrow progress; breadth and terms still pending.
  • HBM4: from "production ramp CQ2 2026" to volume shipments underway for Vera Rubin; 16-Hi sampled; HBM4E timeline set (CY2027 ramp).
  • Industry view: CY2026 DRAM bit growth raised slightly to low-20s%; Micron supply stance now explicitly "in line with industry" (~20%), consistent with last quarter's reversal from below-industry growth.
  • Consumer outlook hardened: management now explicitly forecasts PC and smartphone units declining low double digits in CY2026 due to supply constraints/pricing — last quarter this was only an elasticity concession under questioning.
  • Capital return: dividend +30% added to the buyback; two credit upgrades; net cash jumped from ~$0.25B to $6.5B in one quarter.
  • New framing: "SCA" terminology replaces LTA; memory as a "defining strategic asset"; structural-margin argument now made explicitly against the historical ~60% peak.

Bull case

  • Pricing momentum is accelerating, not decelerating: DRAM ASPs +mid-60s% and NAND +high-70s% q/q, with Q3 guide embedding further price/cost/mix gains to an 81% GM — and management asserts tightness persists beyond CY2026 with structural supply limits (greenfield-only capacity, HBM trade ratio, bits-per-wafer decline).
  • Cash generation is compounding: record FCF $6.9B, Q3 guided to roughly double, net cash $6.5B, dividend +30%, ROIC >30% heading toward 50% — funding a >$25B CapEx year entirely internally with room for buybacks.
  • First five-year SCA signed proves the concept; multiple customers/markets in discussion could convert spot-driven earnings into multiyear contracted revenue — a potential re-rating event if terms are favorable.
  • HBM4 in volume for Vera Rubin with claimed faster yield ramp than HBM3E, 16-Hi sampled, HBM4E on 1γ for CY2027 — execution evidence now exists, not just claims.
  • Non-HBM portfolio strength is the margin engine: management confirmed non-HBM margins exceed HBM, and MCBU/CDBU GMs (79%/74%) show the whole portfolio repricing.
  • Capacity roadmap is now tangible: Tongluo closed early (FY2028 shipments), Idaho mid-2027, New York ahead of plan, Singapore NAND fab 2028 — supply arriving into a market management says stays short.

Bear case

  • Pricing-dependent model at extreme levels: bits were only mid/low-single-digit growth while ASPs rose 65–80%; any contract price normalization hits revenue nearly dollar-for-dollar at a 75–81% GM. The entire bull case rests on the structural-margin claim surviving the next capacity wave — including Micron's own >$25B/year build.
  • Consumer destruction is now explicit: management forecasts low-double-digit PC and smartphone unit declines in CY2026; MCBU (32% of revenue) grew only via price as bits fell — elasticity is already biting the non-AI book.
  • HBM economics conceded inferior: non-HBM margins exceed HBM; Micron is rationing HBM share (no share target, achieved parity in 2025 and holding) — if competitors price HBM4 aggressively for CY2027 while Micron manages mix, share could erode with no disclosure mechanism to track it.
  • SCA risk: one signed agreement, zero disclosed terms, no confirmation of margin floors or capex co-funding. If SCAs lock volume/price through a downturn, they could cap upside; if they lack teeth, the structural-visibility narrative fails. Unknowable today.
  • CapEx escalation: >$25B FY26, FY27 construction +$10B y/y, rising OpEx/R&D in FY27 — fixed-cost base expanding massively just as prices are at unsustainable levels; returns on Tongluo/New York depend on demand holding into 2028–2030.
  • No Q4 GM guide and CFO's caveat that incremental price has less GM effect at these levels — the GM peak may be near even in the bull path.
  • Tariff/geopolitical impacts excluded from guidance; inventory ticked up ($8.3B) though days fell.
