    Nov 12, 2026+7.08%Q3 FY2026
    Read transcript briefing

    Quarter in one view

    • Q3 FY2026 core revenue $231M, +10% QoQ / +70% YoY, beating the $215M midpoint by $16M (7%) — third consecutive beat on all guided metrics. Cloud/services $145M (+272% YoY, +13% QoQ); hardware $86M.
    • Core GM 41% (+40bp QoQ, +200bp vs. guide) — management now says the trough was Q2, not Q3 as guided last quarter. Cloud GM jumped to 46.9% (+510bp QoQ); hardware GM fell to 31% on final low-priced legacy shipments.
    • Core operating margin -14.3% (vs. ~-24% guided midpoint, ~10pts better); core net income turned positive at $30M for the first time; adjusted core EBITDA positive YTD ($2M).
    • Q4 guidance raised across the board: revenue $310–330M (~40% QoQ, ~85% YoY), GM 47–49% (+700bp step function), OM -1% to +1% (breakeven). FY26 raised: revenue $940–960M (from $880–890M), GM 42–44%, OM -10% to -8%.
    • Two new hyperscaler wins: Oracle (hardware into OCI data centers, inference service launching summer 2027) and Microsoft (announced today; details and revenue start H1 2027). Three hyperscalers in six months.
    • OpenAI contract detail disclosed for the first time: take-or-pay for 750MW, with delay credits already deducted from RPO; <5% of committed MW at cancellation risk this year; OpenAI holds an option for +1.25GW from 2029. Cerebras admits it is behind schedule on initial capacity tranches; expects to be back on schedule by H2 2027.
    • Capacity now ~700 gross MW contracted through end-2027 (up from >600MW), with >75% of 2027 capacity coming online outside the U.S. (Canada, France, Norway, Finland; São Paulo in final contracting).

    What management is focused on

    • Reframing the OpenAI narrative proactively: Feldman led prepared remarks with OpenAI — confirming growth QoQ, admitting Cerebras lacked capacity for a broad "UltraFast" rollout (including "Astra"), denying rumors of technical inability to support Astra-scale models, and normalizing OpenAI's multi-vendor compute strategy.
    • A long, unusual defense of manufacturing yields: detailed explanation of fab vs. parametric yield, redundancy across ~1M tiles, edge exclusion, and per-section voltage tuning across 84 wafer sections. Explicitly rebutting "persistent falsehoods" that low hardware GM reflects yield problems — attributing it instead to ramp costs and legacy special pricing.
    • Positioning for any industry outcome: "no matter how AI grows, Cerebras grows" — foundation models, open models, agents, hardware or cloud. Kimi K3.1 (2.8T params) day-zero support at up to 2,000 tokens/sec is the new flagship capability proof point.
    • Disaggregation is now production, not lab: AMD prefill + CS3 decode live in the Cerebras cloud at GA, with the software platform built to work with other GPUs; framed as up to 5x more tokens per MW.
    • Capital efficiency as identity: "We are not a neocloud" — BOM-cost hardware, no HBM, no third-party 70% GM, OpenAI working-capital loan and pass-through fit-out payments funding most 2026 capex/working capital; only debt on the balance sheet is the OpenAI loan.
    • Hardware margin recovery story: legacy low-priced shipments are done; new orders at "significantly higher current prices" ship from Q4, with hardware GM expected to improve "markedly" and eventually exceed 60%.

    Key numbers and quarter mechanics

    • Revenue $231M vs. $214–216M guided — beat driven by cloud ($145M, +13% QoQ) and hardware ($86M). Q4 guide of $310–330M implies ~40% sequential growth, driven by hardware shipments (including Oracle) starting in Q4 and continuing through at least H1 2027.
    • Cloud guided flattish sequentially in Q4 — explicitly not pricing or demand ("demand outpaces supply by a wide margin") but data center capacity, which begins ramping in December, too late for Q4. Bulk of the 700MW delivers from mid-2027.
    • GM mechanics: Q3 41% total; cloud 46.9% (+510bp QoQ on higher-priced new business, partially offset by rent-back); hardware 31% (down QoQ on final legacy-agreement shipments). Rent-back drag now expected to persist through at least mid-2027 — longer than previously implied.
    • Q4 GM guide 47–49% (+700bp step function); >50% "in the near term"; 60% long-term target reiterated. Hardware mix guided toward ~50% of revenue in Q4/Q1 (vs. ~37% in Q3).
    • Profitability: core operating loss ~$33M (flat QoQ) but margin improved 170bp to -14.3%; core net income +$30M; Q4 guided to breakeven OM; "significant progress" toward 40% long-term OM target during 2027.
    • RPO: increased in Q3 but no dollar figure given in the transcript; adjusted down for estimated OpenAI delay credits; excludes Oracle (signed post-quarter, described as "large") and Microsoft. OpenAI is "the majority" of RPO.
    • Cash $7.8B (down from $8.6B in Q2) plus undrawn $850M revolver. $1B OpenAI working-capital loan (January) plus pass-through fit-out payments funded the majority of 2026 working capital and capex.
    • Pipeline claimed to have grown >10x in the last few months across hyperscalers, neoclouds, sovereigns, enterprises, AI natives. Lockup fully released November 9.

    Product and launch scorecard

    • CS4: now shipping ("began shipping our CS4"), full production "soon," 2x CS3 performance/throughput. CS3→CS4 crossover in deployments expected in 2027 ("T127" per transcript, likely H1/H2 2027 — wording unclear).
    • CS5/WSE5: ahead of schedule — taped out June 15, back from fab, brought up, running models, in-chassis testing within five months of tape-out. >2x CS4 speed; production early summer 2027 (previously "H2 2027" — pulled in). TSMC 5nm with claimed lower wafer competition/cost.
    • Disaggregation: graduated from labs to production GA — AMD GPU prefill + CS3 decode serving live cloud customers; platform works with other GPUs; to be deployed across Cerebras Inference Cloud and select data center customers. Claimed up to 5x tokens per data center.
    • OpenAI model support: "ultra-fast SALT56 preview" (transcript rendering; consistent with the GPT-5.6 Sol claim) described as overwhelmingly successful, but capacity constrained the broad rollout including "Astra." Feldman asserts CS3/CS4/CS5 can support Astra-scale and larger models — a direct denial of technical-limitation rumors.
    • Kimi K3.1 (Moonshot, 2.8T params): day-zero launch partner, claimed fastest in industry at up to 2,000 tokens/sec — the concrete benchmark-style claim this quarter.
    • Oracle: hardware purchase deployed in Oracle's U.S. data centers; OCI inference service launching summer 2027; pipeline-building underway; framed as "the start of a large partnership."
    • Microsoft: signed today; no structure, size, or economics disclosed; details and revenue start H1 2027.
    • Neocloud channel: off-takers named (Firework); neoclouds to begin distributing Cerebras-powered inference "soon." Consumer reach via "built" neighborhood OS/personal assistant announced this week.

    Sell-side read-through

    • O'Malley (Barclays) went straight for deal size on Oracle/Microsoft and RPO content. Got: no Oracle revenue in Q3 (signed post-quarter), Microsoft details deferred to H1 2027, and a useful RPO definition — committed take-or-pay revenue only, no options/extensions, net of delay credits, including pass-through setup and recurring data center costs.
    • Moore (Morgan Stanley) pressed the delay-credit risk: Komin called the RPO deduction "the absolute worst case," said they expect to do better, and pinned the cause as exclusively land/power/shell — "we have capacity, we have all materials." Notably, penalties are framed as commonplace and discretionary ("you make a business decision").
    • Malik (Citi) asked the cannibalization question differently: could CS4/CS5 throughput eventually eliminate the need for GPUs in disaggregation? Feldman didn't take the bait — emphasized disaggregation is now GA and produces more tokens when capacity is constrained. His follow-up extracted the Q4/Q1 hardware mix (~50%) and a promise of fuller 2027 mix disclosure on the Q4 call.
    • Bolton (Needham) confirmed no cancellations received to date (only credit assumptions) and got the most concrete competitive framing of the call: Cerebras claims cost-per-token crossover vs. low-batch GPUs at ~250–300 tokens/sec, "vastly more economical" at 500–1,000, and GPUs "can't play" between 800–2,000 tokens/sec. Management-asserted, no configurations disclosed.
    • Beckhammer (TD Cowen) tested the obvious hedge — could OpenAI convert cloud commitments to hardware purchases? Answer: the right exists in the contract, but OpenAI's own data centers are also behind schedule, so it doesn't solve near-term capacity. Also confirmed "everybody's data centers are late... across the world."
    • Notably unchallenged: the triple-2027 claim, the 700MW deliverability, the yield defense, the 5x disaggregation claim, and the flat-Q4-cloud explanation. No analyst asked about the cash decline ($8.6B→$7.8B) or the extended rent-back timeline (now mid-2027).

    Management credibility

    • Third consecutive beat-and-raise as a public company, plus first positive core net income delivered "ahead of schedule" — the under-promise/over-deliver pattern is now established across three quarters.
    • The margin trough moved again — but this time backward, in Cerebras' favor: last quarter guided Q3 (38–40%) as the trough; actual Q3 came in at 41% and management now says the trough was Q2 (40.6%). Directionally good, but the trough call has now been revised twice in two quarters.
    • New candor on OpenAI: first disclosure of contract structure (take-or-pay 750MW, delay credits, 90-day cancellation option, <5% of MW at risk this year, +1.25GW option from 2029) and open admission of being behind schedule on initial tranches. This is more specificity than any prior quarter — delivered unprompted, suggesting the company is getting ahead of a known investor concern.
    • The yield defense is a response to external criticism, delivered with unusual technical depth (tile redundancy, edge exclusion, 84-section voltage tuning). It is assertive but unverifiable from the call; the claim that low hardware GM reflects legacy pricing rather than yields is testable — hardware GM must recover "markedly" from 31% starting in Q4.
    • CS5 pulled in from "H2 2027" (last quarter) to "early summer 2027 production," with tape-out-to-system-test in under five months as evidence — a concrete, checkable execution claim.
    • Still evasive on: Microsoft deal structure/size, Oracle deal size, RPO dollar figure (not stated in the call), customer concentration percentages, and benchmark configurations behind the tokens/sec economics claims.
    • Rent-back language quietly extended: previously framed as a near-term drag resolving as owned capacity arrives; now explicitly "through at least the middle of 2027."

    What changed versus the prior quarter

    • Guidance: FY26 raised again (revenue $940–960M from $880–890M; GM 42–44% from 41–43%; OM -10%/-8% from -19%/-17%). Q4 guided to ~40% sequential growth and breakeven OM — the back-end-loaded ramp flagged last quarter is now explicit and large.
    • Margin narrative inverted: Q3 was supposed to be the trough (38–40%); instead Q3 printed 41% and Q2 is retroactively the trough. Cloud GM recovered sharply (41.8%→46.9%); hardware GM dropped to 31% (from 38.8%) on legacy pricing burn-off.
    • Hyperscaler count went from one (AWS) to three: Oracle (hardware into OCI, summer 2027 service) and Microsoft (details H1 2027) are new. AWS was not mentioned in the transcript beyond the "three hyperscalers" framing — Q1 2027 Bedrock GA timing not reconfirmed.
    • OpenAI moved from vague reassurance to structured disclosure: contract mechanics, delay credits deducted from RPO, cancellation thresholds, and an admitted schedule slip with H2 2027 recovery. Last quarter Feldman said OpenAI grew and would remain meaningful; this quarter he conceded capacity shortfalls limited the UltraFast/Astra rollout.
    • Capacity: ~700 gross MW contracted (from >600MW); geographic pivot disclosed — >75% of 2027 capacity outside the U.S., citing U.S. policy risk; new sites Canada, France, Norway, Finland, São Paulo (final stages).
    • Disaggregation graduated from "in labs, deployable Q4" to production GA with AMD in the Cerebras cloud.
    • CS5 accelerated (H2 2027 → early summer 2027 production); CS4 moved from "unveiling next week" to shipping.
    • Cash declined $8.6B → $7.8B; lockup fully released November 9.
    • New competitive framing: explicit cost-per-token crossover claims vs. low-batch GPUs (250–300 tok/s parity, 800–2,000 tok/s uncontested) — a direct response to the low-batch GPU threat narrative that emerged this quarter.

    Bull case

    • Three hyperscalers in six months (AWS, Oracle, Microsoft) with Oracle already a hardware purchase shipping from Q4 and Microsoft revenue starting H1 2027 — the diversification story is converting from narrative to signed deals, and none of Oracle/Microsoft is in the reported RPO yet.
    • The Q4 guide implies the ramp is arriving: ~40% sequential growth, +700bp GM step function to 48%, breakeven OM — all driven by hardware shipments at "significantly higher" current pricing, with shipments continuing through at least H1 2027.
    • OpenAI risk is now bounded and disclosed: worst-case delay credits already deducted from RPO, <5% of MW at cancellation risk this year, take-or-pay structure, and a +1.25GW option from 2029 as embedded upside. OpenAI still grew QoQ despite the capacity constraints.
    • The capacity constraint is land/power/shell, not silicon: TSMC wafer commitments secured, no HBM exposure (a claimed multi-year cost/supply advantage as memory shortages persist through 2028), manufacturing >10x by year-end, and disaggregation live at GA multiplying tokens per MW up to 5x.
    • CS5 ahead of schedule with tape-out-to-system in five months; if early-summer 2027 production holds, the annual-doubling roadmap has its first verified cadence proof point.
    • Capital efficiency is structurally differentiated: BOM-cost hardware, OpenAI-funded working capital and fit-out, minimal debt, $7.8B cash — the build-out is funded without neocloud-style leverage.

    Bear case

    • Cerebras is behind schedule on the OpenAI contract — the anchor relationship that is "the majority" of RPO. Delay credits are real, cancellation rights exist from 90 days of delay, and recovery to schedule isn't expected until H2 2027. The "worst case" RPO deduction is management's own estimate.
    • Cloud growth stalls just as the story peaks: flat sequential cloud revenue guided for Q4, rent-back drag now extended through at least mid-2027, and the 2027 triple depends on 700MW of data centers — the one input management admits is slipping industry-wide and where >75% of 2027 capacity is international, adding execution/geopolitical variables.
    • Hardware GM of 31% is being explained away as legacy pricing, not yields — but that explanation requires Q4 hardware GM to inflect sharply as promised. If it doesn't, the yield "falsehoods" management spent several minutes rebutting regain credibility.
    • Microsoft is a press release, not a deal: no size, structure, MW, or economics until H1 2027. Oracle has no disclosed size and no revenue until summer 2027. Both carry 2027–28 expectations with zero current disclosure.
    • Cash fell ~$800M sequentially despite the OpenAI loan and pass-through funding — the burn rate of the build-out is becoming visible, and the transcript offers no capex bridge.
    • Concentration hasn't actually diversified yet: OpenAI remains the majority of RPO, the Q4 ramp leans on hardware shipments dependent on customers' (late) data centers, and the OpenAI hardware-election right exists but is blocked by OpenAI's own delayed facilities.
    • Competitive claims (cost crossover at 250–300 tok/s, GPUs "can't play" above 800) are management-asserted with no disclosed configurations, against a backdrop of low-batch GPU, megakernel, and custom-silicon announcements management felt the need to address.
