Quarter in one view

  • Revenue $90.0B (+18%, +17% cc); operating income +18%; EPS $4.74, +23% adjusted for OpenAI investment impact. Adjusted for discrete items ($3.2B Anthropic investment gain, lower-than-guided Voluntary Retirement Program expense, offset by severance and Xbox impairments — net +$0.27 to EPS), results beat on revenue, operating income, and EPS.
  • Azure +43% (+43% cc implied; guide was 39–40% cc) — another beat, driven by fleet efficiency gains and faster capacity delivery, plus GitHub Copilot consumption revenue after the June usage-based pricing change. Q1 FY27 guided ~45% cc with explicit "H1 growth to accelerate."
  • FY26 full year: revenue >$331B (+18%), Microsoft Cloud >$214B (+27%), Azure >$100B (+41%), operating income >$155B (+21%).
  • Microsoft Cloud $59.3B (+27%); Cloud gross margin 65% vs. ~64% guided (beat); guided "relatively stable QoQ" in Q1.
  • Bookings: +10%/+11% cc including OpenAI, +18% ex-OpenAI (flipped back from Q3's +7% ex-OpenAI/-4% headline). RPO $678B (+84%); 30% recognizable in 12 months (+37%); beyond-12-months +112%. New claim: all sequential commercial RPO growth came from non-frontier-model customers.
  • CapEx $41.0B (as guided ">$40B"); finance leases $5.6B; cash PP&E $35.8B; CFO $55.4B (+30%); FCF $19.6B; $10.2B returned ($43B+ for the year).
  • M365 Copilot: >30M paid seats (from 20M), net seat adds more than doubled QoQ; E7 launched and "millions of seats" sold in two months, EY 400K employees the largest win.
  • Accounting change: data center/building useful life extended 15→25 years effective FY27, shifting future DC leases from finance to operating leases — restates CY26 CapEx optics to ~$175B (from ~$190B) with "minimal" FY27 operating-income benefit. Q1 FY27 CapEx guided >$50B.

What management is focused on

  • The "harness separated from model" architecture is now the core strategic claim: models are swappable inputs; the enterprise keeps its own context, memory, and evals ("every firm having a frontier," firms must not "outsource their core IP"). Nadella positioned this as both the Copilot/GitHub/Security product design and the Foundry pitch — and cited the Hugging Face incident as evidence single-model dependence is a resilience risk.
  • Model diversification with quantified efficiency: 11,000+ model catalog; 5x increase in multi-provider customers since January; MAI family expanded to a dozen+ models including first reasoning model (MAI-Thinking-1); MAI-Code-1-Flash at 10% lower median tokens on GitHub and GPT-5.6-comparable quality in Excel at lower cost; 89% GPU cost reduction in Dynamics 365 (MAI-Voice-2-Flash), up to 84% in PowerPoint (MAI-Image-2.5); MAI-Cyber-1-Flash matching "Mythos"-class performance at half cost.
  • Efficiency as the Azure answer: Copilot workload throughput +4x since start of year; dock-to-live GPU times down nearly 50% over the fiscal year (from ~20% YTD last quarter); 88 data centers added this year (31 this quarter); another ~1 GW; still on track to double capacity in two years. Hood stressed efficiency gains are "quickly monetized in quarter" because demand exceeds supply.
  • Business-model transition is now executing, not just announced: usage-based billing added to Cowork (thousands of paying customers already); GitHub Copilot consumption revenue "significant," Copilot revenue accelerating >60% QoQ; Dynamics customer-service credit consumption +4x QoQ (from ~2x last quarter).
  • AI sovereignty: Mistral partnership on Microsoft Sovereign Cloud (public, customer-controlled, fully disconnected).
  • Microsoft Frontier Co.: a new 6,000-person "outcome-driven engineering" organization embedding FDEs with customers; 330+ projects across 164 customers in a year of testing (Novo Nordisk, LSEG cited).
  • Xbox "reset": portfolio/content/operations decisions made; growth promised in FY27, but impairments hit opex again this quarter.

Key numbers and quarter mechanics

  • Company gross margin 67% (down YoY; Azure mix + AI investment/usage, partly offset by efficiency); operating margin 45%, up slightly; opex +10% (R&D compute, talent, data); headcount down 2% YoY — the decline continues.
  • Other income ex-OpenAI +$2.8B (Anthropic gain), vs. ~-$100M guided. OpenAI investment impact exists but was not quantified on the call — check the filing.
  • Segments: P&BP $37.8B (+14%; op margin 58%); Intelligent Cloud $39.3B (+32%, +31% cc; op margin 41%, flat YoY); MPC $12.9B (-4%, -5% cc; op margin 21%, down).
  • P&BP: M365 Commercial Cloud +16% cc adjusted / +14% reported (2 pts of in-period recognition normalization as guided); M365 Commercial products +19%, well ahead (large long-duration contracts with higher in-period recognition from the Windows Commercial on-prem component); M365 Consumer Cloud +24%/+22% cc; LinkedIn +12%/+10% cc; Dynamics 365 +13%/+12% cc (ERP bookings healthy, CRM still moderating with longer sales cycles). Commercial seats +6%.
  • Intelligent Cloud: Azure +43%; on-prem roughly flat (-1% cc), ahead of guide on renewal-driven in-period recognition.
  • MPC: Windows OEM -5% vs. high-teens-decline guide (another big beat, again on OEM/channel inventory builds against rising component prices); Search ex-TAC +10%/+9% cc; Xbox content/services -10% (guide was low-teens decline; impairment charges hit again).
  • Cloud GM 65% beat ~64% guide; Cloud GM pressure attributed to Azure mix, AI investment, usage, and growing GitHub Copilot usage (though GitHub margins "improved through the quarter" post-pricing change).
  • Useful-life change (15→25 years): minimal FY27 op-income benefit, but materially reclassifies future DC leases from finance to operating — mechanically lowers reported CapEx and raises operating lease payments (already visible: CFO note cited higher operating lease payments this quarter).
  • Q1 FY27 guide: revenue $89.85–90.95B (+16–17%); P&BP $36.7–37.0B (+11–12%); M365 Commercial Cloud ~16% cc adjusted / 15% reported; Intelligent Cloud $40.95–41.25B (+33–34%); Azure ~45% cc; MPC $12.2–12.7B with Windows OEM/Devices down low-20s; Xbox content/services down mid-single digits; Search ex-TAC mid-single digits (third-party partnership impact); COGS $29.6–29.8B (+23–24%); opex $16.8–16.9B (+7–8%); operating margin "relatively flat YoY"; other income ~-$100M ex-OpenAI; tax ~20%; CapEx >$50B including lease-reclassification effects.
  • FY27 framing: double-digit revenue and operating income growth; opex mid-to-high single digits; operating margin down less than 1 point (a step back from FY26's "up ~1 point"); FCF positive; CapEx grows YoY; Windows OEM/Devices down high-teens for the year; M365 Commercial products and Server both down mid-single digits for the year (launch-timing comps).

Product and launch scorecard

  • M365 Copilot — >30M paid seats (20M → 30M, net adds >2x QoQ); 50K+-seat customers +7x YoY; majority-of-knowledge-worker enterprise deployments +75% QoQ; time-to-high-usage (80% MAU) from months to days; conversations/user ~2x YoY; satisfaction scores doubled over three quarters; latency -25% in quarter. Named wins: NHS England 505K (largest healthcare deployment; 43 min/day saved in trial), KPMG 276K+, HSBC 200K, and ten more at 60K+. Still no direct Copilot revenue figure.
  • E7 — first real proof points: launched ~2 months ago; "hundreds of enterprise customers" bought "millions of seats"; EY 400K employees is the largest win. Hood says the Agent 365 component (SecOps/FinOps/token-spend observability) is the differentiator. No pricing detail disclosed.
  • Cowork GA + usage-based billing (added this month, "thousands" already paying); Autopilots (long-running autonomous agents, "OpenClaw"-powered personal agent) introduced; "super app" unifying chat/Cowork/Autopilot/Code across consumer and commercial announced but not launched.
  • GitHub Copilot — usage-based pricing now live with claimed immediate results: "significant consumption revenue," Copilot revenue +60% QoQ acceleration, continued business/enterprise seat growth. GitHub Copilot now cited as 50M users (a metric change from prior subscriber counts — see credibility); GitHub overall 225M users; 1 in 3 pull requests involves an agent.
  • Foundry — 100,000 customers; revenue more than doubled YoY; 1T-token-run-rate customers +4x YoY (from +30% QoQ language last quarter); 17,000+ use Foundry + Fabric (+60%). Telefónica named.
  • Fabric — 40,000+ paid customers (+60%; from 35K); new Rayfin agent-first SDK (2,500+ customers already; powers Replit-built app backends).
  • Data/AI infrastructure: PostgreSQL revenue +55% (third straight quarter of acceleration); PostgreSQL+Foundry customers +80%; HorizonDB (managed PostgreSQL, 3x throughput) launched; Cosmos DB called out.
  • Agent 365 — nearly 40M agents registered across tens of thousands of companies, two months in (from "tens of millions" last quarter).
  • Security — Project Perception launched (private preview): multimodel red/blue/green-team agent system, planned as consumption-based. Purview audited 50B+ Copilot interactions (+360% YoY).
  • Web IQ — new; supplies real-world web intelligence, already used by "most popular AI assistants including ChatGPT."
  • Healthcare: on pace for 100M+ automated patient encounters in CY26 (28M this quarter, 2x YoY); Mass General Brigham Dragon Copilot 4,000+ providers (21% burnout reduction study).
  • Microsoft Discovery (science) broadly available: BHP, GSK, PNNL.
  • LinkedIn AI recruiter products: 20,000+ companies; seats "increased 140% QoQ" (unusual phrasing — likely a growth rate, unclarified).
  • Consumer/devices: Bing/Edge five straight years of share gains; Windows positioned as "offload for unmetered intelligence" (edge AI narrative); Xbox reset with FY27 growth promise.

Sell-side read-through

  • Keirstead (UBS) asked the strategic question — open/custom model traction and how MSFT benefits given frontier-lab exposure. Nadella answered with the harness/context-ownership architecture ("token capital," swappable models, "every firm having a frontier"); Hood added Azure is fungible across models. Directional, not quantified.
  • Thill (Jefferies) asked whether the Azure beat was execution or a changed constraint environment. Hood: constraints persist (cited spot-market pricing); the beat came from CPU/GPU fleet efficiency and faster plug-in processes, quickly monetized because of the supply/demand imbalance — implicitly conceding part of the acceleration is non-linear efficiency timing.
  • Moerdler (Bernstein) pressed overcapacity risk and component-price margin impact. Hood's answer was the most substantive of the call: ~2/3 of CapEx is short-lived CPU/GPU assets that can be slowed; land/buildings are a smaller, staggeable share; diversification of book plus first-party app demand provides flexibility; component inflation "impacting everybody equivalently," cloud ROI improves vs. on-prem, and new contracts can reprice. Nadella added portfolio/mix framing ("run an efficient railroad"). No hard downside math.
  • Wood (Morgan Stanley) asked Copilot pilot-to-deployment and monetization drivers. Nadella: time-to-usage collapse (months→days), Outlook/Teams-parity engagement, E7/Agent 365 wiring, seat-plus-usage TAM expansion; Hood confirmed E5→Copilot→E7 ARPU progression and token-spend observability as E7's hook. Nadella gave an extended personal demo anecdote (Morgan Stanley ROIC report → Power BI/Fabric/GitHub/Agent 365 workflow).
  • Zelnick (Deutsche Bank) asked about Project Perception and trust. Nadella framed cyber as the same agentic/harness pattern; cited CyberGym evals (MAI-Cyber-1-Flash at 90% of tasks, 50% cost, frontier models for 10%) and model-resilience rationale.
  • Borges (Goldman) asked how CapEx ROI tracking has changed vs. a year ago. Hood effectively said the math hasn't changed; she pointed to TAM confidence and margin levers (silicon price-performance, model diversification, app+infra efficiency, portfolio breadth). No quantitative ROI framework was offered despite the direct ask.
  • Only six questions; no one asked about the Anthropic gain, the OpenAI equity line, the useful-life change's effect on comparability, gaming impairments, or the restated $175B CY26 CapEx figure. Scrutiny remains light relative to the capital intensity.

Management credibility

  • Delivery vs. guide: Azure 43% vs. 39–40% cc guide (beat); Cloud GM 65% vs. ~64% (beat); Windows OEM -5% vs. high-teens-decline guide (beat, second straight quarter of big OEM upside from channel inventory builds — the guide mechanics are repeatedly overshooting to the downside here); CapEx $41B vs. >$40B (met); M365 Commercial products +19% vs. mid-single-digit decline guide (large beat via in-period recognition — flagged mechanics, but magnitude unguided); Xbox content -10% vs. low-teens guide (in line/better). Q3's guided normalization items again played out as telegraphed.
  • The Q3 promise of "modest acceleration in H2 CY26" was upgraded: Q1 FY27 guided ~45% cc with explicit "H1 growth to accelerate" — a stronger, checkable commitment delivered one quarter later.
  • Commitment integrity watch: Q3 guided "constrained at least through 2026" and accelerating growth simultaneously; Q4 reconciled by attributing the beat to efficiency monetization, not capacity normalization. Consistent so far, but the efficiency source of beats is inherently less persistent than capacity adds.
  • Metric drift continues: GitHub Copilot moved from "4.7M subscribers" (Q2) to org counts (Q3) to "50M users" (Q4) — three different denominators in three quarters, making trend verification impossible from disclosures. Copilot seat series (20M → 30M) remains the one clean continuous metric.
  • The useful-life extension (15→25 years) is disclosed plainly with stated rationale, but it flatters future reported CapEx optics (leases moving below the CapEx line) and depreciation timing at the exact moment CapEx scrutiny peaks; "minimal benefit to FY27 operating income" is the only quantification — the full effect will be visible only in filings and cash-flow classification.
  • Bookings framing improved in candor: +18% ex-OpenAI vs. +11% cc reported, plus the new claim that all sequential commercial RPO growth came from non-frontier-model customers — a direct, checkable response to concentration concerns. Beyond-12-month RPO growth decelerated to +112% from +138%.
  • Under pressure (Moerdler overcapacity, Borges ROI), answers were qualitatively confident but quantitative payback math was declined both times. Nadella's "1873" railroad-cycle reference is a notable acknowledgment of bubble discourse from the CEO.
  • The $3.2B Anthropic gain within "other income ex-OpenAI" is a new disclosure item (a second frontier-lab equity stake now flowing through P&L) — disclosed, but its recurring volatility is unguided.

What changed versus the prior quarter

  • Azure: 39% cc → 43% actual, guided to ~45% cc with "H1 acceleration" — the re-acceleration promised for H2 CY26 arrived a quarter early, sourced from efficiency gains and process speed rather than just new capacity.
  • CY26 CapEx: ~$190B → ~$175B via useful-life-driven lease reclassification (real spend "unchanged"); Q1 FY27 CapEx guided >$50B, continuing the step-function up.
  • Bookings flipped back: +7% ex-OpenAI / -4% reported → +18% ex-OpenAI / +10–11% cc reported; RPO $627B → $678B; near-term tranche +39% → +37%; long tranche +138% → +112%.
  • GitHub Copilot usage-based pricing went from announced (June 1) to revenue-positive in-quarter: >60% QoQ Copilot revenue acceleration and an explicit contribution to the Azure beat; GitHub usage is now also a named gross-margin drag being actively repriced.
  • Copilot seats accelerated again: 15M → 20M → 30M, with net adds doubling QoQ; E7 went from teased (Q3) to millions of seats sold with a 400K flagship win.
  • FY26 margin outcome vs. FY27 guide: FY26 closed with margins "up ~1 point" as promised; FY27 guided to down less than 1 point — the margin-expansion phase explicitly ends as depreciation and AI COGS compound.
  • Useful-life extension introduced — a first-order accounting change affecting CapEx/lease classification and depreciation timing starting FY27.
  • Anthropic appears in the P&L for the first time as a $3.2B investment gain; OpenAI bookings swung back to tailwind; no new OpenAI contract-term detail this quarter.
  • Xbox: "reset" language formalized with an FY27 return-to-growth promise, but impairments recurred; Windows OEM guide moves from high-teens decline to low-20s decline for Q1 — the memory-price drag deepens.
  • Search decelerated (+10% vs. +12%) on third-party partnership impact; guided to mid-single digits.
  • New products launched: HorizonDB, Rayfin, Web IQ, Project Perception, Autopilots, Microsoft Frontier Co. (6,000 FDEs), MAI-Thinking-1 (first reasoning model), Cobalt 200 to 25+ DCs, AMD Helios / NVIDIA Vera Rubin commitments.

Bull case

  • Azure accelerated to 43% and is guided to ~45% with H1 acceleration still promised, while management says constraints persist — demand-led, with efficiency gains converting directly into revenue. RPO $678B (+84%) with 30% recognizable in 12 months (+37%), and the new disclosure that all sequential RPO growth came from non-frontier-model customers directly counters concentration risk.
  • Copilot monetization is compounding across all three levers simultaneously: seats (30M, adds doubling), ARPU (E5→E7, millions of E7 seats in 8 weeks, EY 400K), and consumption (Cowork metered billing live with thousands paying). Management guided M365 Commercial Cloud to accelerate through FY27 — an unusual explicit commitment.
  • The consumption transition is now producing revenue, not just bookings drag: GitHub Copilot revenue +60% QoQ, Dynamics service credits +4x QoQ, and usage-billing added to Cowork.
  • Unit economics improving at every layer: 4x Copilot throughput, 89%/84% GPU cost cuts in Dynamics/PowerPoint, MAI-Cyber at 50% cost of frontier, Maia 200 at 30%+ perf-per-dollar, dock-to-live down ~50% — and margins held (IC op margin flat YoY at 41%; company op margin up slightly).
  • Capital flexibility argument is credible: 2/3 of CapEx is short-lived silicon that can be throttled; FCF $19.6B; CFO +30%; FY27 FCF positive with margin down <1 point despite >$50B/quarter CapEx.
  • Foundry (100K customers, revenue >2x) and the agent stack (40M agents in Agent 365) show platform adoption beyond first-party.

Bear case

  • Q1 FY27 CapEx >$50B (>$200B annualized) against FY27 operating margins guided down ~1 point and opex still growing mid-to-high single digits — the cost curve is still steepening while the margin-expansion era ends. Cloud GM has marched 66% → 65% → "relatively stable."
  • The Azure beat leaned on efficiency monetization and in-quarter process improvements — inherently episodic levers; management itself flags Azure growth "can vary quarter-to-quarter based on capacity timing and contract mix."
  • Bookings quality: ex-OpenAI +18% is healthy but the 2.3-year RPO duration shortened, and OpenAI remains large enough to swing headline bookings by 7–8 points either direction each quarter.
  • M365 Commercial products +19% this quarter was pulled-forward in-period recognition; FY27 guides both Commercial products and Server to mid-single-digit declines — transactional revenue is a headwind next year.
  • Consumer deterioration continues and is compounding: Windows OEM/Devices guided to low-20s decline in Q1 and high-teens for FY27 (memory inflation + inventory + comps); Xbox impaired again with content/services guided down mid-single digits; Search decelerating on partnership losses.
  • The useful-life extension, while disclosed, improves reported CapEx and depreciation optics precisely when scrutiny is highest; operating-lease payments will migrate costs into CFO instead (already noted as a CFO offset this quarter).
  • ROI on the AI build remains qualitatively asserted — two direct analyst questions (Moerdler, Borges) got frameworks, not numbers. E7's "millions of seats" in two months is unaudited and pricing is undisclosed; GitHub's shifting user metrics obscure the base.

  • "Fungible fleet" doctrine — Nadella repeatedly frames CapEx discipline as building globally distributed, multi-workload (pretrain/post-train/inference), continuously modernized infrastructure rather than bespoke builds for single customers. This is his answer to both bubble and concentration questions.
  • Copilot/agent monetization across "high-value domains": information work (M365 Copilot), coding (GitHub), security, health, consumer. Emphasis on ARPU expansion (E5 + Copilot) over seat growth.
  • OpenAI relationship repositioning: new agreement framed as adding certainty on IP/AGI definitions; Amy Hood flags "increased volatility" in other income and switches guidance to exclude OpenAI investment impact going forward.
  • Sovereignty/localization: Azure customers in 33 countries building in-border AI capabilities.

Key numbers and quarter mechanics

  • Microsoft Cloud revenue $49.1B (+26%); gross margin 68%.
  • Segments: P&BP $33.0B (+17%); Intelligent Cloud $30.9B (+28%); More Personal Computing $13.8B (+4%).
  • M365 Commercial Cloud +17% (1pt benefit from in-period recognition); paid commercial seats +6% — growth is ARPU-led (E5, Copilot).
  • M365 Consumer Cloud +26%; consumer subs +7% to 90M+. LinkedIn +10% (Talent Solutions weak on hiring market). Dynamics 365 +18%.
  • Windows OEM & Devices +6% (Windows 10 end-of-support pull-in + elevated channel inventory — both flagged as temporary); Search ex-TAC +16% (third-party partnership benefit); Gaming -2%.

  • Other income/expense: $401M adjusted, but a ~$4.1B OpenAI-related drag sits in the GAAP line — confirmed as purely equity-method share of OpenAI losses, no accounting change, no impact from the new deal in Q1.
  • Q2 guide: revenue $79.5–80.6B (+14–16%); Azure ~37% cc; Microsoft Cloud GM ~66%; operating margins flat YoY, down sequentially (seasonality); tax rate ~19%.

Sell-side read-through

  • Weiss (Morgan Stanley) named the core tension: bookings +112% yet stock underperformance — asked directly whether AGI/architecture shifts could weaken Microsoft's position. Nadella's answer rested on "jagged intelligence" and systems layers, plus an unsolicited statement that contractually-defined AGI won't be achieved "anytime soon."
  • Thill (Jefferies) and Murphy (JPMorgan) both pressed concentration risk — OpenAI's role in the bookings/RPO blowout and AI-native commitments "20x their current revenue." Hood's defense: short ~2-year duration, broad customer/product base, GPU/CPU spend matched to contract duration, lead time on deliveries.
  • Moerdler (Bernstein) asked the bubble question outright. Hood's answer: RPO is booked, short-dated business; they've been capacity-short for many quarters and demand is broadening, not narrowing.
  • Zelnick (Deutsche Bank) quantified the cost side: Azure bears most of the capacity-shortfall revenue impact because first-party Copilot/R&D gets priority allocation; Hood conceded "the number could be higher."
  • Rangan (Goldman) probed the competitive loss narrative (another hyperscaler taking business). Nadella effectively confirmed they declined skewed, single-meter demand — "each time we say no… I feel better." Notable implicit admission that they are walking away from some large AI-infrastructure deals.

Management credibility

  • Beat-and-raise-quality quarter with specific, quantified claims (RPO duration, capacity %, token throughput +30% per GPU) rather than vagueness — supports credibility.
  • Straight answers under pressure: Hood directly confirmed the $4.1B OpenAI loss line and conceded Azure revenue would "be higher" absent constraints — no evasion detected.
  • Consistency: capacity-constraint messaging has persisted across quarters per Hood ("we thought we were going to catch up, we are not"), and the guide now formally extends it through FY26 — management is not over-promising a near-term fix.
  • Watch items: (1) ARPU-led M365 growth with only +6% seats depends on continued Copilot/E5 upsell; (2) in-period recognition contributed 1pt to M365 Commercial Cloud; (3) "demand exceeds supply" framing conveniently caps Azure growth scrutiny — Q2's ~37% cc guide vs. Q1's 39% cc is a deceleration the constraint narrative absorbs.
  • Language change: switching guidance to ex-OpenAI basis while flagging "increased volatility" signals the OpenAI P&L line will get bigger and less predictable.

Bull case

  • $392B RPO (+51%) with ~2-year duration implies near-term consumption, not long-dated promises; the $250B OpenAI increment isn't even in it yet — a bookings pipeline for Q2+.
  • Azure +40% while capacity-constrained and share-gaining; constraint resolution is upside, not risk, to the growth rate.
  • Copilot monetization is showing real seat expansion and ARPU lift at enterprise scale (PwC, Lloyds), with GitHub/Fabric/security providing multiple second engines.
  • Operating margin expansion to 49% despite $34.9B CapEx shows efficiency gains partially offsetting AI COGS; FCF +33%.
  • Fungible-fleet strategy mitigates stranded-asset risk; management demonstrably willing to decline mispriced demand.

Bear case

  • Bookings quality question: OpenAI is a material driver of +112% bookings, and its losses already cost Microsoft ~$4.1B in a single quarter via equity method — the anchor AI customer is deeply loss-making.
  • Azure growth decelerating on a cc basis (39% → ~37% guided) with constraints lasting at least three more quarters; capacity shortfall revenue impact unquantified but admitted.
  • Gross margin compression is structural in the guide (Microsoft Cloud GM to ~66%, down YoY); CapEx accelerating (FY26 growth > FY25) means depreciation pressure compounds from here.
  • Q1 had identifiable one-time/flattering items: Windows pull-in ahead of end-of-support, elevated channel inventory, in-period recognition point, Office 2024 transactional strength, third-party search partnership benefit — several reverse or normalize in Q2.
  • Competitive signal: Nadella's comments confirm hyperscaler rivals are winning large AI deals Microsoft chose not to take — the "fungibility" framing may partly rationalize lost share in the largest training clusters.
