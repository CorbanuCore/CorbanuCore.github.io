Jul 30, 2026+15.32%Q2 FY2026
Read transcript briefing

Quarter in one view

  • Revenue $200.6B, +20% y/y ex-FX; operating income $27.5B, +43% y/y. Results include Prime Day shifted into Q2 (was entirely Q3 in 2025) — a material y/y flatterer that reverses in Q3.
  • Operating income includes ~$1.2B of one-time benefits: ~$600M tariff refunds (NA segment; "significant majority" of expected refunds) and ~$600M energy-derivative fair-value gain (AWS segment). Adjusted, OI is ~$26.3B.
  • AWS is the story again: $42.2B revenue, +36.7% y/y — fifth straight acceleration, fastest in 18 quarters; +$4.6B q/q (~80% more than the prior largest increase); $169B run rate; backlog $496B, growing triple digits y/y. AWS operating income $16.6B, ~39% margin (+650bps y/y; +520bps ex-derivative gain).
  • NA $116.2B (+16%), 7.9% op margin; International $42.2B (+15% ex-FX), 4.1% op margin; units +17%. Ads $19.8B, +26% y/y.
  • Cash CapEx $53.1B in Q2; 2026 cash CapEx guidance raised to ~$220B from ~$200B, explicitly on memory cost inflation. Management says capacity will still fall short of demand in 2026 and 2027, with "striking" 2028 demand already visible.
  • Q3 guide: revenue $197–202B (Prime Day shift costs ~400bps y/y; FX ~80bps headwind); operating income $22.5–26.5B — a sequential step-down at the midpoint.

What management is focused on

  • Justifying the CapEx supercycle with an explicit ROIC framework: data centers (30+ year life, capital spent ~2 years pre-monetization, 5–6 server generations of economics) vs. servers/networking (purchased months before service, <3-year breakeven, 5–6-year useful life, most AI capacity contracted 5+ years). Jassy claims AI margins/returns are tracking "a little ahead" of core cloud at the same stage.
  • Demand visibility as the answer to capital-intensity skepticism: 2027 capacity "largely reserved," meaningful 2028 reservations already; "barbell" demand framing (labs + runaway apps on one end, enterprise productivity use cases on the other, with the middle — existing production workloads — still to come and expected to be the largest segment).
  • Full-stack AI positioning without model exclusivity: Bedrock as the multi-model inference layer; Bedrock Agents (policies, payments, web search, Strands harness); agentic apps (Kiro, Amazon Q, Connect, Transform, new Continuum security product). Jassy confirmed Amazon is building its own frontier model — new disclosure — for cost control, prioritization, and speed, while insisting AWS can succeed without one.
  • Custom silicon momentum: chips run rate >$25B (up from >$20B last quarter), triple-digit growth; Anthropic and OpenAI multi-year, multi-gigawatt Trainium commitments; revenue commitments "nearly 3x q/q"; Graviton5 growing ~2x faster than Graviton4; 98% of top 1,000 EC2 customers on Graviton.
  • Stores: everyday essentials/grocery via same-day perishables (2,300 U.S. cities), Amazon Now expansion, Amazon Supply Chain Services (P&G, 3M, Lands' End, American Eagle as launch customers), and Alexa for Shopping as the agentic commerce vehicle.
  • Funding: Jassy acknowledged debt issuance this year and "a lot of options" for funding the build-out — a new topic introduced under questioning, with no specifics.

Key numbers and quarter mechanics

  • AWS: $42.2B, +36.7% y/y; $16.6B OI; ~39% margin (+650bps y/y, +520bps ex-derivative gain). Backlog $496B (vs. $364B ex-Anthropic last quarter), triple-digit y/y growth. AI revenue run rate >$25B (from >$15B), chips run rate >$25B (from >$20B). Bedrock: customers spent more in Q2 than all prior quarters combined (per Anmuth's question, not disputed).
  • Segments: NA $116.2B (+16%), $9.1B OI, 7.9% margin (flat y/y vs. Q1's 7.9%); International $42.2B (+15% ex-FX), $1.7B OI, 4.1% margin (up from 3.6% in Q1). Units +17% (vs. +15% in Q1).
  • One-timers: ~$600M tariff refunds in NA (most of expected total; some will be passed back to customers where pass-through is traceable); ~$600M energy-derivative gain primarily in AWS — management flagged these can reverse in future quarters.
  • CapEx: $53.1B cash CapEx in Q2 (vs. $43.2B in Q1); 2026 guide raised ~$20B to ~$220B on memory inflation. Memory/SSD/HDD inflation acknowledged as broad-based; new contracts will price in costs, existing contracts are fixed-price for their duration.
  • Cost headwinds: fuel inflation (Middle East conflict) and higher line-haul rates (driver capacity); partially offset by the April FBA fuel/logistics surcharge. Ex-fuel/line-haul, shipping cost growth ran below unit growth, consistent with last quarter.
  • Q3 guide mechanics: $197–202B revenue; Prime Day timing alone is ~400bps of the y/y deceleration; FX ~80bps headwind; OI $22.5–26.5B. No Leo cost commentary this quarter (last quarter flagged ~$1B y/y NA headwind and Q4 capitalization start — not updated).
  • Stores metrics: grocery >$150B 2025 gross sales (#2 U.S. grocer); monthly active perishables customers +50% YTD; same-day perishables in 2,300 cities; 9 of top 10 best sellers in those cities are perishables; Pharmacy new customers +2x H1, same-day prescriptions ~5x, ~$250M customer savings (+400% y/y); same-day/overnight items +40% y/y H1.
  • Alexa for Shopping: 350M+ users LTM, active users ~doubled, interactions +5x y/y; sponsored-prompt clickers convert 48% more often and spend 21% more. Alexa+ users sign up for Prime at ~25% higher rates; Alexa-for-Shopping users spend 40%+ more per order.

Product and launch scorecard

  • Trainium: strongest quarter of evidence yet — >$25B chips run rate, commitments ~3x q/q, Anthropic + OpenAI multi-gigawatt commitments, startup adopters named (Neurorobotics, Odyssey, Twelve Labs, Poolside, etc.) plus Uber and Pinterest. External rack/chip sales now "actively" being explored with "a real chance we'll do that" — firmer than last quarter's "openness over the next couple of years." No Trainium3/4 subscription-status update this quarter.
  • Amazon Q: named enterprise roster (3M, Allianz, AstraZeneca, BMW, Exxon, Intuit, NBA, NFL, Southwest, etc.); Q2 added autonomous background agents, activity feed, 16 integrations (Adobe, Moody's, Snowflake). Real production deployments claimed; no revenue figures.
  • Kiro: usage tripled q/q; claimed up to 50% more cost-effective than competitors. Early but directional.
  • AWS Continuum (new): agentic vulnerability discovery/validation/remediation; launched on the thesis that enterprise AI adoption is gated by security concerns. No metrics; one to watch.
  • Amazon's own frontier model: newly confirmed as in development — a strategic shift from prior "no need for our own frontier model" posture; rationale is cost/prioritization control, not model-race ambition.
  • Amazon Now: 9 countries, 250+ cities (added 80 U.S. cities/towns + Egypt cities in Q2); gross sales and units +80% q/q; customers +60% q/q. Scaling fast from a small base.
  • Amazon Supply Chain Services (new): B2B logistics productizing Amazon's network; P&G, 3M, Lands' End, American Eagle signed. Potentially significant; no revenue disclosure.
  • Amazon Leo: ~400 satellites in orbit (from >250 last quarter); initial service "this year" (previously "commercial launch Q3" — language slightly softened); "meaningful revenue commitments" from enterprise/government, 20+ distribution partners. No revenue sizing, no cost update, no confirmation of Q4 capitalization.
  • Alexa+: expanded to Germany, Austria, France, Brazil; engagement and Prime-conversion stats cited above. Monetization still indirect.
  • Ads: $19.8B, +26% (accelerating from +22%); sports inventory (TNF, NBA, WNBA, NASCAR) sold out; Ads Agent users see 8% lower CPM / 6% lower CPA, expanded to 11 countries.

Sell-side read-through

  • Question focus: (1) AWS margin sustainability at 39% and whether AI is dilutive (Anmuth); (2) whether acceleration is capacity-driven and gigawatt adds in H2/2027 (Post); (3) whether long-lived data center spend can slow in 2027 and Trainium external-sale ROIC (Nowak); (4) application-layer ambitions and funding sources for the build-out (Sebastian); (5) RPO vs. capacity outlook and contract pricing under component inflation (Gawrelski); (6) grocery/fast-commerce consumer signal (Sheridan).
  • Notable disclosures extracted under questioning: Bedrock Q2 spend exceeded all prior quarters combined (surfaced via Anmuth); Amazon is building its own frontier model (first explicit confirmation); debt already issued in 2026 with more funding options under review; existing AWS contracts are fixed-price, so memory inflation lands on Amazon until re-pricing — new deals will reflect costs.
  • Evasions/soft spots: no gigawatt figures for H2 or 2027 despite a direct ask (only "on pace" to double 2025 power capacity by end-2027); no answer on whether data center spend can slow in 2027 (answered with demand narrative instead); no funding plan detail ("nothing to share today"); no quantification of memory-inflation margin impact beyond the $20B CapEx raise; no Leo financial update despite it being a flagged watch item.
  • The 39% AWS margin question was answered with "disciplined efficiency" and "capacity optimization" plus a reminder margins will fluctuate — management did not endorse 39% as a run rate, and 130bps of the y/y margin gain was the derivative gain.

Management credibility

  • Delivery vs. prior guidance: Q2 revenue $200.6B vs. $194–199B guide (above high end); OI $27.5B vs. $20–24B guide (well above, though ~$1.2B of one-timers did meaningful work — adjusted ~$26.3B still above range). The Prime Day timing tailwind flagged last quarter materialized as advertised.
  • Consistency: the "capacity constrained through 2027" message is consistent with prior quarters and now backed by the $496B backlog and 2028 reservation commentary. The CapEx raise to $220B was attributed specifically to memory — the same cost risk flagged (unquantified) last quarter, which lends credibility to prior warnings.
  • New tension to monitor: last quarter's framing was that AWS doesn't need its own frontier model; this quarter Jassy confirmed one is being built while arguing it isn't necessary. The rationale (cost, prioritization, speed) is coherent, but the shift deserves tracking — including its cost, which was not addressed.
  • Specificity remained high on AWS/chips/backlog; lower on: Leo (no revenue, cost, or capitalization update — a regression in disclosure on a flagged item), AI revenue composition within the >$25B run rate, and funding plans.
  • The ROIC framework (server breakevens <3 years, 5–6-year lives, 5-year AI contract terms, 30+ year data centers) is the most detailed capital-return articulation management has given — checkable over time via AWS margins and FCF, but currently assertion.
  • One-timer transparency was good: both items quantified, segmented, and the derivative gain's volatility flagged. Tariff-refund treatment (mostly reinvested in prices, some customer refunds) was explained plainly.

What changed versus the prior quarter

  • AWS accelerated again: +36.7% vs. +28% in Q1 (fifth straight acceleration); backlog $496B vs. $364B (ex-Anthropic); AI run rate >$25B vs. >$15B; chips run rate >$25B vs. >$20B; Trainium commitments "nearly 3x q/q" (previously >$225B cumulative — metric basis changed, not directly comparable).
  • 2026 CapEx raised ~$200B → ~$220B on memory inflation; Q2 cash CapEx $53.1B vs. $43.2B in Q1.
  • New disclosures: Amazon building its own frontier model; debt issued in 2026; OpenAI now named alongside Anthropic as a multi-gigawatt Trainium commitment; Trainium external sales moved from "openness" to "active conversations, real chance."
  • Rufus branding absent — the shopping assistant is now "Alexa for Shopping" (350M users, interactions +5x y/y vs. Rufus MAU +115%/engagement +400% last quarter; metric basis changed).
  • Leo language shifted from "commercial launch Q3" to "initial satellite internet service this year"; ~400 satellites vs. >250; no cost or capitalization update.
  • Ads accelerated to +26% from +22%; International margin improved to 4.1% from 3.6%; NA margin flat at 7.9% despite the flagged Leo headwind (aided by the $600M tariff refund).
  • New products: AWS Continuum, Amazon Supply Chain Services, Q autonomous agents; Amazon Now scaled from early signal to 250+ cities with +80% q/q GMV.
  • Q3 guide implies deceleration, with Prime Day timing (~400bps) now a headwind rather than a tailwind.

Bull case

  • AWS is accelerating on a $169B base with $496B backlog, 2027 capacity largely reserved, and 2028 demand already visible — multi-year revenue visibility is exceptional, and management's $1T-revenue-business framing signals conviction.
  • AI margins are tracking ahead of the core-cloud precedent, not dilutive: 39% AWS margin (+520bps y/y ex-one-timer) while spending $53B/quarter undercuts the "AI is lower margin" bear thesis.
  • Custom silicon flywheel strengthening: >$25B run rate, commitments ~3x q/q, both frontier labs committed, external chip sales now probable — a margin and supply advantage plus a potential new revenue line.
  • Stores momentum is broad: units +17%, perishables inflecting (2,300 same-day cities, +50% MAUs), Amazon Now scaling, Ads +26% with agentic-commerce monetization data (48% higher conversion on sponsored prompts) directly countering the disintermediation bear case.
  • Multiple new revenue surfaces opened this quarter (Supply Chain Services, Continuum, Q enterprise deployments, Leo initial service) on top of an already-record profitability base.

Bear case

  • CapEx raised to ~$220B with explicit acknowledgment of FCF headwinds for years; memory inflation is now a realized $20B cost, existing contracts are fixed-price (margin absorption risk), and funding is shifting toward debt with no articulated plan.
  • ~$1.2B of Q2 operating income was one-time; the derivative gain can reverse, and the tariff refunds are largely complete. Underlying OI beat is smaller than headline.
  • Q3 guide implies a sharp sequential OI step-down ($22.5–26.5B vs. $27.5B) and revenue deceleration even after adjusting for Prime Day optics.
  • Demand concentration risk persists: the "barbell" framing concedes current consumption is dominated by AI labs and a few runaway apps; the enterprise middle of the barbell is thesis, not yet volume. Backlog breadth remains undetailed.
  • Leo disclosure regressed (no cost, revenue, or capitalization update) while the launch language softened from "commercial launch Q3" to "initial service this year."
  • The frontier-model build adds a new, unquantified cost and strategic complexity, and partially contradicts prior messaging.

Next-quarter watchlist

  • AWS growth vs. 36.7% and backlog trajectory; any disclosure on enterprise (non-lab) AI adoption to validate the "middle of the barbell" thesis; whether the energy-derivative gain reverses.
  • CapEx: H2 run rate vs. the ~$220B guide; memory-cost commentary; any 2027 CapEx framing; debt issuance and funding mix detail.
  • AWS margin durability ex-one-timers — does ~39% hold, and does the claimed Trainium advantage show up in segment margins as capacity comes online?
  • Trainium external sales: first deal, structure (chips vs. racks), and stated ROIC vs. internal cloud use; updated commitment figures on a comparable basis.
  • Leo: confirmation of initial service launch, first revenue, launch cadence, and whether Q4 cost capitalization proceeds as previously flagged; request the missing cost update.
  • Q3 results vs. guide with Prime Day timing stripped; NA margin without tariff refunds; fuel/line-haul cost trajectory vs. surcharge offsets.
  • Frontier-model program: scope, cost, and whether it changes the OpenAI/Anthropic relationships; Kiro/Q/Continuum traction metrics; Alexa for Shopping monetization (sponsored-prompt revenue disclosure).

Quarter in one view

  • Revenue $181.5B, +17% y/y (+15% ex-FX; FX added $2.9B / ~180bps). Operating income $23.9B; operating margin 13.1%, described as the highest ever.
  • AWS was the story: $37.6B revenue, +28% y/y (fastest in 15 quarters, +480bps acceleration), $150B annualized run rate, $14.2B operating income. Backlog $364B, excluding the newly announced >$100B Anthropic deal.
  • Stores: units +15% y/y (fastest since late COVID); NA segment $104.1B (+12%), 7.9% op margin; International $39.8B (+11% ex-FX), 3.6% op margin. Ads $17.2B, +22% y/y.
  • Cash CapEx $43.2B in Q1, primarily AWS/genAI. Q2 guide: revenue $194–199B, operating income $20–24B, with ~$1B y/y Leo cost headwind in NA and a seasonal SBC step-up.

What management is focused on

  • AI as a "once-in-a-lifetime" platform shift: Jassy repeatedly framed AWS's full stack (SageMaker, Bedrock, Trainium/Graviton, agent tooling) as the reason customers choose AWS, and committed to "significant" multi-year capital investment.
  • Custom silicon as a strategic and economic weapon: Trainium claimed to save "tens of billions" of CapEx annually at scale and deliver "several hundred basis points" of operating margin advantage vs. third-party chips for inference.
  • Agentic AI across every surface: Bedrock managed agents with OpenAI (stateful runtime), QRO/Transform/Quick enterprise agents, Rufus in Stores, Creative Agent in Ads, Health AI, Alexa+.
  • Fulfillment efficiency: unit growth (15%) outpacing outbound shipping (+12%) and fulfillment expense (+9%); next-gen robotics in all 2026 U.S. large-format FC launches.
  • New long-duration businesses: Amazon Leo (commercial launch Q3, Globalstar acquisition, Apple direct-to-device deal), Zoox, entertainment.

Key numbers and quarter mechanics

  • AWS: +28% y/y; +$2B q/q revenue (largest Q4→Q1 increase ever); AI revenue run rate >$15B, growing triple digits; Bedrock customer spend +170% q/q; 125,000+ Bedrock customers; ~80% of Fortune 100 using Bedrock.
  • Chips: ~40% q/q growth; >$20B annual run rate, triple-digit y/y; management claims a ~$50B "stand-alone equivalent" run rate and top-3 global data center chip position. Trainium revenue commitments >$225B; Trainium2 ~30% better price/performance than comparable GPUs and "largely sold out"; Trainium3 (shipping since start of 2026, 30–40% better than T2) "nearly fully subscribed"; Trainium4 (~18 months out) partly reserved.
  • Stores/grocery: >$150B 2025 grocery gross sales (claims #2 U.S. grocer); perishables +40x y/y; same-day perishable shoppers add ~3x items, spend >80% more; >1B items delivered same-day/overnight YTD; average prices on Amazon.com declined y/y in Q1.
  • Rufus: MAU +115%, engagement +400% y/y. Alexa+: 2x conversation volume, 3x purchases on devices vs. classic Alexa.
  • Q2 guide mechanics: Prime Day shifts into Q2 for most large geographies (was Q3 for all countries in 2025) — a material y/y timing tailwind; FX ~10bps headwind; ~$1B Leo cost increase in NA; fuel inflation partly offset by new FBA fuel/logistics surcharge; Leo cost capitalization begins Q4.

Product and launch scorecard

  • OpenAI on Bedrock (GPT-5.4 live "yesterday," 5.5 within weeks; managed agents in preview): strategically significant — closes a model-gap, and the stateful managed-agents product is claimed as unique. No revenue data yet; demand described as "heavy customer interest."
  • Trainium3: shipping since start of 2026, nearly fully subscribed — strongest evidence of silicon traction alongside $225B commitments and Meta's "tens of millions of Graviton cores" deal.
  • Quick desktop app: just announced; new-customer growth >4x q/q but from an undisclosed base.
  • Amazon Leo: >250 satellites in orbit, 20+ launches planned 2026, 30+ in 2027; commercial service Q3; named commitments (Delta ≥half fleet from 2028, JetBlue, AT&T, Vodafone, Apple, NASA, others). Pre-revenue at scale; ~$1B y/y cost drag in Q2; capitalization starts Q4 — margin optics will shift.
  • Amazon Now (30-min delivery): 9 countries, India orders +25% m/m, Prime members tripling frequency — early but real adoption signal.
  • Zoox: ~2M miles, 350K+ riders, public in Las Vegas/SF, Uber app integration announced — still subscale.
  • Health AI and Seller Central AI: launched, "early," no metrics.

Sell-side read-through

  • Question focus clustered on: (1) durability and breadth of AI demand/backlog (Nowak, Sebastian), (2) capital intensity and custom-silicon economics (Sheridan, Post), (3) new-business sizing (Leo — Sanderson), (4) cost inflation (memory — Khajuria) and agentic-commerce ad model risk (Khajuria).

  • Management pushed back on the agentic-commerce threat to ads: argued multi-turn agent conversations create more sponsored surface area (sponsored prompts; ~20% of shoppers engaging Brand Prompts in Rufus continue the conversation).
  • Memory inflation framed as net positive for cloud migration (suppliers prioritize hyperscalers) — an analyst-friendly answer that also concedes cost risk is being actively managed, not quantified.

  • The $50B "stand-alone" chips framing is a management construct — useful for scale context but not a reported figure; treat with care.
  • CapEx messaging is consistent and pre-emptive: acknowledges near-term FCF pressure when CapEx growth outpaces revenue, cites 6–24-month monetization lag and prior AWS cycle as precedent. No new total CapEx number given despite a direct question — a mild evasion.

What changed versus the prior quarter

  • No prior-quarter summary was provided; baseline established from this transcript only. Within-transcript markers of change: AWS growth accelerated 480bps to 28% (fastest in 15 quarters); OpenAI models and managed agents newly added to Bedrock; Anthropic >$100B deal and Meta Graviton commitment announced since the last call; Prime Day moved to Q2 in most geographies (vs. Q3 for all countries in 2025); Globalstar acquisition and Apple Leo partnership newly announced; FBA fuel surcharge newly implemented.

  • Custom silicon is a structural margin and supply advantage: sold-out Trainium2/3, reserved Trainium4, claimed CapEx savings and margin uplift, plus optionality to sell racks externally.

  • AI revenue concentration risk: labs drive outsized spend; backlog breadth is asserted, not detailed; the >$100B Anthropic deal sits outside reported backlog.

  • Agentic commerce could disintermediate Amazon's high-margin ad and retail funnel if third-party agents improve; management's rebuttal is thesis, not yet data.
  • Leo is consuming ~$1B y/y incremental cost pre-revenue with execution risk on 20+ launches this year; Q4 capitalization will flatter margins mechanically.
