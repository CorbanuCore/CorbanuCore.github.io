Quarter in one view

  • Q2 FY2026 revenue of $16.1B, $1.8B above the midpoint of the $13.8–14.8B guide and above the high end — seventh consecutive quarter above guidance. Non-GAAP gross margin of 41.8% (~280bps above the 39% guide) and non-GAAP EPS of $0.42 vs. $0.20 guided.
  • Unlike Q1, the beat was not attributed to one-offs: upside drivers cited were higher revenue, better yields, and higher ASPs from mix and pricing actions. No one-time gain or reserved-inventory sale was flagged this quarter.
  • Demand still outruns supply: despite wafer outs exceeding internal expectations, Zinsner reiterated supply remains "very tight," with supply growth skewed to late Q3 and into Q4, especially servers. Arcuri's framing of >$1B/quarter undershipping was not corrected.
  • Q3 guide: revenue $15.8–16.8B (midpoint $16.3B), gross margin 42%, EPS $0.38, tax 11% — a sequential gross-margin step-up despite continued 18A mix dilution.
  • The capital posture changed materially: 2026 CapEx raised to "more than $20 billion" (from "flat vs. last year" last quarter), 2027 CapEx guided "significantly above" 2026, and the 14A decision was made in Q2 to fully commit to high-volume ramp in 2028 — the commitments-before-capacity doctrine is now being exercised in the spend direction.

What management is focused on

  • Supply expansion as the binding constraint: wafer starts on Intel 3 (Granite Rapids called "extremely tight"), 18A, and 18A-P, plus back-end chokepoints — substrates, T-glass, memory — which Zinsner called "probably our most challenged parts of our supply chain." Tool POs are being locked in aggressively and cleanroom build-outs accelerated.
  • The CPU:GPU parity thesis firmed up: Zinsner now says the ratio is "almost in parity at this point and could eventually even skew more to CPUs on a unit basis" — an upgrade from last quarter's 3–4:1 inference framing. Server CPU industry unit growth now forecast at strong double digits for both 2026 and 2027, with momentum into 2028.
  • Gross margin consolidation: Zinsner's stated #1 goal this year was gross margins "comfortably into the 40s in every quarter" — declared achieved for H1 and guided for Q3, with improvement off that base as the next phase.
  • 14A commitment: PDK 0.9 on track for October, risk production for internal products H2 2027, and the Q2 decision to commit to high-volume ramp in 2028 — justified by yield/defect-density/performance "ahead of the schedule" and customer engagement levels.
  • Capital funding for the ramp: $30B cash + $10B revolver, ~$10B of monetizable noncore assets, customer prepays already received, and an explicit acknowledgment that capital markets may be tapped "if we're super successful."
  • New adjacencies: design services revenue growing ~3x YoY, ASIC run rate now ~$2B heading to $4B, edge/physical AI (CCPG rename), and a disclosed memory-architecture effort (Seok-Hee Lee, ex-SK Hynix CEO, hired).

Key numbers and quarter mechanics

  • Segments: CCPG (renamed from CCG) $8.9B (+15% QoQ, better than expected; operating profit $2.3B, 26% margin, down ~$173M QoQ due to inventory write-downs on stranded match-set products). DCAI $6.3B (+24% QoQ, +59% YoY, "meaningfully ahead"; operating profit $2.5B, 40% margin, up ~$1B QoQ). Foundry $5.8B (+6% QoQ; operating loss $2.1B, improved $348M QoQ). External foundry revenue $293M (vs. $174M in Q1).
  • AI-driven businesses grew >70% YoY and are now ~70% of revenue (vs. 60% / +40% YoY in Q1). AI PC revenue +26% QoQ, now ~2/3 of client mix. Edge ~10% of CCPG revenue.
  • 18A output ~25% above target and up >50% QoQ. Panther Lake primary SKU cost down ~50% year-to-date, with another ~20% reduction on track this year and further cuts planned in 2027.
  • Cash: Q2 operating cash flow $7B; ~$30B cash and short-term investments; $10B revolver. No FCF figure was given in the transcript.
  • CapEx: 2026 now >$20B, "up significantly versus our expectations entering the year"; tooling spend +40% YoY in 2026 (vs. +25% cited last quarter); 2027 "significantly above" 2026 with the vast majority in the U.S. Gross-to-net offsets now mostly the investment tax credit (~$0.35/$1 back on U.S. spend, low-single-digit billions currently, with a lag).
  • OpEx: "roughly $16.5 billion for the year" — formally above the abandoned $16B target.
  • NCI: ~$250M in each of Q3 and Q4 2026; ~$1.1B total for 2027–2028 (GAAP) — consistent with the post-Fab-34-buyout guide.
  • Q3 mechanics: client revenue flattish (edge growth offsets), with CPU inventory build expected in Q3 and softness in Q4; PC TAM still down low double digits for 2026 with subseasonal H2. Server supply loosens late Q3/Q4 but "we will not catch up. We will be behind in the fourth quarter."
  • Gross-margin walk into Q3: Q2's inventory write-down lifts, offset by Panther Lake/Granite becoming a larger mix share while still below corporate average — netting to roughly flat-to-up (41.8% → 42% guide). Long-term drop-through framework reiterated at 40–60%.

Product and launch scorecard

  • Panther Lake / Core Series 3 (18A): 400+ designs across consumer and commercial; launched on the A stepping as a cost-optimized mainstream part. Still below corporate-average margin but cost down ~50% YTD; Zinsner now commits that Panther Lake margins "will be above the corporate average" as yields improve — timing unspecified. Wildcat Lake named as a second lead 18A product in ramp.
  • Xeon 6 / Granite Rapids (Intel 3): "one of the fastest ramping products in Intel history"; Q2 server YoY growth "strongest on record"; Granite Rapids specifically called "extremely tight" on demand reception. Intel 3 wafer-start expansion is the key server supply lever for H2 2026 and 2027.
  • Clearwater Forest (Xeon 6+): launched in Q2 — first server-class product on 18A. A concrete 18A server milestone delivered on schedule.
  • 18A-P: entered risk production; ~5% performance uplift over 18A claimed; "ready before the end of this year"; positioned as the external-customer node.
  • 14A: PDK 0.5 complete, PDK 0.9 on track for October; 256Mb SRAM yield, defect density, and performance all "ahead of schedule"; internal risk production H2 2027; committed high-volume ramp 2028 (decision made in Q2). Still no named external customer or announced design win.
  • Diamond Rapids / Coral Rapids: mentioned by Tan in the roadmap list (Coral Rapids restores SMT/multithreading) but again with no dates — third consecutive call without timing.
  • ASIC / purpose-built silicon: revenue +~20% QoQ, nearly tripled YoY; run rate now "~$2 billion or at least approaching" (vs. >$1B last quarter), with a $4B run rate projected "in the not-too-distant future"; Fortinet security-processor win announced; Tan cited a >$100B TAM. Design services revenue ~3x YoY.
  • Advanced packaging / EMIB-T: backlog growing, yield and reliability hitting targets, customer ramps in 2027; CapEx dollars flowing here including substrate prepayments to vendors.
  • Arc graphics: 40+ integrated designs; new Arc G-Series handheld-gaming processors launched. vPro activations +1,500% over four quarters. 130 Series 3 edge-AI design wins including robotics.
  • Memory: exploratory — Z-Angle Memory raised by Rakers; Tan confirmed internal compute-memory integration work and the Seok-Hee Lee hire, "stay tuned."

Sell-side read-through

  • Reitzes pressed whether the CapEx hike implies 14A/18A-P part orders; Zinsner framed it as broad-based confidence across all businesses anchored in LTAs, skewed to front-end — no specific external order confirmed. His CPU-TAM question (competitor's $220B-by-2030 figure) got agreement on direction, no number endorsement.
  • Moore asked directly about server share gains vs. AMD and Arm; Tan made no share-gain claim — "some areas we are still behind, but we are catching up very fast... time will tell" — and notably pivoted to Arm as partner/customer rather than threat.
  • Rasgon extracted the client decomposition: Q2 strength was "largely ASP" (mix + like-for-like price increases to pass through cost inflation), with the underlying market down YoY. He also surfaced the inventory write-down (stranded match-set products) and confirmed the Q3 gross-margin guide is roughly flat ex-charge because 18A mix dilution offsets the write-down lift.
  • Arcuri established the Q4 setup: supply logjam breaks late Q3, Q4 revenue should step up, but Intel remains behind demand through year-end. He also got the 40–60% drop-through framework reaffirmed for next-year modeling.
  • Arya challenged when foundry confidence becomes announced customers — answer pointed to PDK 0.9 (October) as the catalyst, still no commitment to a named win. Zinsner declined to give a 2027 CapEx number ("need another quarter or 2").
  • Muse got the server revenue shape: unit growth plus ASP-per-core uplift from rising core counts implies "well north of a double-digit CAGR" for DCAI over the next few years — the most explicit multi-year growth frame management has given.
  • Rakers got the ASIC run-rate upgrade ($2B → $4B) and opened the memory-strategy thread, which Tan engaged with (SK Hynix hire, compute-memory integration) without economics.

Management credibility

  • Beat streak extended to seven quarters, and this beat is cleaner than Q1's: no flagged one-time EPS gain or non-repeatable inventory sales; upside attributed to yields, mix, and pricing. The Q1 concern about beat quality was directly addressed by the nature of this print.
  • Delivery against prior-quarter flags: DCAI guided "up double digits" and delivered +24% QoQ; gross margin guided 39% and printed 41.8%; the Q2 Panther Lake margin drag was absorbed better than guided. 18A yield claims ("ahead of targets set in March") remain unquantified but directionally consistent for a third quarter.
  • Credibility dents: CapEx guidance has now moved four times in four quarters (down → flat-to-down → flat → >$20B, a roughly $3B+ raise in 90 days per Reitzes' framing). OpEx settled at ~$16.5B vs. the original $16B target. The "commitments before capacity" doctrine is being stretched — the 2028 14A high-volume commitment was made with zero announced external design wins, justified by engagement levels and internal demand.
  • Under pressure: Tan again gave no Diamond/Coral Rapids dates (third call); Zinsner declined a 2027 CapEx number and would not delineate internal vs. external CapEx split; no external foundry customer was named despite direct questioning. The ASIC run-rate number moved from "north of $1B" to "~$2B" in one quarter — favorable, but the pace of revision warrants verification.
  • Positive specificity: Panther Lake cost-down numbers (50% YTD, +20% more this year), 18A output vs. target (+25%), and the $0.35/$1 investment-tax-credit mechanics are concrete, checkable disclosures.

What changed versus the prior quarter

  • The Q2 guide ($13.8–14.8B / 39% / $0.20) was beaten decisively ($16.1B / 41.8% / $0.42), and the beat quality improved — execution- and pricing-driven rather than one-off-driven.
  • Gross margin trajectory: the 40%+ waypoint is now declared a durable floor ("comfortably into the 40s in every quarter"), with Q3 guided to 42% — the 18A dilution headwind is being absorbed faster than the prior quarter's framing implied.
  • Capital posture reversed from discipline to build-out: CapEx >$20B in 2026 (from flat), 2027 "significantly above," tooling +40% YoY, and the 14A 2028 high-volume ramp fully committed — all before any announced external 14A win. Management explicitly inverted the logic: "you can read that now inversely... we must have pretty significant confidence in our customers."
  • Server outlook upgraded again: double-digit industry unit growth now extended through 2027 with momentum into 2028; DCAI framed as "well north of a double-digit CAGR" over the next few years.
  • CPU:GPU ratio framing advanced from 3–4:1 in inference to "almost in parity... could eventually even skew more to CPUs."
  • ASIC run rate doubled in the telling (> $1B → ~$2B, targeting $4B); design services (~3x YoY) and Fortinet added as new named vectors.
  • Client story clarified: Q2 strength was pricing/mix, not units; H2 client is flattish-to-soft with production pivoting to server CPUs — the constrained-supply reallocation is now explicit.
  • New disclosures: CCPG rename (physical AI), Clearwater Forest launched, 18A-P risk production, memory-architecture ambitions with a marquee hire, customer prepays as a funding source, and possible capital-markets issuance if growth outruns internal cash generation.
  • Notably absent this quarter: any mention of Terafab/SpaceX/xAI/Tesla — the prior quarter's heavily promoted partnership received no update.

Bull case

  • Demand continues to exceed supply by >$1B/quarter with the logjam breaking late Q3 into Q4; server CPU industry unit growth is double-digit through 2027 with momentum into 2028, and Intel's DCAI is compounding well north of double digits on units plus ASP-per-core expansion.
  • Margin inflection is real: 41.8% in Q2, 42% guided for Q3, Panther Lake cost down 50% YTD with margins committed to cross above corporate average, and foundry losses narrowing ($2.4B → $2.1B) on yield and scale gains.
  • The 18A execution story is compounding: output 25% above target, +50% QoQ, Clearwater Forest launched as the first 18A server part, 18A-P in risk production with +5% performance — internal validation ahead of external foundry engagements.
  • 14A is ahead of its own aggressive schedule (SRAM yield, defect density, performance) with PDK 0.9 in October as the customer decision catalyst, and the 2028 volume commitment signals management's conviction.
  • Multiple non-wafer revenue engines are scaling now: ASIC ~$2B run rate heading to $4B, design services ~3x YoY, EMIB-T backlog with 2027 ramps, edge/physical AI at ~10% of CCPG — each described as multibillion-dollar annual opportunities.
  • Balance sheet supports the build: $40B liquidity, ~$10B monetizable noncore assets, customer prepays, $0.35/$1 U.S. investment tax credits, and $7B of quarterly operating cash flow.

Bear case

  • CapEx has been revised four times in four quarters and is now >$20B in 2026 with 2027 "significantly above" — the spending trajectory is being set on demand signals and engagement levels, not signed external foundry orders. Zinsner conceded positive free cash flow next year is "a little bit more challenged" by back-end/third-party investments.
  • The 14A 2028 high-volume commitment was made with zero announced external customers — a direct inversion of the "no capacity before commitments" doctrine that underpinned the discipline narrative, now rationalized by the same management that articulated it.
  • Foundry still lost $2.1B in the quarter; external foundry revenue of $293M remains immaterial against a >$20B annual CapEx program.
  • Client strength was manufactured: ASP/mix and price increases to offset cost inflation, against a market down YoY and a PC TAM still guided down low double digits with subseasonal H2 — Q4 client is expected to be soft.
  • Server product competitiveness remains undated: Diamond Rapids and Coral Rapids got no timing for the third consecutive call, and Tan conceded "some areas we are still behind" vs. AMD/Arm with no share-gain claim.
  • Supply-chain risk has shifted to components Intel doesn't control: substrates, T-glass, and memory are "the most challenged parts of our supply chain," requiring prepayments and exposing the ramp to third-party constraints.
  • The Terafab partnership — last quarter's headline announcement — went entirely unmentioned, reinforcing the pattern of promotional announcements without follow-through economics.
