That makes it liquid enough to hedge common technology beta while leaving semiconductor selection risk to the peer basket; it is preferred to a broader United States benchmark for this target and to leveraged or memory-only exchange-traded exposure.

The block is a relative-value semiconductor sleeve around INTC. It uses XYZ100 as the single index hedge for common large-cap technology beta, then sets six positive peer weights that sum to exactly 1. AMD carries the largest weight because it is the closest business comparator; NVDA, MRVL and MU are stepped down for weaker product or business-model overlap; SMSN and SNDK are smaller because foreign common-listing access or NAND-only exposure lowers comparability even where liquidity is high. Stated 24-hour perp liquidity is treated in USD millions exactly as displayed: INTC 6.434 USD millions, AMD 1.18 USD millions, NVDA 3.384 USD millions, MRVL 1.269 USD millions, MU 11.877 USD millions, SMSN 2.55 USD millions, SNDK 31.164 USD millions, and XYZ100 21.944 USD millions. Liquidity caps but does not replace comparability: very liquid SNDK stays small, while lower-liquidity AMD remains the anchor because its economics are closest.
