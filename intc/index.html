    Quarter in one view

    • Q2 FY2026 print: revenue $16.1B ($1.8B above the $14.3B midpoint of the $13.8–14.8B guide), non-GAAP GM 41.8% (~280bps above the 39% guide), EPS $0.42 vs. $0.20 guide — the seventh consecutive beat, and the second straight blowout.
    • Demand outrunning supply is now the central fact: Tan calls it "one of the most severe supply constraints in industry history" across wafers, memory, and substrates; Zinsner says Intel will still be behind demand in Q4 even as supply inflects late Q3.
    • CapEx discipline formally broke: 2026 CapEx raised to >$20B (from "flat y/y" last quarter, ~$17.7B in 2025), tool spend now +40% y/y (from +25%), and 2027 guided "significantly above" 2026 — justified by LTAs and customer prepays, but the "no spend without commitments" doctrine is now being read inversely ("our confidence implies the customers").
    • Cash inflected hard positive: OpCF $7.0B (vs. $1.1B in Q1), ~$30B cash + $10B revolver = $40B liquidity; no FCF figure disclosed on the call.
    • Q3 guide: revenue $15.8–16.8B (midpoint $16.3B, roughly flat q/q), GM 42%, EPS $0.38, tax 11% — flat revenue despite supply growth because the supply ramp is back-end-loaded into late Q3/Q4, especially servers.
    • New disclosures: ASIC run rate now ~$2B, heading to $4B (vs. >$1B last quarter); "AI-driven businesses" now ~70% of revenue, +70% y/y (from 60%/+40%); 14A committed to 2028 high-volume ramp — a decision made in Q2; PC business renamed CCPG (Client Computing and Physical AI Group).

    What management is focused on

    • Supply maximization above all: wafer starts up on Intel 7/3/18A; 18A output ~25% above target and +50% q/q; the binding constraints are increasingly back-end — substrates, T-glass, memory — which Zinsner calls "our most challenged parts of the supply chain."
    • CPU:GPU parity claim escalated: Zinsner now says the ratio is "almost in parity" on a unit basis and could skew toward CPUs — a step beyond last quarter's 1:3–4 inference framing. Server industry unit growth now guided strong double-digit for 2026 and 2027 with momentum into 2028 (extended a year vs. last quarter).
    • 14A commitment: PDK 0.9 on track for October; internal risk production 2H27; and — new — a Q2 decision to fully commit to high-volume ramp in 2028, made without a named external customer.
    • 18A-P: entered risk production; ~5% performance uplift over 18A; positioned as the external-customer node, ready before year-end.
    • Physical AI / edge: the CCPG rename, 130 Series 3 edge-AI design wins (robotics "brain and control"), edge now ~10% of CCPG revenue; Tan claims edge/physical AI could "at least match the client TAM over time."
    • Memory as strategic theme: collaborating with the three big memory vendors; hired Seok-Hee Lee (former SK Hynix CEO); Tan left open a bigger Intel role in memory architectures (Z-Angle Memory question answered warmly but vaguely).

    Key numbers and quarter mechanics

    • Consolidated: $16.1B revenue; GM 41.8%; EPS $0.42; OpCF $7.0B; ~$30B cash/ST investments.
    • CCPG (renamed client): $8.9B, +15% q/q; op profit $2.3B (26%), down ~$173M q/q due to inventory write-downs on stranded match-set products (incomplete units written off to pivot capacity to server). AI PC revenue +26% q/q, now 2/3 of client mix; vPro activations +1,500% over 4 quarters.
    • DCAI: $6.3B, +24% q/q, +59% y/y — strongest server y/y growth "on record"; op profit $2.5B (40%), +~$1B q/q on volume, product margins, and lower OpEx. Purpose-built silicon +~20% q/q, ~3x y/y.
    • Intel Foundry: revenue $5.8B, +6% q/q; external foundry revenue $293M (up from $174M); operating loss $2.1B, improved $348M q/q on yields, cycle times, and scale across Intel 4/3/18A.
    • Panther Lake cost curve: primary SKU cost down ~50% YTD, another ~20% expected this year, further cuts in 2027 — the key GM bridge item; Panther Lake and Granite Rapids still below corporate-average margin but expected to cross above it as 18A yields improve.
    • GM mechanics: Q2's 41.8% driven by revenue, yields, and ASP (mix + pricing); Q3 guided 42% — flat-ish because the Q2 inventory write-down lift is offset by Panther Lake/Granite becoming a larger mix at below-average margin. Zinsner's stated 2026 goal: GM "comfortably into the 40s every quarter."
    • FY26 items: OpEx now "roughly $16.5B" (vs. "$16B but likely higher" last quarter); NCI ~$250M in each of Q3/Q4, ~$1.1B for 2027–28 (unchanged); CapEx >$20B with tools +40%; gross-to-net offsets now mostly the investment tax credit at $0.35/dollar on U.S. spend, running low-single-digit billions with a lag.
    • Balance sheet: $40B liquidity; ~$10B of "noncore assets" still monetizable; customer prepayments already unlocking capacity; Zinsner flagged possible capital-markets tap "if we're super successful."
    • Client shape: Q3 CCPG up slightly (edge growth, client flat — CPU inventory build at customers), Q4 client soft; server gets pivoted wafers.

    Product and launch scorecard

    • Panther Lake / Core Ultra Series 3: 400+ designs; cost down 50% YTD; still below corporate-average margin but on a credible path above it — the clearest margin-ramp evidence yet.
    • Core Series 3 (mainstream, A-stepping): launched as a cost-optimized response to memory inflation — defensive but timely.
    • Arc G-Series: new handheld-gaming processor family; 40+ Arc integrated graphics designs. Small but new growth vector.
    • Clearwater Forest (Xeon 6+, first 18A server part): launched this quarter — resolves last quarter's silence; rack-scale/disaggregated inference work with SambaNova and Foxconn announced.
    • Granite Rapids (Intel 3): "extremely tight" supply, "fantastic reception"; Intel 3 wafer-start expansion is the top server capacity priority.
    • Diamond Rapids / Coral Rapids: named in the roadmap answer to Moore, but again no dates or tape-out status — fourth consecutive quarter without Diamond Rapids timing; SMT still a Coral Rapids event.
    • ASIC / purpose-built silicon: run rate now ~$2B (Zinsner: "approaching $2B"), targeting $4B "in the not-too-distant future"; Fortinet security processor is the new named win alongside the Google IPU; Tan cites a >$100B TAM. Margin profile question from Rakers went unanswered.
    • Advanced packaging (EMIB-T): backlog growing, yields/reliability at target, customer ramps in 2027; substrate prepayments required — CapEx skewing here too.
    • 14A: PDK 0.5 complete, 0.9 in October; 256Mb SRAM yield, defect density, performance "ahead of schedule"; 2028 HVM now committed.
    • Still dark: Crescent Island and Jaguar Shores unmentioned for a third straight quarter; Terafab/Musk partnership not mentioned at all on this call.

    • Delivered/validated: 18A output +50% q/q and 25% above target; Panther Lake cost down 50% YTD (quantified for the first time); Clearwater Forest actually launched; external foundry revenue rebounded to $293M; foundry loss improved $348M as promised; PDK 0.9 date (October) is now specific and near-term — checkable next quarter.
