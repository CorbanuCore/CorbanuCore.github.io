Jul 28, 2026-21.24%—
Read transcript briefing

Quarter in one view

  • SK hynix reported Q2 2026 (call dated 2026-07-28) with revenue of KRW79.3 trillion, +51% Q-o-Q and +257% Y-o-Y — a second consecutive record — and operating profit of KRW60.5 trillion, +61% Q-o-Q and +557% Y-o-Y, at a 76% operating margin (+5pp Q-o-Q, another all-time high).
  • Pricing remained the driver but decelerated sharply from Q1's parabolic pace: DRAM ASP +~30% Q-o-Q (vs. +mid-60% in Q1) with bit shipments +high-single-digit % (met guidance); NAND ASP +mid-50% (vs. +mid-70%) with bit shipments +mid-teens % (met guidance, off Q1's low base).
  • Headline net profit of KRW93.9 trillion (118% net margin) is heavily distorted: net non-operating profit of KRW62.2 trillion, including KRW63.3 trillion of investment asset sale/valuation gains (Kioxia stake sale referenced in Q&A) and KRW1.1 trillion FX gains. Operating profit is the only meaningful earnings read.
  • Balance sheet: cash KRW88.0T (+KRW33.6T Q-o-Q), debt KRW18.6T (-KRW0.7T), net cash KRW69.4T (from KRW35T), debt ratio 7% (from 12%) — already ~70% of the way to the KRW100T net-cash target set last quarter.
  • Key forward items: Q3 DRAM shipments guided +~10% Q-o-Q, NAND +low-single-digit %; 2026 CapEx now quantified at "high KRW40 trillion range"; HBM4 mass-production shipments began in Q2 with full ramp in 2H; LTAs concluded with ~10 customers; ADR listed on Nasdaq July 10.

What management is focused on

  • Converting the upcycle into contracted visibility: the headline strategic development is LTAs — negotiations concluded with ~10 customers including key customers, ~5-year typical terms, varied pricing mechanisms designed to damp volatility, and deposits/prepayment-style financial mechanisms to enforce purchase commitments. This is a material step beyond last quarter's "reviewing various approaches."
  • Defending AI demand durability against a new variant of the slowdown question: the lead question (JP Morgan) raised data-center leasing and model efficiency as capex-slowdown signals; management reframed both as utilization/monetization and adoption-expansion, citing continued customer requests for more supply.
  • Justifying a major capacity expansion: M15X mass production pulled forward, Yongin Fab1 cleanroom early 2027, new P&T7 (advanced packaging) and M17 (NAND) investments announced, plus a new domestic cluster beyond Yongin — all framed as phased and demand-backed to preempt oversupply concerns.
  • Positioning HBM4 execution as proven: mass-production shipments began in Q2, yields "nearing HBM3E levels," HBM4E samples already delivered to a major customer in 1H (ahead of the prior "2H 2026" timeline), and next-gen iHBM (integrated cooling, >30% lower thermal resistance) introduced for HBM5.
  • Capital allocation triangulation: record cash generation vs. sharply higher investment needs vs. shareholder returns — specifics withheld citing ADR-related regulatory restrictions, with communication promised within the year.

Key numbers and quarter mechanics

  • Revenue: KRW79.3T, +51% Q-o-Q, +257% Y-o-Y (record).
  • Operating profit: KRW60.5T, +61% Q-o-Q, +557% Y-o-Y; margin 76% (+5pp, record).
  • D&A: KRW4.0T; EBITDA KRW64.6T, 81% margin.
  • DRAM: bit shipments +high-single-digit % Q-o-Q (met guidance); ASP +~30% Q-o-Q — a marked deceleration from Q1's +mid-60%. Management attributed the below-expectation ASP to high-value product shipments pushed into 2H and mix effects, and guided 2H bit growth above 1H with HBM4 and 1cnm mix lifting blended ASP.
  • NAND: bit shipments +mid-teens % Q-o-Q (met guidance, off Q1's reduced base); ASP +mid-50% Q-o-Q. Enterprise SSD revenue doubled Q-o-Q; Solidigm ≥30TB eSSD revenue more than tripled Q-o-Q.
  • Non-operating: +KRW62.2T net, including KRW63.3T investment sale/valuation gains (Kioxia sale confirmed via Q&A) and KRW1.1T FX gains — an even larger distortion than Q1's +KRW14T.
  • Pre-tax KRW122.7T; net profit KRW93.9T; net margin 118% — net income exceeds revenue-quality operating earnings by ~55%; treat headline EPS as non-run-rate.
  • Cash KRW88.0T (+KRW33.6T); debt KRW18.6T (-KRW0.7T); net cash KRW69.4T; debt ratio 7% (-5pp).
  • Q3 2026 guidance: DRAM shipments +~10% Q-o-Q (server-focused); NAND +low-single-digit % Q-o-Q.
  • 2026 CapEx: "high KRW40 trillion range" — the first absolute figure in at least two quarters, driven by M15X acceleration and Yongin Fab1 ramp investment. Implies roughly ~30% of annualized revenue at current run-rates (inference, not management-stated).
  • Demand outlook: 2026 DRAM bit demand growth ~mid-20%, NAND ~high-teens.

Product and launch scorecard

  • HBM4: the key credibility event of the call. Language firmed from Q1's "getting ready to ramp" to "began mass production shipments in Q2" for key customers, with yields and quality "nearing HBM3E levels" and full ramp in 2H. Still no qualification breadth, HBM4 share-of-HBM, or customer-level detail — but the regression in specificity last quarter was reversed.
  • HBM4E: samples supplied to a major customer in 1H 2026 — ahead of the prior "samples in 2H 2026" timeline; volume production still targeted 2027 on a mature, proven process.
  • SOCAMM2: full supply commencement on 1cnm in Q2; server LPDDR including SOCAMM2 called out as a significant growth contributor; sample shipments planned to broaden the customer base.
  • NAND/eSSD: strongest product evidence this quarter — eSSD revenue 2x Q-o-Q, Solidigm ≥30TB eSSD revenue >3x Q-o-Q; 321-layer became the largest share of NAND production in Q2 with the >50%-of-domestic-capacity year-end target reaffirmed. Portfolio strategy spans TLC, QLC (HDD replacement/data lake), and SLC-mode high-performance SSDs for KV-cache offload — no single-product revenue figures given.
  • iHBM: newly disclosed — integrated in-package cooling, >30% thermal resistance reduction, targeted at HBM5-era products; pre-commercial, no timeline or customer evidence.
  • Conventional DRAM: 1cnm shipment increase flagged as a 2H driver; no LPDDR6/mobile update this quarter.

Sell-side read-through

  • AI capex slowdown (JP Morgan, lead question): data-center leasing and efficient models raised as demand risks. Management's rebuttal — leasing signals utilization/monetization, efficiency expands adoption — is coherent and consistent with prior quarters, but rests on customer conversations, not disclosed data.
  • Oversupply vs. capacity expansion (Hana): management anchored expansion to LTA-secured visibility and phased execution; the answer leans heavily on LTAs whose volume coverage it declined to quantify ("appropriate level").
  • LTA structure (Meritz): the most informative answer of the call — ~5-year terms, customer/product-specific pricing mechanisms, deposits to enforce commitments, ~10 customers concluded. Still no revenue-coverage percentage or pricing floors/caps disclosed.
  • DRAM ASP miss (Daiwa): the only directly challenging question on the quarter's numbers. Management conceded high-value shipments slipped into 2H and mix diluted blended ASP — an admission that Q2's +30% was partly timing, with 2H recovery promised. This is checkable next quarter.
  • HBM competitiveness (SK Securities): competitor progress put directly to the Head of HBM Sales; response emphasized yield/quality/track record over benchmarks — confident but still without share or qualification data.
  • 2027 HBM pricing (UBS): management confirmed negotiations underway and "progressing smoothly," acknowledged rising conventional DRAM prices "may have some influence" on HBM pricing — a subtle signal that HBM pricing leverage is being asserted, not conceded, for 2027.
  • Overseas production (CLSA): no decisions beyond announced Korea investments; site selection framed around power, water, supply chain, customer access — a non-answer that keeps US/Japan optionality open.
  • Shareholder returns (DS Investment): management explicitly hid behind ADR regulatory restrictions to avoid disclosing format/size/timing — a legitimate but convenient deferral; within-year communication reiterated.

Management credibility

  • Guidance delivery: Q2 DRAM +high-single-digit % and NAND +mid-teens % both met. Shipment guidance reliability now extends to at least four quarters.
  • HBM4 language restored: last quarter's drift from "mass production" to "preparing to ramp" was a flagged credibility risk; this quarter management stated mass-production shipments began in Q2 with yields nearing HBM3E — the claim is now specific and time-stamped, and verifiable via 2H revenue mix.
  • HBM4E pulled forward: samples delivered in 1H vs. prior 2H guidance — over-delivery on timeline.
  • CapEx transparency improved: first absolute figure (high KRW40T range) after two quarters of "increase significantly" with no anchor.
  • LTA claims advanced from "reviewing approaches" to "~10 customers concluded" with structural detail (5-year terms, deposits) — a concrete follow-through, though coverage percentage remains undisclosed.
  • ASP visibility remains short in both directions: Q2 DRAM ASP (+~30%) came in below market expectations per Daiwa, after Q1 massively exceeded them; management's mix/timing explanation is plausible but confirms it does not pre-signal pricing accurately.
  • Earnings quality worsened optically: KRW63.3T of investment gains inside pre-tax profit; net margin (118%) now absurdly above operating margin (76%). The Kioxia sale is a real cash event, but headline net income is not an operating measure.
  • Shareholder-return deferral: the "within the year" buyback/cancellation promise from Q1 was not advanced this quarter, with ADR regulations cited — the clock on that promise is now the credibility test.

What changed versus the prior quarter

  • Pricing decelerated hard: DRAM ASP from +mid-60% to +~30% Q-o-Q; NAND from +mid-70% to +mid-50%. Still rising, but the parabolic phase ended; mix/timing now matters as much as contract price momentum.
  • HBM4 went from preparation to mass-production shipments, with yield claims (near HBM3E) offered for the first time; HBM4E samples delivered a half-year early.
  • LTAs moved from negotiation to conclusion with ~10 customers, with first structural disclosure (5-year terms, deposits, volatility-dampening pricing).
  • CapEx quantified for the first time: high KRW40T range for 2026, plus new P&T7, M17, and post-Yongin cluster announcements; M15X pulled forward.
  • ADR executed: listed on Nasdaq July 10 (largest foreign-company US IPO per management); fungibility mechanics disclosed (17.79M-share conversion cap, one-way conversion initially).
  • Net cash nearly doubled to KRW69.4T, boosted by the Kioxia stake sale — a new cash source disclosed only via Q&A.
  • Spot-price concerns from Q1 were not raised; the bear question rotated to AI capex sustainability and oversupply from capacity additions.
  • Non-operating distortion escalated from +KRW14T to +KRW62.2T.

Bull case

  • The cycle is being contractually locked in at the top: ~10 concluded LTAs with 5-year terms, deposits, and volatility-dampening pricing convert spot-driven earnings into multi-year visibility — directly addressing the historical oversupply failure mode.
  • HBM execution risk is receding: HBM4 in mass production with near-mature yields, HBM4E samples early, 2027 pricing negotiations "progressing smoothly" with conventional DRAM strength as leverage, and iHBM extending the roadmap.
  • NAND has become a second AI leg: eSSD revenue doubling and Solidigm ≥30TB revenue tripling Q-o-Q, with inference/KV-cache demand broadening the portfolio across QLC, TLC, and SLC-mode tiers.
  • Financial firepower is now extreme: KRW69.4T net cash, 7% debt ratio, KRW33.6T cash added in one quarter, and a completed Nasdaq listing — funding the high-KRW40T CapEx and capacity land-grab (M15X, Yongin, P&T7, M17) without balance-sheet strain.
  • 2H setup is guided better: DRAM bit growth above 1H, HBM4 ramp and 1cnm mix lifting blended ASP, and deferred Q2 high-value shipments returning.

Bear case

  • ASP momentum halved in one quarter (DRAM mid-60s→~30%, NAND mid-70s→mid-50s%), and management admitted high-value shipments slipped — if 2H mix recovery disappoints, the "timing" explanation becomes a demand-elasticity problem.
  • Q3 NAND guidance of only low-single-digit % bit growth is weak against a claimed eSSD demand surge — worth probing whether capacity, qualification, or demand is the constraint.
  • Reported earnings are now mostly non-operating: KRW63.3T of one-off gains inside KRW122.7T pre-tax; any model anchored to headline net income (118% margin) is anchoring to asset sales.
  • The capacity buildout (high-KRW40T CapEx, M15X acceleration, Yongin, M17, new cluster) is being committed at 76% operating margins; the oversupply defense rests on LTAs whose revenue coverage management refused to quantify.
  • PC/mobile "temporary sales adjustments" from memory cost pressure continue with no stabilization evidence offered this quarter.
  • Shareholder returns remain unexecuted promises, now gated behind ADR regulatory restrictions — the within-year deadline could slip.

Next-quarter watchlist

  • Q3 delivery vs. guidance: DRAM +~10%, NAND +low-single-digit % bit growth; whether DRAM ASP re-accelerates on HBM4/1cnm mix as management promised, or the Daiwa "timing" explanation unravels.
  • HBM4 ramp evidence: share of HBM revenue, yield trajectory vs. the "near-HBM3E" claim, customer breadth beyond the lead key customer; HBM4E development milestones toward 2027 volume production.
  • LTA conversion and disclosure: additional signings beyond ~10 customers, any revenue-coverage percentage, and whether deposit mechanisms show up in balance-sheet items.
  • 2027 HBM pricing outcome: whether conventional DRAM strength translates into HBM price increases or flat-to-down resets.
  • CapEx trajectory: spend vs. the high-KRW40T plan; M15X accelerated ramp; Yongin Fab1 cleanroom early-2027 milestone; P&T7/M17 groundbreaks.
  • eSSD momentum: whether eSSD revenue doubles again or the Q2 2x/3x figures prove base-effect-driven; 321-layer migration toward >50% of domestic capacity by year-end.
  • Shareholder returns: the within-year buyback/cancellation plan — now the outstanding promise; ADR conversion-limit changes and any increase in ADR proportion.
  • Non-operating normalization: whether net margin collapses back toward operating margin absent further asset sales; FX swings.
  • PC/mobile: whether "temporary" adjustments stabilize as management expects, or spread; any server-customer pushback on pricing as contract negotiations for 2027 proceed.
